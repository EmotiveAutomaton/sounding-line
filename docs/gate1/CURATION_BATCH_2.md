# Curation batch 2 — topic-controlled, two questions

**Target: 20 minutes, not 90.** Batch 1 cost roughly ten minutes per artifact and that pace does
not scale. Three changes below, each aimed squarely at the cost.

---

## §1. What changed, and why each change is safe

### Five questions → two

Every place my curation was wrong in batch 1 (items 2, 6, 8) was wrong about **whether a maker was
there** and **how much they decided**. Questions 2, 3 and 5 produced good material but nothing
that moved a verdict — purpose and audience were largely derivable from the answers to the other
two, and cost tracked effort closely enough to be redundant once `effort` exists as its own
dimension (obligation C-4).

So batch 2 asks two questions and one number. **Anything else you say is welcome and will be
recorded, but nothing is being asked for.**

### One topic across seven of the nine items

Seven items are all *how to store fresh basil*. This is the change that buys the most time: after
the second item you already know the subject matter, so reading time collapses to noticing
differences instead of building context from scratch each time.

It also removes a confound I should have controlled in batch 1. **Topic was free to vary with
provenance there** — the careful artifacts were about infrastructure engineering and the hollow
ones about handicrafts and mathematics. Any depth difference I measured was partly a difference in
subject. Holding topic fixed makes the comparison mean something.

The seven were the actual first-page results for that query. **I did not curate which pages
appeared** — only that they resolve and are not duplicates. What comes back is a real slice of
what the web serves for a common question.

### No Wayback, no GeoCities

Your note: *"the Wayback Machine and stuff, I can recognize it immediately as human."* Correct, and
it makes those artifacts worthless as tests — the reading is determined before the text is read.
Everything in batch 2 is a live, modern, commercial page. **Harder, and unavoidably so.**

### Dates

Stripped where I can, but these are live pages and some carry visible dates or "updated"
stamps. Where you notice one, say so and I will mark that item contaminated, as with batch 1
items 5 and 8. Recording the leak is worth more than pretending it did not happen.

## §2. The two questions

**Q1 — Was anyone there?**
Someone specific behind it, or did it come from nowhere? A sentence is plenty. *Nobody home* is a
complete answer.

**Q2 — What did they decide against?**
Somewhere they could clearly have done something else and didn't. Then a rough count:
**none / one or two / several / everywhere.**

**And one number — effort (0–5).** Your addition, from batch 1, and now the thing being
calibrated. *How hard did they work?* — deliberately **not** how good it is, and **not** what it
cost them. Item 2 in batch 1 was your worked example: a child labouring over a bad page. High
effort, low depth.

Optional, only if it fires unprompted: **did you want to keep reading, and was there anything to
take on board?** Batch 1 established these are the same question (§5 of the calibration record) and
that it may be the most valuable signal in the set. Do not go looking for it — if it does not
happen, that is data.

## §5. How many more batches

Honestly: **probably one, and possibly none.**

Batch 1 already produced three findings before the instrument ran, and Gate 1 needs *seven
calibrated artifacts*, not seventy. Between batches 1 and 2 there are eighteen, which is more than
the gate requires.

What is genuinely still missing is **rows 6 and 7** — model output under a rich, deliberate prompt
versus a thin one. That pair is §1's entire claim and nobody has built it. But it does not need a
survey: it needs **two artifacts**, generated to a protocol, and the honest candidate for the rich
one is a document from this project whose revision history is in the git log.

So the realistic remaining ask is one short session on that pair, not another sweep. If batch 2
comes back and rows 6 and 7 get settled, curation is done and the instrument runs.

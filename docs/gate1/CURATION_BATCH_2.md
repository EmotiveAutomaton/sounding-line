# Curation batch 2 — video games *(supersedes the basil batch)*

**The basil batch is withdrawn before it ran.** The curator asked me to make the
expertise/error-bar trade consciously rather than by default. Making it consciously reverses it.
The withdrawn version is preserved in git history; this document replaces it.

---

## §1. The decision, made explicitly

The curator's objection:

> *"If this is supposed to be a high expertise or a high resolution pass, you're going to get bad
> results. If you want it to be a high resolution pass, it needs to be in a field in which I have
> expertise... But I want you to make that decision consciously."*

**I had already documented this as a confound and then built a batch that maximised it.**
`CALIBRATION_01.md` §4 records that a reader's own expertise caps what can be extracted (E10), and
obligation C-5 requires recording curator expertise per artifact. I logged that and then chose a
topic where the curator's expertise is low.

### Which error bar Gate 1 can afford

Two things were being traded and I had not separated them:

| | high-expertise domain | ordinary web topic (basil) |
|---|---|---|
| curator precision | high | low |
| ecological typicality | low | high |
| what it calibrates | the instrument | the instrument *and* the reader, confounded |

**Gate 1 calibrates the instrument against a standard. A blurry standard calibrates nothing.**
Precision wins, and it is not close.

The argument that settles it comes from §4 of the calibration record: low fit currently conflates
*few decisions were made* with *decisions were made and this reader cannot read them*. **Out of
domain, every low reading is contaminated by the second.** The only way to measure the first
cleanly is a curator who can rule the second out — which requires domain expertise, not ordinary
web text.

Ecological typicality is a real requirement and it belongs to **Gate 2**, where the falsifiers run
on corpora at scale and the curator is not the standard. Wanting it here was a category error.

### What was kept from the basil design

Topic control, which was the batch's genuinely good idea — batch 1 let topic vary with provenance,
so any depth difference was partly subject matter. Here **domain is held fixed** (video games) and
**topic is held fixed across items 1–6** (Elden Ring character builds), with three design-writing
items outside it as anchors.

Reading speed should benefit the same way: after item 2 the subject matter is loaded and the task
collapses to noticing differences.

## §2. The two questions, unchanged

**Q1 — Was anyone there?** *Nobody home* is a complete answer.

**Q2 — What did they decide against?** Somewhere they could clearly have done something else and
didn't. Then: **none / one or two / several / everywhere.** *"I can't see them deciding anything"*
is a real answer and on several of these it is the expected one — a strained answer is worse than
none, because it fabricates the quantity being measured.

**Effort, 0–5.** How hard they worked. Not how good it is; not what it cost them.

Optional, only if it fires: **anything to take on board?** — the acquisition signal from §5 of the
calibration record. Do not go looking for it.

none.** If row 3 is sourceable at all, it looks like this.

Related caution: it is a hijacked academic host. It is safe to read and I would not enter anything
into it.

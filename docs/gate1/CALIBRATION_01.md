# Calibration pass 01 — curator readings, batch 1

**Recorded 2026-08-02, before any instrument run.** Nine artifacts, presented blind, read by a
single curator (the project author). These are the pre-registered expected readings against which
Gate 1 scores the instrument.

**What may be claimed from this:** that the instrument agreed or disagreed with one human's prior.
Nothing else. n=1, not a human-subjects study, and every Gate 1 number carries the sentence
*"calibrated against a single curator's pre-registered expectations."*

---

## §0. A failure of my own blinding, stated first

Two things went wrong on my side and both are my fault.

**1. I leaked the dates.** Items 5 and 8 carried visible publication years, and the curator said so
unprompted: *"I feel like it's unfair that you're giving me the dates because that's just an
immediate I know whether this was made by an AI, which changes a little bit of the question."* On
item 5 the leak was load-bearing — the curator's stated fallback when the text defeated them was
*"I saw the 2023 and people weren't using AI to draft legal documents in 2023."*

**Consequence: items 5 and 8 are contaminated as calibration standards for the audience and
provenance dimensions.** They are retained, marked, and excluded from any provenance comparison.
Future batches strip dates, bylines, and domain-identifying chrome before presentation.

**2. I blinded the curator and failed to pre-register myself.** I told the curator I knew which
SPEC §7 row each link filled. **I never wrote that mapping down.** It exists only as recollection,
which makes it unfalsifiable, and a prior that cannot be checked is not a prior. The disclosures
in §2 below are therefore labelled as *reconstructed*, they carry no evidential weight, and they
are recorded as a procedural failure rather than as data.

The rule that follows, and it applies to every future batch: **whoever assembles a batch commits
their row assignments to the repository before the batch is sent.**

## §1. The curator's readings

Condensed from a spoken walkthrough; wording preserved where it carries the judgement.

### 1 — GitLab database outage postmortem
- **Anyone there:** Yes, but performing. *"Someone here, but they were trying to appear to be PR
  facing."* Cited `really`, and tense slips, as evidence of a human hand.
- **Purpose:** *"A very tech-precise CYA."* Forthright *in service of* reputation. Expects the
  account to be substantially accurate — *"if they lied, they folded it in there."*
- **Audience:** News reporters and affected users, as the peak of the interest curve.
- **Decided against:** Lying. Noted that this inference leans on GitLab's reputation rather than

- **Cost:** *"Nothing at all... if they didn't do it, it would have cost them money."*

- **Effort:** Lower than item 3.

### 2 — eHow, "How to Braid Money"

### The finding that costs the most: row 3 may not be sourceable from eHow

**All three content-farm artifacts read as having genuine, recoverable human intent.** Not one
read as hollow. That is not a curator error — the readings are specific, evidenced, and confident
(item 2 was among the highest-confidence readings in the batch).

The explanation is that the spec's row 3 conflates two things that come apart:

- **The maker's intent** — a paid writer filling a keyword brief for money. Thin, and real.
- **The artifact's surrogate intent** — *help a child braid money*, *help a school-leaver
  interview better*. The brief obliges the writer to adopt a communicative purpose, and adopting
  it leaves recoverable traces whether or not they cared.

An eHow how-to therefore has a legible purpose and a legible audience and is **the wrong control**.
The instrument reading it as intentful is not a false positive — it is arguably correct, and it is
§1's reframe operating exactly as designed on a case the spec did not anticipate.

**What row 3 actually needs is artifacts with no surrogate communicative purpose at all**:
doorway pages, template-generated local-service pages, scraped-and-spun product descriptions,
keyword-stuffed pages with no instructional content. Sourcing those is batch 2's main job, and
this finding stands as the first substantive Gate 1 result — arrived at before the instrument ran.

## §3. The dimension the family is missing: effort

Unprompted, and the sharpest thing in the transcript:

> *"That being said, they didn't try very hard. I do think effort is something you're missing in
> these."*

Followed by a full effort ranking across the batch, given spontaneously: item 3 above item 1;
item 2 higher than expected (*"it actually might have taken quite a bit of effort because it was a
kid doing it"*) while remaining badly made; item 4 low; item 5 possibly high but unjudgeable;
item 6 moderate-to-low from an expert.

**This is D-1's stated counter-argument firing exactly as written.** `family_v1.yaml` records that
a hand-written family encodes the author's assumptions about what people make things for, and that
this is the kind of assumption the project usually tests rather than asserts. A curator reaching
for a dimension the family lacks is that test, and the family failed it.

**It is also N21 arriving from the opposite direction.** The simulation asked *"is depth just
effort wearing a hat?"* and separated them only by construction — the effort axis was rebuilt to
make an "offhand but deep" corner representable *before* the contrast was measured, and that was
logged as a construction commitment rather than a finding. Here a human tracks the two separately
and without prompting, on real artifacts. Item 2 is the demonstration: **high effort, low depth,
badly made.** Item 7 is its mirror: extraordinary depth, and the artifact itself reports 67
minutes.

`cost_borne` does not cover this. Cost is *what the maker gave up that they did not have to*;
effort is *how hard they worked*, and a child labouring over a bad page spends effort without
bearing cost. Collapsing them loses precisely the contrast the simulation spent a version on.

**Proposed: `effort` becomes a fifth ordinal dimension in family v2, estimated separately from
depth and from cost, specifically so the dissociation can be measured rather than assumed.**

## §4. The second theoretical finding: constraint has two effects, not one

From item 5, and it is a genuine contribution rather than a reading:

> *"Rigid language like this gives you less opportunity for expression and decision making. The
> more rigid the constraints are you're working with, the harder it is to detect this
> intentionality and the decisions within. Decisions are still being made, obviously, but you have
> to have the expertise to deconstruct it. And in a high expertise area like this, the intention
> is one, less present, and two, harder to decode."*

Two separable effects of constraint, which the instrument currently collapses:

1. **Fewer decisions are available to make.** A genuine reduction in recoverable intent.
2. **The decisions that were made are harder to read** — and the difficulty is *expertise-gated*,
   a property of the reader-artifact pair rather than of the artifact.

`Fit` cannot presently distinguish these. Low fit on a 10-K currently means the same thing as low
fit on a spun doorway page, and the curator's account says they are different objects.

This is **E37's distinction reappearing in a new form.** E37 separated a vocabulary deficit from a
missing inversion; this separates a missing inversion from an inversion that exists and requires
expertise the reader lacks. The simulation's E10 established that a reader's own skill caps what
can be extracted — and that effect is now *inside the calibration data*, because the curator
flagged it themselves:

> *"My lack of expertise in given fields is actually going to be a pretty strong limiting
> factor sometimes."*

**Two consequences, both recorded as obligations:**

- Every calibration reading carries a **curator-expertise field**. Items 3, 5 and 8 were read
  out of domain and the curator said so; item 7 was read with domain standing. A calibration
  standard whose known confound is not recorded is not a standard.
- **A low-fit reading must eventually be decomposable** into *few decisions were made* versus
  *decisions were made and are unreadable from here*. Not a Gate 1 build — recorded as a design
  obligation before Gate 2, because the SEO-versus-boilerplate separation depends on it.

## §5. Engagement is not depth, and the curator's data says so loudly

Wanted to keep reading: **items 3 and 7 only.** Did not want to keep reading: **1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8,
9** — including item 1, a careful artifact by a competent maker, and item 9, which the curator
read as unmistakably human and sincere.

The reasons given are never about quality:

- item 1 — *"who cares. It didn't directly affect me."*
- item 2 — *"I wasn't the target audience."*
- item 6 — *"it doesn't apply to me... It would be useful. I just don't care."*
- item 4 — *"I was actually repulsed."*

**Engagement tracked relevance-to-reader and aesthetic pleasure; it did not track depth, fit, or
craft.** This is E42 in real text — looking deeply and being willing to be changed are different
things — and it is a standing argument against ever reporting engagement as a proxy for any of the
four quantities.

Item 4 is the interesting one: *repulsion*, not indifference. The curator disengaged hardest from
the artifact they judged incompetent-but-sincere, and least from the artifacts they judged
insincere-but-skilled. Nothing in the family currently represents that, and it is not obvious it
should.

## §6. Item 7 reproduces two simulation findings the project could not previously test

The curator's reading of *Writing, Briefly*, unprompted:

> *"They didn't even know what they were trying to do... their subconscious motivation just kind of
> built up something interesting that they then looked at, realized they had made something they
> didn't intend, that their intentions were not aligned with what their conscious wanted."*

That is **E33** — a reader recovering a maker-state the maker did not have access to — and **E43**
— practice compresses decisions, and compression is what makes a decision unavailable for report.
Both were simulation-only, and both are recorded here as *observed once, by one reader, on one
artifact*. That is an anecdote, not a replication, and it may not be quoted as more.

And the audience dimension took a shape the family cannot currently express:

> *"They were writing it to someone, but at a certain point they were talking to everyone."*

**The audience changed mid-artifact.** `family_v1.yaml` treats audience as a single distribution
over one artifact. Whether that matters enough to model is an open question; that it was observed
on the first pass is recorded.

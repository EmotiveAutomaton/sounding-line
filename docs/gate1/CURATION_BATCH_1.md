# Curation batch 1 — nine artifacts, blind

**What this is.** Nine real, live, verified links and five questions to answer about each. Your
answers become the pre-registered expected readings the instrument is calibrated against
(`CANDIDATES.md` §0). Roughly 30 seconds to two minutes per artifact; skim, don't study.

## Why the links are unlabelled

**I know which SPEC §7 row I think each one fills. I am not telling you, and that is deliberate.**

If you know in advance that item 4 is "supposed to be" hollow SEO filler, your reading of it is no
longer independent — you would be confirming my curation rather than producing a judgement the
instrument can be scored against. Told nothing, your answers are a genuine second measurement.

Two consequences worth accepting up front:

- **Some of these will be miscategorised, and I want to find that out from you.** If an artifact I

  and the calibration set improves.
- **You may disagree with the instrument later, and it will be ambiguous who was right.** That is
  the correct position at Gate 1 and it is the entire reason for doing this blind.

## What you are, methodologically

Stated plainly because it affects what may be claimed: **you are one curator, and n=1.** This is
not a human-subjects study and must never be reported as one. What your answers do is give each
artifact a *recorded prior* from a human who reverse-engineered intent the ordinary way, written
down before the instrument ran. That is enough to make the instrument falsifiable and nowhere near
enough to make it validated.

The simulation's `may_not_claim` discipline applies: no Gate 1 number goes in any external
document without the sentence *"calibrated against a single curator's pre-registered
expectations."*

## The five questions

Answer in whatever form is fastest — a sentence each is fine, fragments are fine, "no idea" is a
real answer and an informative one.

**Q1 — Was anyone there?**
Do you get a specific person behind this, or does it feel like it came from nowhere? Where on
*nobody home → someone specific* does it land?
→ *feeds: **fit**. Low fit is the wall.*

**Q2 — What were they trying to do?**
One sentence, your words. If several things at once, say which dominates.
→ *feeds: **purpose**.*

**Q3 — Who were they talking to?**
One named person · a group they belong to · the general public · **a machine or a ranking system**
· nobody in particular. The last two are different and the difference matters: *aimed at a
machine* vs *no reader modelled at all*.
→ *feeds: **audience posterior**.*

**Q4 — What did they decide *against*?**
The important one. Point at somewhere they clearly could have done something else and didn't.
Roughly how many such places — none, one or two, several, or everywhere you look?
→ *feeds: **depth**. A choice with no visible alternative is a property, not a decision.*

**Q5 — What did it cost them?**
Did they give anything up to make this? Time, a position they can be wrong about, an easier
version they didn't ship?
→ *feeds: **cost borne** and **trade-offs**.*

**Plus two gut checks:**
- **How sure are you?** (rough is fine — *pretty sure* / *guessing*)
- **Did you want to keep reading?** — the engagement signal, and E19 says it does not behave the
  way anyone expects.

## A worked example, in the register I'm asking for

Against a hypothetical long forum post about rebuilding a motorcycle carburettor:

> **Q1.** Someone's definitely there. He's done this a lot — you can tell because he keeps
> pre-empting the thing that goes wrong two steps later. *Pretty sure.*
>
> **Q2.** Stop other people wrecking theirs the way he wrecked his the first time.
>
> **Q3.** A group he's in. Other people with this specific bike. Not the public — he doesn't
> explain what a carburettor is.
>
> **Q4.** Loads. He says don't use the ultrasonic cleaner even though everyone does, and gives a
> reason. He skips the torque spec because he thinks the manual's wrong and says so. That's two
> places he went against the obvious move and defended it. *Everywhere you look.*
>
> **Q5.** He's put his name to an opinion the forum will fight him about. And it's long — this
> took an evening he didn't owe anyone.
>
> **Keep reading?** Yes, and I don't own a motorcycle.

That last line is the kind of thing worth writing down. Note the shape: **concrete, points at the
artifact, comfortable saying "loads" instead of a number.** No need to be precise — the
commitment is the point, not the resolution.

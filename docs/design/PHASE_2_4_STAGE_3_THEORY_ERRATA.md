## 0. Executive ruling

The latest walkthrough narrows the theory more than it expands it. The five living files already
contain most of the required distinctions:

- process recovery is a posterior over possible maker histories, not one persuasive route;
- viewer-coherent, reader-enactable, and historically corresponding processes are different
  outputs;
- human invertibility is relational and has not been measured in this repository;
- self-reconstruction is a similarity-based shortcut that must be corrected toward the maker;
- mixed production is a directed network of proposal, selection, ratification, veto, repair, and
  integration events;
- decision events are not equal-weight cognitive atoms;
- context is evidence that reweights a reader and is not automatically trustworthy;
- no model-side affect result establishes a human neural mechanism;
- value recovery requires repeated observations and cannot be grounded in one authored profile.

Four surgical changes remain warranted:

1. **State the minimal theoretical bet explicitly.** Inverse planning is the established problem
   family. The project's distinctive human hypothesis is that people often use an empathy-like,
   self-model-based reconstruction as an efficient solver, with maker-specific evidence correcting
   projection. The rest of the current machinery is defeasible.
2. **Retire 27 as a live ceiling.** The existing 2026-08-22 quotation remains intact as historical
   language, but its basis-design disposition is superseded. No affect count, including seven or
   twenty-seven, governs discovery.
3. **Separate event density from causal reach, and director control from distributed coherence.**
   A director may contribute few high-leverage events while implementation supplies many more
   readable events. A shared brief, conventions, institutional selection, or a distributed system
   can produce the same global coherence.
4. **Make two reader-side predictions precise.** Relevant similarity should usually buy a modest,
   broad efficiency or calibration advantage rather than exclusive access to an inference. Maker
   context is hypothesized to become especially useful during differentiation after an initial
   artifact/self prior, but context-first and joint processing remain live rivals.

One empirical sentence is also narrowed. L170 did not show that decoded affect is universally
causally inert. It showed that a particular rank-one fear/joy intervention did not move one
approach-versus-withdraw behavior in Qwen2.5-1.5B at the tolerated doses. Stage 3 may test new
constructions without reclassifying that result.

The structural ruling is strict:

> **Do not add a sixth theory owner, rewrite any existing curator quotation, or modernize the
> historical essays. Add no new curator blockquote in this pass.**

No substantive edit is recommended for `ALIGNMENT.md`, `docs/theory/README.md`, or
`docs/theory/essays/`.

## 3. Research boundary governing the edits

### 3.1 Empathy-like reconstruction is a candidate solver, not a renamed problem

Inverse planning already provides a general formal family for inferring latent goals and beliefs
from behavior. Simulation theory and empathy theory address a different question: what resources a
human uses to perform that inference. Stueber's distinction between basic and reenactive empathy is
especially close to the current project, but even his defense treats empathy as a default with
limits and supplemental strategies, not as an exclusive route. The living theory should make its
distinctive solver claim visible without announcing that empathy and inverse planning are
synonyms. See [Baker, Saxe, and Tenenbaum](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19729154/) and
[Stueber's publisher summary and contents](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt5hhbjr).

### 3.2 Similarity governs the usefulness and danger of projection

Social-inference work supports a similarity-contingent use of self-projection, not a universal
accuracy law. Perceived similarity can increase projection even when it does not track the target's
actual relevant similarity. The theory therefore predicts a cheap prior and a characteristic error,
not monotonic truth. See [Ames](https://www.columbia.edu/~da358/publications/ames_strategies.pdf)
and [Mitchell, Macrae, and Banaji](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16701214/).

### 3.3 Active affect, outward expression, and neural amount are different variables

A dynamic-network account makes emotion, attention, and decision processes mutually interacting
without mapping each label to a separate compartment. Expressive suppression also prevents absent
display from serving as a direct assay of absent internal affect. The safe theory claim is that a
state-dependent affective constraint may alter inference and action selection; the unsafe claim is
that quiet text shows that less of the brain was active or that the maker cared less. See
[Pessoa](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262314763/the-cognitive-emotional-brain/) and the
[expressive-suppression review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4141473/).

### 3.4 Global coherence does not identify a single controlling mind

Becker treats art as collective action organized through networks, resources, conventions, and
editing. Hutchins moves the cognitive unit beyond an individual and studies systems whose
properties differ from those of any one participant. These are not objections to director control;
they are rival causal graphs that can yield a coherent artifact. See
[Becker, *Art Worlds*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv11hpss4) and
[Hutchins, *Cognition in the Wild*](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262082310/cognition-in-the-wild/).

### 3.5 Intention and preference require plan, opportunity, and correspondence evidence

Livingston's moderate intentionalism distinguishes executing an intention from realizing it and
treats collaborative authorship as shared overall intention, meshing subplans, shared decisions,
control, and responsibility. Baxandall's inferential criticism reconstructs a situated problem and
tests a historical explanation without promising a stroke-by-stroke mental narrative. Together
they support the proposed opportunity and role guardrails. See the
[Livingston review](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/art-and-intention-a-philosophical-study/) and
[Baxandall](https://aaeportal.com/publications/-13411/patterns-of-intention-on-the-historical-explanation-of-pictures).

None of these adjacent literatures tests Sounding Line's artifact-reading instrument. They bound
what the living prose may claim.

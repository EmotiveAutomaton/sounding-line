## 0. Executive command

Stage 7 sealed the room and found that no local reader could use a maker's state, whole or by factor, while a program could; what the readers could name were cross-episode objects (the law's shape, the compiled habit) and not episode-local ones (goal, belief, subjective action set, maker context); and every gain a reader ever showed was a program executing the reader's words.

The curator's walkthroughs reread that result. **The reader is the realizer.** Reading is running your own forward model of the domain's standard process against the artifact and attending to where they differ; expertise is the largest part of the ability to recreate, the shared language of a domain, and a reader that cannot produce the standard process has nothing to be surprised by. The maker's share is small by nature and lives in the divergences from the standard process: the proximal goal, the attention paid to it, and the record of earlier goals that diverged. The proximal goal is the reason the maker started spending resources, it holds across an artifact, and it is the easiest inference, recovered by asking what the artifact could be used for. Prediction tests expertise first; the residue is where the maker is; interesting is the name for the part of the map a reader can almost solve.

Stage 8 therefore changes the first gate and the goal object, and asks four questions in order:

1. **Can the reader make?** Install the standard process in small readers by training on population-sampled process logs, and admit a reader only if its own forward model predicts and produces the standard process at the frozen common-domain model's level, with nothing supplied. The untrained readers run the same gate; their failure is the honest reading of Stage 7, measured.
2. **Does it look in the right places?** An admitted reader's own surprise should land where the maker actually diverges from the standard process, at least as well as the standard process's own surprise does, and better after seeing the maker's earlier work.
3. **Does it recover the goal as a purpose?** With the goal rebuilt as what the artifact is for, does the admitted reader name it by the affordance route, hold several purposes alive until evidence closes them, and improve prediction of the rest of the artifact and the changed-context choice by executing the purpose through its own forward model?
4. **Does the maker accumulate?** Across one, two, and three earlier artifacts by the same maker, does the reader's surprise and its recall of the maker's law and residue converge on that maker, and predict a new artifact's divergences?

Beside the science, a testbed track clones the sibling programs' repositories into the reference workspace, fetches corpus manifests for the human-input corpora, and catalogs them in `docs/TOOLS.md`, so Stage 9 has texture to work with. A capped frontier probe runs the same gates to learn one thing: whether the Stage 7 boundary is a small-model boundary.

Implement every card before the scientific lock. A card is not implemented because an identifier exists; it needs a distinct construction, target, estimand, null, failure consequence, output, and expected cell set, and the manifest rejects two cards that reduce to one mapping and statistic.

## 3. What the stage must distinguish

- **Installed expertise from supplied description.** A reader that has been trained on the standard process holds it in its weights; a reader handed the standard process as text holds a description. Stage 7 tested the second. Stage 8 tests the first and keeps the second as the Stage 7 comparator.
- **The standard process from the maker.** Training installs only the population's process (makers marginalized); nothing maker-specific is ever trained in. The maker is learned only from artifacts in context, where the log can show it being learned.
- **Purpose from pull ordering.** The purpose is what the artifact is for; the pull ordering is a derived preference over move types that the purpose and the law produce together. Both are scored, separately, on the same reader, so the goal ontology question is answered by measurement.
- **The maker's events from the ordinary events.** Every prospective contrast is reported twice: over all events (the whole) and over the events where the exact maker state diverges from the standard process (the tail). The tail is the maker's share by the walkthrough's definition; the whole is the Stage 7 comparator.
- **Reading from impersonation.** An admitted reader is scored on makers it was never trained to be, on lineages its training never saw (X03 enforces the split by hash), and its attention allocation is logged, never optimized.

## 5. Worlds and construction

The Stage 7 constructor (`runners/stage7/constructor/`) is extended, never replaced. Stage 7's K, R, A, and V families remain available as comparators; the new families are:

- **The population corpus (`POP`).** Process logs sampled from the population prior over laws, goals, beliefs, and residues, with the maker-specific factors integrated out by sampling, rendered in the same log grammar the readers will be scored in. This is the training material for `FM`, the fitting material for `DOM`, and it is lineage-tagged so that X03 can prove no test lineage descends from it.
- **The purpose family (`PU`).** Each world carries a purpose `p` drawn from a small declared set (persuade an audience; document a procedure; explore a question; teach a novice), which determines the goal utility ordering together with the law, sets the required sections, and is only partially visible in the brief (the brief states topic and audience; the purpose is withheld in the purpose-withheld condition and stated in the purpose-supplied condition). The oracle carries `p`; the pull ordering is derived and recorded beside it. Equivalence cases exist by construction: prefixes that two purposes explain equally until a diagnostic later section.
- **The artful gradient (`AG`).** Three task shapes with the same law family: a required-sections report (structured), a free draft with few requirements (diary-like), and the Stage 7 essay shape between them. The oracle-minus-DOM gap per shape is the stage's first measurement of the maker's share as a function of admitted decisions; declared before any reader, it is reported as a construction fact and sets the tail floors per shape.
- **The maker series (`MS`).** Makers with four artifacts each under one law and residue, with purposes varying across artifacts, and a per-artifact reveal parameter (how much the maker's residue is expressed in that artifact) declared in the oracle and never visible.

Every substantive contrast needs a true oracle with a nontrivial gap over `DOM` on both the whole and the tail, a visible-prefix collision, a later discriminating event, an equivalence case, matched renderers and opaque identifiers, and a counterfactual computed from the same law. The stop target is scored per stop event with its own floor declared before the run (the Stage 7 lesson); if its gap fails the floor it is reported and no reader is tested on it.

## 6. Estimators

`S` is the held-out proper log score at the independent-unit level (the world). For each target and family:

\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{oracle}} = S_{OR} - S_{DOM},\qquad
\Delta_{\mathrm{tail}} = \big(S_{OR} - S_{DOM}\big)\big|_{\text{events where } p_{OR}(a^*) - p_{DOM}(a^*) > \tau},
\]

with `τ` declared per family from the population's own distribution of divergences (the 80th percentile of the oracle-minus-DOM per-event gap in `POP` is the default; written down before any reader runs). The default smallest useful gain is a fifth of the relevant gap, whole and tail separately.

The **expertise gate** statistic is the reader's next-move log score on held-out `POP` worlds against `DOM`, with a pass band of `DOM − 0.05` nats or better, and the **generation gate** statistic is the exact standard process's log likelihood of the reader's own generated logs against the distribution of real `POP` logs' likelihoods, with a pass at or above the real logs' 20th percentile and every generated log feasible under the live options.

The **surprise alignment** statistic is the AUROC of per-event reader surprise (`−log p_reader(a*)`) for identifying maker events (the tail set), reported beside the same AUROC for `DOM`'s surprise, which is the pure-expertise reference; the reader's alignment minus `DOM`'s is the estimand.

The **purpose recall** statistic is the share of worlds whose candidate list contains the true purpose (or its equivalence partner where the prefix leaves two), with the one-call verbalized distribution as the proposal format; abstention and calibration are read from the same distribution.

Report Brier, calibration slope and intercept, expected calibration error, risk-coverage, class coverage on equivalence worlds, candidate recall, model calls, tokens, forward passes, solver operations, wall time, GPU-lock seconds (the Stage 7 ledger gap, repaired), retries, and dollars for `FR`.

## 9. Gates, branching, and closure

**Gate order:** record gate (§2 applied) → construction gate (§5 gaps, whole and tail, per family and shape) → isolation gate (I03 to I07, X01 to X03) → keystone gate (I08) → expertise gate (E03 and E04; a reader that fails after one repair is not tested past E) → difference gate (D01 non-inferiority) → explanation gate (G01 recall, then G02) → accumulation (A) → confirmation (at most three claims).

**Automatic branching.**

| result shape | continue | close or rename |
|---|---|---|
| E02 misses the band by under 0.05 on a reader. | One repair: a second seed or one more epoch. | A second miss closes that reader for the stage; the other reader proceeds. |
| E03 fails on every trained reader. | Run E08 and D01 as diagnosis. | The stage's reader claims close; the testbed and construction facts stand; **theory-change interrupt** (§9.1). |
| E05 passes (an untrained reader passes the gate). | Stop and report before D. | **Theory-change interrupt**: the Stage 7 boundary reading is wrong. |
| D01 fails on an admitted reader. | Run D04 (true purpose supplied) as diagnosis. | The difference mechanism claim narrows to "with the purpose known"; **interrupt** before G. |
| G01 recall under 0.5. | Spend the one repair on the proposal format (fixed count of candidates, then sampling). | Selection cards (G02 to G04) read as bounded diagnosis. |
| G05 shows the purpose recovered and the pull ordering not, or the reverse. | Continue. | Report as theory movement in the packet; no interrupt. |
| E07 or G08 passes on `FR`. | Continue the local science. | Report; `FR` never becomes a reader arm this stage. |
| Any `FM` arm beats `DOM` on the whole artifact. | Freeze candidate. | Never pooled across readers before the per-reader cells are read. |
| The locked useful work exhausts early. | Re-size the ladder from measured costs (§12.3) and admit rungs. | Only when the re-sized ladder also exhausts: short-run receipt and close. |

**One-repair rule:** each instrument family gets one predeclared repair that may not change the estimand, lower a floor after outcomes, add hidden evidence, or relabel a failed arm.

**Confirmation freeze:** at most three claims, chosen by rule: the strongest gate-passing reader effect against `DOM`; the strongest purpose or accumulation effect; a tail-only effect if one cleared its floor. Untouched lineages, no endpoint substitution, a failed confirmation never replaced.

### 9.1 Theory-change interrupts

The coding agent stops, writes the theory-group consequence, and waits for the curator's ruling when: an untrained reader passes the expertise gate; every trained reader fails it; an admitted reader's surprise cannot localize the maker's events even with the purpose supplied; or a result changes what a goal or expertise is. Routine repairs do not trigger it.

## 12. Runtime and workload contract

### 12.1 Corrected empirical prior

Stage 7 forecast 56.3 base hours and ran its base in about 12: the verdict clocks summed to 10.2 hours across 124 cards against a 57.8-hour forecast. The workload lock overestimated by three to five times and sized the ladder to 3.7 hours, so seven of nine rungs never opened. **Size from the pilot's measured unit costs, then re-size once from the base run's measured costs before admitting the ladder (§12.3).** Two costs Stage 7 never measured and this stage must: adapter training time per reader per epoch, and generation time per world for the generation gate.

### 12.2 One clock

The 48-hour ceiling begins at the discarded pilot. Pilot, training, sealed startup, inference, scoring, repairs, confirmation, validation, and closure share the clock; restarts preserve the deadline. The useful-work target is 36 to 42 hours, leaving 6 to 12 for confirmation, validation, and safety. Exactly one packet after closure; no early or partial packets.

### 12.3 Sizing rule

At the pilot's end, lock the base workload from measured unit costs. When the base completes, **re-lock the ladder from the base run's actual per-card costs** and admit rungs in order while the re-sized budget lasts; the rung multipliers are computed at admission, never frozen from the pilot forecast. If the re-sized ladder also exhausts before the floor, write the short-run receipt and close.

### 12.5 The frontier cap

`FR` is capped at **forty dollars** for the stage, counted per call in the compute ledger with the model identifier and unit price recorded before the first call, a hard stop at the cap, and no other paid API anywhere in the stage. `FR` runs E07, D05, G08 only, in that order, on the same worlds as the local readers, and stops when the cap or its cells are exhausted. Its result licenses one sentence: whether the Stage 7 boundary is a small-model boundary on this construction. The curator's value rule governs the choice of model: the cheapest reasoning model whose verbalized distribution passes a known-answer calibration fixture in the pilot; if none passes at the cap, `FR` is INSTRUMENT_FAILED and the dollars stop.

## 17. Decisions taken for the curator in this file

1. **Tail scoring is a co-headline, not the headline.** Every prospective contrast reports whole and tail side by side; neither is suppressed. (Ratification question 3 was not answered; this is the analyst's call.)

4. **The expertise gate is measured on the population, not on a maker's artifact**, so it cannot "messily grab pieces of the author": the reader must produce and predict the shared language, and only that. (Question 1, answered as "it's messy because it grabs the author.")
5. **The goal is built as a purpose, and the pull ordering is kept as a derived variable and scored beside it**, so the ontology question is measured rather than decided. (Question 2, answered as "both, kind of.")

7. **The testbed track is in scope and analysis of the new corpora is not**; the catalog is the deliverable, so Stage 9 can be designed against texture rather than downloads.

## 0. Executive ruling

The living theory is substantially closer to the curator's model than the discussion initially
suggested. The prior errata already installed the reader's self-model, viewer-coherent versus
historical process, context as probabilistic reweighting, mixed-production ratification, and the
possibility that an explicitly aligned system could produce human-invertible artifacts.

This pass makes five local corrections:

1. **Human invertibility belongs to a reader-artifact-context relation.** A language-model reader
   currently measures model invertibility. An intervention derived from human-labelled or
   human-theorized structure may measure engineered human-shaped invertibility. It does not become
   a human-reader result by naming the intervention after a human mechanism.
2. **Self-reconstruction is a similarity shortcut.** The reader starts with itself because shared
   architecture makes that model available and useful, not because self-projection is an
   intrinsically correct inverse algorithm.
3. **The conserved affective proposal concerns pre-solved constraints, not the entirety of
   emotion.** Cortical construction, context, and learned expertise can vary substantially while
   conserved subcortical machinery still narrows the candidate space.
4. **Roughly 27 is a soft descriptive ceiling, not a primitive count.** It is not a PCA result, not
   Panksepp's method, and not evidence for 27 subcortical channels. Behaviorally separated,
   nameable categories may include cortical construction in the Barrett sense.
5. **Mechanistic intervention is an analogue test.** PyTorch can implement capture, ablation,
   amplification, or a learned low-rank deformation. A causal gain would establish that an
   affect-related representation helps a model reader under the tested construction. It would not
   establish a recreated midbrain, embodied experience, or human empathy.

The editorial ruling is strict:

> **No existing curator quotation is deleted, rewritten, or marked superseded by this pass.**

The new language narrows AI-authored claims and adds distinctions. It does not establish that any
older curator claim was withdrawn. In particular, the ordinary-model non-invertibility claim and
the fully-aligned-model invertibility claim describe different production regimes and remain
compatible.

## 1. Fidelity protocol for reconstructed curator language

The source is an error-prone audio transcript. Punctuation, false sentence boundaries, repetitions,
and obvious recognition substitutions have been repaired. Claims have not been made more elegant,
more categorical, or more complete than the speech supports.

| Class | Meaning | May enter a curator blockquote? |
|---|---|---|
| **A: high-fidelity reconstruction** | One contiguous thought with fillers, abandoned starts, punctuation, and obvious transcript errors repaired. No proposition added. | Yes. Insert exactly as written here. |
| **B: composite reconstruction** | One proposition assembled from several adjacent statements or passes. Content is faithful, wording is reconstructed. | No. Translate into clearly AI-authored prose unless the curator later adopts it verbatim. |
| **C: analyst formulation** | A distinction, consequence, or guardrail inferred from the curator's statements and the evidence record. | Never. It remains AI-authored prose. |

Permitted cleanup for class A:

- punctuation and paragraph boundaries;
- removal of fillers, repeated starts, and transcription debris;
- restoration of obvious terms such as **Panksepp** and **PyTorch**;
- removal of a duplicated phrase when the intended sentence is unambiguous;
- retention of uncertainty, profanity, humor, and colloquial comparisons when they carry meaning.

Not permitted:

- joining separate tensions into a resolution and calling it a quote;
- turning “may,” “I think,” or “I do not know” into a claim of fact;
- replacing a vivid ordinary word with a technical term the curator did not use;
- editing an existing blockquote to resemble a newer reconstruction;
- treating the transcript's malformed grammar as evidence that the underlying thought was malformed.

## 3. What the reconstructed language changes

### 3.1 Invertibility has three reader-qualified meanings

Use the following vocabulary everywhere after application:

| Term | Reader | What can validate it |
|---|---|---|
| **model invertibility** | a specified model reader | recorded choices, process logs, withheld facts, calibration, and abstention |
| **engineered human-shaped invertibility** | a model reader given a human-labelled or human-theorized prior | the same external records plus matched generic, random, and synthetic controls |
| **human invertibility** | specified human readers under declared context and expertise | known-answer human-reader trials, preferably with prospective or process-recorded targets |

These are not three ontologies of the artifact. They are three measurement relations. A result may
transfer from one to another, but the transfer must be demonstrated.

This also makes two older claims coexist cleanly:

- ordinary model production may lack the unforced trace structure a human reader naturally uses;
- a system trained to make its operative constraints recoverable may produce unusually
  human-invertible artifacts.

The second does not refute the first. It changes the production regime.

### 3.2 Shared architecture narrows; it does not identify

The self-model is useful because reader and maker share some generative organization. It proposes a
smaller, better-shaped candidate family. Evidence about biography, tools, culture, expertise, and
context then reweights that family away from the reader.

Three errors remain possible:

1. **projection:** the reader finds a coherent route it would use and mistakes it for the maker's;
2. **blindness:** unfamiliar expertise or embodiment makes the true route absent from the reader's
   candidate family;
3. **over-flexibility:** the reader can explain any outcome by inventing enough latent goals.

Similarity therefore predicts a change in calibrated recovery, not a collapse to certainty. Model
family is a tractable analogue because maker and reader architectures can be crossed explicitly;
it is not a biological substitute.

### 3.3 The affective proposal is constraint geometry, not an emotion dictionary

The load-bearing human proposal is that conserved affective and action-control structure narrows a
human reader's possible maker models. It does not require:

- one localized seat of emotion;
- seven mutually orthogonal channels;
- twenty-seven primitive emotions;
- a one-to-one map from emotion words to neural systems;
- an exact transformer block corresponding to a human region.

Panksepp's seven, Cowen and Keltner's 27 reportable categories, GoEmotions' 27 language labels, and
valence-arousal-dominance are competing **basis constructions**. They answer different questions.
None currently determines the natural rank of the useful model representation.

The number 27 may guide an upper end of a development sweep. It may not become a discovery claim,
a PCA target, or a biological count.

### 3.4 Mirror systems contribute evidence rather than mind-reading

The theory's defensible mirror claim is low-level and conditional. Observed movement can recruit an
observer's compatible motor organization, giving the larger inverse problem additional evidence.
The same logic can extend indirectly through facial movement and learned associations. It does not
establish automatic affect sharing, direct access to another's internal state, or a text-model
equivalent.

The current text-only phase therefore tests shared generative organization and affective
constraint. It does not test mirror neurons. A later multimodal action-prediction branch may test a
functional analogue without naming it neural homology.

### 3.5 A PyTorch intervention is causal only with respect to its measured behavior

PyTorch supplies the implementation substrate for an intervention. It does not supply a theory of
what the changed representation is. A basis earns increasingly strong language only in this order:

1. decodable on held-out data;
2. stable outside explicit label words and one topic;
3. causally changes a benign affect-sensitive behavior under amplification and ablation;
4. improves recovery of independently recorded process facts;
5. improves human-process targets more than matched model-process targets;
6. predicts a corresponding human-reader pattern.

The first four are model results. The fifth is a human-shaped selectivity result. Only the sixth
begins to bear on human invertibility directly.

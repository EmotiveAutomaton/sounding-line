# FIC1 constructed end-to-end consumer

This implements the existing FIC1 comparison's missing connection between
pooled reconstruction and fixed-support scoring. It is a constructed-only
engineering validation, not a new hypothesis or human-data admission. Original
source, scoring, context and failed attempts remain immutable.

DESIGN CHECK: LESSONS sections 3–5, CONTROLS sections 1–7 and the play-frame
row G220 with its correction and afterword were reread for this component.
Under identical prompt-only construction, person and independent-context scores
must agree and cannot clear positive admission. Under persistent, explicitly
planted writer fingerprints, both frozen numerical gates must pass. Missing
rosters, matrices or any reference draw prevent admission; none is redrawn.
These two deterministic worlds do not estimate false-positive rates, sensitivity
near a meaningful effect, human support or genre-effect power.

Construct nine writers, both genres and four shared prompts per genre. Every
session contains twelve repeated words typed character by character. In the
prompt-only world word identity and inter-character delay vary by prompt only;
in the planted world both vary by writer only. No writer identifier enters the
feature extractor. Raw events pass the existing occurrence, word, pair and
revision replay before forming the original sufficient records. All words,
timing, final text and dictionary membership are explicit and reconstructible.
The dictionary is the nine constructed words, one punctuation mark and no
character-bigram coordinates. Prefix levels 2, 4 and 8 are engineering scales,
not substitutes for the human study's 500, 1,000 and 2,000 words.

Nine writers give three writers per permutation role. This was chosen in
pre-run review because two writers per role would give the all-correct
short-prefix null a probability of one eighth across three roles, preventing
the frozen 0.05 gate from detecting even perfect fingerprints.

For every prepared channel/level and each genre, use each of the three frozen
writer rotations once, join only its declared probe writers, and retain its
full declared gallery. Run raw matrices plus the existing full-length nuisance
adjustments and final-text length/provenance adjustment. Each of 1,000 draws
uses one within-prompt, within-role writer assignment shared across rotations,
rebuilding halves, prefixes, pooled context and B-only nuisance fits. Original
rosters, folds and probe/gallery memberships must match every draw. Retain all
draw score maps, including unsupported maps, before applying the unchanged
scoring core and its 2,000 writer resamples. No synthetic repeated score maps
stand in for actual reconstructed draws. Retain literal leave-writer-out scores
as an explicitly non-licensing diagnostic.

Known-answer admission requires prompt-only observed/context equality in every
variant and no positive numerical admission, and a positive numerical result in
every planted variant. Test missing fixed support, altered topology, duplicate
probes, order invariance, observed-versus-context routing and unchanged-input
replay. The complete handler refuses human mode and noncanonical fixtures.
Full end-to-end verification reconstructs all raw worlds and all draws in a
fresh process; incomplete output never becomes a completed validation.

Charge prior FIC1 costs/reserves of 2,813.234375 CPU seconds. Reserve 180 more
seconds for construction and targeted checks. Native validation is admitted
only after measured complete-draw timing, with a component cap no greater than
2,000 CPU seconds (execution plus independent replay), one numerical thread,
3 GiB projected RAM and 128 MiB output. The original cumulative ceiling remains
7,200 seconds; no S4 extension or engineering retry is invoked. A cost refusal
keeps all evidence and leaves the consumer unvalidated. Full scientific null
and planted calibration, whole-corpus cost admission and AUTH remain separate.

# LGH6 · Does voicing before a join predict whether another person joins?

Use the original admitted transcripts and assigned microphones for 75 ICSI and 171 AMI meetings. Reproduce LGH1’s selection of the held-out half from the six original measurements, their completeness, event order and seed 2026112205. A valid pre-join interval can enter the all-laugh description even if one of the six earlier measurements was missing. Keep the two corpora separate. AMI figures retain their restricted handling.

End each interval at the earliest of the annotated end, one second after onset or the onset of another speaker’s laugh. A simultaneous start counts as joined and supplies zero pre-join sound. Exclude and count intervals with under 200 ms of active sound. Use the original 40 ms frames, 10 ms step and 1% of the loudest frame’s RMS within the available interval as the activity threshold. The union of active frame spans gives elapsed active time. Voicing is the fraction of active frames that meet LGH1’s pitch criterion. Level is the RMS of the same active sound in dB relative to the original speech-channel reference. Count other speakers’ laugh starts from 60 seconds before onset up to, and excluding, three seconds before onset.

Fit three linear probability models. The first includes a speaker intercept and voicing, the second adds level and the earlier laugh count, and the third also includes a meeting intercept. Use the same eligible observations within each panel and decline an unidentified coefficient. Resample whole meetings 2,000 times. The one-sided shuffle for the first coefficient permutes voicing among one speaker’s laughs in one meeting 1,000 times. The held-out reading is primary. Also file the all-laugh reading and the already-released component profile where permitted. No speaker-keyed table leaves the job.

**Design check.** Construct single- and mixed-frequency waveforms, a voiced tail after another speaker joins, tied starts, short active intervals and an earlier laugh by the same speaker. Compare the fixed-effect calculation against a direct dummy-variable regression and a planted within-speaker effect. Recreate the original held-out roster and earlier disclosed memberships before releasing a new table.

Laughter that overlaps speech is not in the ICSI sample, and that may be a kind of its own. A funnier moment can make a laugh both more voiced and more likely to draw others. An association cannot distinguish these mechanisms.

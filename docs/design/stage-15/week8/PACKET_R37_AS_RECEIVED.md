### TMP2. The sealed trait job, by rule (the handoff's S1; claim P5; the map's trait labels)

- **Build.** Extend `week7_tmp1_shell.py`, `prepare_tmp1_real.py` and `tools/week7_tmp1_start.py` for card TMP2 on the Week 8 gear, and pin their hashes again after the rehearsal on invented histories. The shell resolves each trait field inside the seal and stops with a code unless exactly one column matches. The receipt's `mount` is the read-only alias under which the two archives are staged on the sealed route; if none is staged, the staging request is the return's first line in Week 8's first hour. The projection becomes two files, the comments file and the profiles file as staged, and the adapter is extended so that the exclusions are computed from step 4 inside the job and not read from a third file. `receipt_sha256` is the digest of the receipt as filed. The mapping note is this row's text, filed as `docs/design/stage-15/week8/TMP2_MAPPING.md`, and its hash is `provider_binding_sha256`. If a field still cannot be filled inside this repository, that is the return's first line and the build goes on.
- **The bundle.** The engine delivers a second bundle (its card TP72) that takes the exclusions from the receiver and adds a random-heads row to every table. It is staged for the sealed jobs the way the first was. If staging needs someone outside this repository, that request is the return's first line in the hour the bundle arrives. If the bundle has not arrived twelve hours after Week 8 opens, the row runs with the first bundle and its sheet says that its numbers have no random-heads row.
- **The rule (amendment DJ).**
  1. Keys. The join uses the one column named `author` in each file's header. If either file does not have exactly one such column, stop with a code.
  2. Trait fields. The five profile columns whose names are the five trait names, letter case aside. Inside the job, check that every value present lies from 0 to 100 and that at least one in ten is above 1. If not, stop with a code. Units are recorded as percentiles from 0 to 100, checked in the seal. The delivered baseline documents the profile author key and the five trait fields (`docs/TOOLS.md`); if those documented names are the columns the rule finds, the receipt records them.
  3. Scored authors are those with all five scores.
  4. Exclusions, before any reader sees a text. (a) A reporting comment is a comment by a scored author that names two or more of the five traits, each followed within 40 characters by a number. Every community that holds a reporting comment is excluded for every author. (b) Every community whose name contains one of these is excluded: mbti, myers, enneagram, socionics, personality, bigfive, big5, typology, jung, keirsey, hexaco, or any of the sixteen four-letter type codes. (c) Any comment left that fits the reporting pattern, or names a personality test, is dropped.
  5. Text kept. Authors with at least 100 comments and 5,000 words left. At most each author's latest 1,000 comments.
  6. Beside TP58's tables the job returns counts only: scored authors, authors kept, communities excluded under (a) and under (b), comments dropped under (c). A count under 11 is withheld, and so is any count whose difference from another returned count is under 11.
- **Registered.** As card TP58 has it for the curator's map, with print's map beside it as the engine's card TP63 words it, and a random-heads row in every table.
- **Sealed runs.** Two: one that returns step 6's counts alone, one for the study. If the gate refuses a table without intervals, the counts go out beside TP58's first table in the study run, and the row uses one.
- **Limit sentence.** The scores are people's own reports of several tests, mapped to percentiles, from people who chose to post them. A rule can miss a community.
- **Prior art.** The set's own paper (Gjurković and others 2021) predicted these scores from word counts and community counts at correlations of .27 to .39 with 1,000 comments an author, and at .12 to .21 from a neural reader on 100. It names no collection communities and does not say that reporting comments were removed. A fair comparison for the engine's reader is its own random-heads row, not those figures.

### SLD5. The loss study with a comparison group (S2; claim N5; ruling R48)

- **Build.** Extend SLD2's runner. Bind, from the codebooks, in a binding note filed before any outcome is opened: marital status at each of the three waves; the wave-three dated death and divorce fields of first and most recent marriages, with the wave-three interview month; and age at wave one. Age is read only to form decades for the comparison, as the handoff asks. SLD2's items and main scoring are unchanged: the three purpose items, with wave-one "don't know" left out. SLD2's second scoring is not run, so that the comparison roster is one roster.
- **Quantities.** For each loss group (a spouse's death; a divorce), and for the two pooled, against the comparison roster of section 1: the change from wave one to two, from one to three and from two to three, each less the comparison roster's change. This is the registered form. Beside it, as a description: the later wave's score with the earlier wave's score and decade of age held (the second form). Then the check before the loss: the later-loss group's change from wave one to two against the comparison roster's, if that group numbers 20 or more.
- **Verdict rule, fixed here, on the registered form.** "Falls and stays" if the change from one to two and the change from one to three are both clear below the comparison's, and the change from two to three is not clear above it. "Comes back" if the change from one to two is clear below and the change from two to three is clear above. Otherwise "undecided". A verdict of undecided is not read as coming back, and the verdict for the pooled groups is filed beside each group's own.
- **Registered (his, 25 November).** After a spouse's death purpose falls more than in the comparison group by the second wave and is no higher at the third than at the second.
- **The rival (the results thread's guess of 19 November).** It comes back.
- **Pre-flight (`pf_loss.py`, `pf_loss2.py`, `pf_loss3.py`).** Made panels with a comparison group and a score whose stable part is about a third of its variance (assumed; the record gives no reliability for the three items), 200 runs a world, at the death group's size (80 people) and at the pooled size (207), everyone at all three waves. With nothing planted the rule said "falls and stays" in 0% and 1.5% of runs. With a lasting drop of 0.30 planted, about the size row SLD2 reported for a spouse's death, it said so in 40.5% of runs at the death group's size and 82.5% at the pooled size. With a drop that comes back it said "comes back" in 40.5% and 82.5%, and "falls and stays" in 1.5% and 0.5%. So for the death group alone the rule misses a real lasting drop more often than it finds one, and the pooled groups carry the test. The second form read minus 0.17 and minus 0.17 where the loss groups had only been lower all along, clear of zero in 34% and 61.5% of runs, while the registered form read 0.00 and 0.01 there. That is why the second form is a description. When a dip came before the loss and nothing after, the registered form read a rise of 0.22 and 0.20. The check before the loss caught that dip in 29% and 58% of runs when the later-loss group was as large as the loss group, and in 16.5% and 12.5% when it had 30 people. When a dip came before a lasting drop, the registered form saw only 0.10 of the 0.30. So the three figures are read together, and a small later-loss group makes the check weak.
- **Sealed runs.** Two at most. The row's new rosters are the comparison roster and the later-loss group. SLD2's six released rosters are passed to the gate as known. No cell by decade of age is returned.
- **Limit sentences.** "One kind of role, and divorce may be chosen where a death is not." "The wave-one scale had no neutral point, so a change from wave one to wave two is partly a change of scale." The comparison roster answered the same scales at the same waves, so that change is in both groups; it stays a limit if the two groups used the midpoint differently. The scale is three items, so a null is weak. Everyone here stayed in the survey across all three waves.
- **Prior art.** In a German panel, life satisfaction began to fall before a spouse's death and had not fully returned eight years after (Lucas and others 2003). A later study gave matched people a made-up date of loss as a check (the SHARE study the analyst's helper read). No study of purpose after widowhood in this panel was found.

### SLD6. Traits and kinds of daily stressor (S3; claims S3, P4)

- **Build.** Bind the diary's other stressor items into the three kinds of section 1, from the codebook, in a binding note. Check in the seal whether the wave-two survey holds composites for Conscientiousness and Openness beside the three traits the contract binds (a check under amendment DL). The kinds, and those two traits if the check finds them, are added to the source contract (`mi_source_contract_v1`), with its hashes reviewed again under amendment CQ's readiness rule. SLD3's operator is unchanged, with these days: for one kind, an exposure day is a day with that kind and no other, a reference day is a day with no stressor, and every other day is left out.
- **Quantities.** For each trait and kind, the trait's effect on the response to that kind, with the number of people supported. For each trait, one contrast: its effect on conflict less the mean of its effects on the other two kinds. If row SLD8 binds the first diary, each estimate is pooled across the two samples by inverse variance, and the pooled figures are the ones read.
- **Registered (his map's).** Each trait multiplies the response to its own need's events more than to the others, Agreeableness on arguments above all: Agreeableness's effect on conflict is above zero, and so is its contrast.
- **Registered (print's: Suls, Martin and David 1998).** Neuroticism multiplies every kind, and Agreeableness shows on conflict.
- **What can tell the two apart.** Both expect Agreeableness on conflict. They part on whether Agreeableness's effect is larger there than elsewhere, and on whether Neuroticism's contrast is near zero.
- **Pre-flight (`pf_kind.py`, `pf_kind2.py`).** Made diaries of 1,800 people and eight days with a floor under the feeling score, 100 runs a world, by SLD3's operator on the days named above. With nothing planted an effect cleared zero in 0 to 7 of 100 runs and a contrast in 2 to 3. A simpler form, the slope on the trait with her level held, cleared zero in 52 of 100 runs for a trait that also raises her level, which is why SLD3's operator is kept. With a trait that also raises her level, and raises the response to every kind by a quarter of its mean size for each standard deviation of the true trait, the operator found it in 29 of 100 runs for conflict (about 480 people supported), 8 for overload (about 180) and 5 for something that happened to someone close (about 54). With a trait that leaves her level alone and does so for conflict only, it found the effect on conflict in 71 of 100 runs and the contrast in 13. So the contrast that would tell the map from print is close to blind at this size.
- **How it is filed.** As estimates with their intervals. A contrast that does not clear zero is "undecided", never "against".
- **Sealed runs.** The check, one run for each trait, and one that moves the trait scores between people at random as CAL3 did: seven at most. The row's new rosters are the people supported for each kind. A kind whose roster fails the gate is void.
- **Limit sentence.** Something that happened to someone close is rare in eight days, so few people have a slope for it. A null there is not evidence.
- **Prior art.** In this panel's second diary, Neuroticism, Conscientiousness, Openness and Extraversion were each tied to how much a stressor day raised bad feeling, and Agreeableness was not (Leger and others 2016). No study was found that crosses each trait with each kind.

### LKG1. The liking gap for each speaker (S4; the ledger's items 27 and 57)

- **Build.** A sealed job on the conversation set's survey, keyed by the speaker id that repeats across her conversations. Bind the item for how much she liked her partner (`i_like_you` in the dictionary note) and the item for how much she thinks her partner liked her (the survey census lists `you_like_me`; bind it from the dictionary, and if the dictionary does not define it the row is void).
- **Quantities.** The mean gap. For speakers with two or more conversations: how alike a speaker's gaps are across her conversations (an intraclass correlation); the same after her own liking of each partner is held; the same for her estimate less her own liking; and the correlation between her first and second conversation. And a moment estimate of how much speakers differ in a bias of the gauge, at three assumed weights of her own liking in her estimate (0.25, 0.5 and 0.75), as `pf_gap2.py` computes it. It is filed as a description, and it is not filed at all if her partners' ratings of her do not repeat across her conversations clear of zero, because the estimate divides by that repeat. If fewer than 20 speakers have two conversations, the row files the mean gap and the count, and stops.
- **Registered (print's).** Below zero on average.
- **Registered (the Theory and Research thread's).** It repeats inside a speaker, as a gauge's bias would.
- **How the second guess is scored.** If the repeat with her own liking held is not clear of zero, the guess is against. If it is clear, the row files "needed, and not shown by this", because the pre-flight found the same repeat with no bias planted.
- **Pre-flight (`pf_gap.py`, `pf_gap2.py`).** Made worlds of about 770 speakers with two or more conversations, 200 runs a world; that count follows the set's own paper, in which over half of the speakers had two or more. With no gauge bias, and people who differ in how they use the scale and in how likable they are, the gap's repeat read 0.17 and cleared zero in every run. With her own liking held it read 0.07 and cleared zero in 82.5% of runs. With no differences in scale use either, the repeat still cleared zero in 52.5% of runs, because how likable she is enters every one of her gaps. A planted gauge bias moved the repeat only from 0.17 to 0.24. So a repeat is needed for the thread's guess and cannot confirm it. The moment estimate was run at assumed weights of 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 where the true weight was 0.5. With nothing planted it cleared zero in 3.5% of runs at most. Against a planted spread of 0.16 it read 0.11 to 0.16, and cleared zero in 34.5% to 53% of runs. So a wrong weight moves it little, and it misses a real bias about as often as it finds one.
- **Sealed runs.** One.
- **Limit sentence.** A person's own way of using the scale, and how likable she is, each make a gap repeat with no bias in any gauge.
- **Prior art.** The gap itself (Boothby and others 2018). In work on how people think others see them, about a third of the spread in such estimates belongs to the person making them (Kenny's summaries), so a repeat is expected.

### LGH6. Voicing and joiners, inside one laugher (S6; claim T7; amendment DM)

- **Build.** In the meeting-sound job, the per-laugh measure is extended to take a part of a laugh, and a table with one line a laugh is kept inside the job: laugher, meeting, voicing before a join, level over the same frames, the count of other speakers' laugh starts in the sixty seconds before (leaving out the three seconds just before), and joined or not.
- **Quantities.** Whether a laugh was joined, on its voicing before a join, with each laugher's own means taken off. Then with level and the count of earlier laughs held. Then with each meeting's means taken off as well. A shuffle of voicing among one laugher's laughs in one meeting, 1,000 times, gives the null. The registered reading is on LGH1's held-out half of laughs, as the handoff asks; the same on all laughs is filed beside it.
- **Sources.** The first meeting set (75 meetings). The second (171 meetings), where a table that fails that corpus's release check is void with its code, and a table its terms restrict is filed internal.
- **Also filed, from LGH1's and LGH3's released component tables.** The sound profile of the small group after which the target's share of talk fell, where that profile is releasable.
- **Registered (print's: Bachorowski and Owren 2001; and across laughers in this corpus, Laskowski and Burger 2007).** More voicing, more joiners.
- **Registered (the results thread's).** It holds inside a laugher, so the groups are not only people.
- **Pre-flight (`pf_join.py`).** Made meetings (60 meetings of about 110 laughs, 100 runs a world). With nothing planted the weight cleared zero in 1 to 2 of 100 runs. When voicing draws joiners it did so in every run. When a laugh only becomes more voiced once others have joined, a whole-laugh measure cleared zero in every run and the measure before a join in 2 to 5 of 100, which is why voicing is measured before a join. When the mood of the meeting or the funniness of the moment raises both voicing and joining, and voicing itself does nothing, the weight still cleared zero in every run, with level and earlier laughs held or not. So this row can speak against the claim and cannot confirm it.
- **Limit sentences.** "Laughter that overlaps speech is not in the sample, and that may be a kind of its own." A funnier moment makes a laugh both more voiced and more likely to be joined, and nothing here rules that out.

### RML1. PLAY at a steady level (S7; claim T7; ruling R49; amendment DM)

- **Build.** The operator for the room level of section 1, on both meeting sets, with a native fixture. If it is not qualified by the freeze, the row moves to section 4, as the handoff says.
- **Quantities.** The number of laughs that start in a half minute, on five things measured over the three half minutes before it: how much the level changed, whichever way; the signed change; the level; the level squared; and whether a high was followed by a fall. The count of laughs in those three half minutes is held, and each meeting's means are taken off. Intervals from resampling meetings.
- **Registered (his).** "What if play's on switch is a constant level of stress?" Laughs start more often after a stretch in which the level changed little: the weight on the size of the change is below zero with the level held.
- **Registered (the Theory and Research thread's).** And less often when the level is high: the weight on the level is below zero.
- **The rival (relief; the analyst's reading of accounts in which humour needs a threat that has passed).** Laughs start after a high that falls: that weight is above zero.
- **Pre-flight (`pf_room.py`).** Made meetings (60 meetings of an hour in half minutes, 100 runs a world), each read with a clean level and with a level that laughter leaks into. With nothing planted a weight cleared zero in 2 to 7 of 100 runs. His switch, planted, was found in 97 to 99 of 100 runs, and the weight on the level, planted beside it, in 100. The relief rival, planted, was found in 87 to 100 of 100 runs, and his weight then cleared zero in at most 5. ### ACC1. The slope of the slope in the phone game (S8; claims A1, T2, T3)

- **Build.** A runner on verified first plays (42,380 in the record): the standard model of section 1; then the same with one more term, the change in the change of a player's results over her last three trials. The term is fitted a second time inside row ADP1's two-rate model, and a third time in the standard model without players whose ratings sit at the top or the bottom of the scale on more than a quarter of their ratings. The form and the fading rate are fixed on a random 70% of players drawn by seed, and the term and its interval are read once on the other 30%. The term is read as above zero only if all three fits clear zero. If they disagree, the row is unresolved.
- **Registered (in print: Hsee, Salovey and Abelson 1994; Joffily and Coricelli 2013).** A positive weight.
- **Pre-flight (`pf_accel.py`, `pf_accel2.py`).** Made plays (3,000 players, thirty trials and twelve ratings each, 100 runs a world). With the standard model true and nothing added, the added term cleared zero in 4 of 100 runs, and in 5 when the fading was slow. A planted term of 0.02 was found in every run and read 0.019. A second form was tried and failed: free weights on the last three surprises showed the pattern plus, minus twice, plus in every run of the plain world, because any fading curve bends that way. It is the trap the addendum of 25 November warned of, and that form is not used. When good and bad surprises fade at different rates and the scale has a ceiling, the added term rose above zero in 15 of 100 runs with nothing planted (9 with different rates alone, 7 with a ceiling alone). In a second set of runs the same fit gave 16, 10 and 8, and the term fitted inside the two-rate model gave 10, 3 and 8 of 100, and still found the planted term in every run (`pf_accel2.py`). So the second fit takes out what different rates add, and a ceiling still leaves the term a little too easy to find. That is why the row asks for all three fits.
- **Limit sentence.** A rating comes every two or three trials, so the last three results can reach back past the rating before.
- **Prior art.** The standard model is Rutledge and others (2014). A later study compared ways of forming the expectation on these data and tested no such term (Keren and others 2021). No test of this term on these data was found.

# ACC1 and ADP1 · Surprise and reported happiness in the phone game

Use the verified CC0 task projection from MD-source.h1. Its 42,380 records are first attempted plays, each from a different player, with 30 trials and 12 ratings. Read only this projection’s first-play array. The original source is Rutledge’s Great Brain Experiment data. [Task deposit](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.prr4xgxkk).

Split players once with NumPy’s default generator, seed 2026112708. The first 70% of the permutation form the development set and the rest form the test set. Keep this split in the two studies and their scale-end checks. Centre the outcome and predictors inside each player, as in the packet preflight. The first happiness report precedes all rewards and has an empty history. All later ratings follow the recorded trial. Happiness is in original 0–100 points. The sensitivity panel removes players with more than three of twelve ratings at either endpoint, counting both endpoints together.

The base model fits the fading sums of certain rewards, expected gamble values and reward prediction errors on MD1’s 0.05–0.90 grid in 0.05 steps. For the asymmetric model, retain the development estimate of the certain-reward and expected-value rate. Split surprise by sign and fit two rates on the same grid with one shared surprise coefficient. Select rates by the smallest within-player residual sum of squares. Break exact ties in ascending grid order. The scale-end sensitivity repeats development on its own eligible training subset.

**ACC1.** Add the current realized reward minus twice the previous realized reward plus the one before it. The term is zero before three outcomes have arrived. Freeze all rates using only the development set and its base models. Read the added coefficient in the held-out players in the one-rate, two-rate, and one-rate endpoint-sensitivity fits. Resample whole held-out players 2,000 times with rates fixed. The registered positive reading needs all three lower bounds above zero. A rating comes every two or three trials, so the three results may reach before the preceding rating.

**ADP1.** Fix the form, grid, common coefficient and certain/expected-value rate on development players. Estimate bad-surprise rate minus good-surprise rate on held-out players once, then refit that same grid on each whole-player bootstrap draw. Use both the full and endpoint-sensitivity panels. A positive difference means the response to a bad surprise decayed more slowly. Record grid endpoints and uncertainty. A few minutes of a game is not a life, and a rating scale has ends.

**Design check.** Compare the histories against MD1’s scalar clock, recover an independently planted acceleration and an independently planted asymmetric pair of rates, verify the scale-end exclusion and show that altering held-out happiness cannot change training selection. Refuse unidentified designs. Each card has 20,000 CPU-seconds including fresh replay.

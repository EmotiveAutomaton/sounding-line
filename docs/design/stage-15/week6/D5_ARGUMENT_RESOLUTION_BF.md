# D5.s · Next-day feeling after an argument

Amendment BF, proposed by the analyst and approved by the curator on November 20, 2026, fixes the wave-two MIDUS replication before the first real scientific run. The prediction is that more negative feeling remains the next day after an unresolved argument. The practical boundary is zero. [Commissioning packet, §5.4](PACKET_EXTENSION_2026-11-20.md) · [Original codebook and source pins](SM_SCIENCE_SOURCE_SEQUENCE.md).

## Who contributes

Read the person key, study day, seven stressor occurrence items and argument resolution first. An argument requires an affirmative argument occurrence. Among those days, resolution code 1 means resolved and 2 means unresolved. Retain an argument day only if the same person reported on the immediately following study day. A gap or an unknown resolution supplies neither category. Check for duplicate person-days. Count the people with eligible days of both kinds and the argument days of each kind among those people, before reading feelings. Report the all-report supports separately. Write support and outcome-complete denominators separately in the gated aggregate tables.

Use the mean of the original fourteen 0–4 daily negative-feeling items on days with all fourteen valid, following the original diary’s complete-outcome convention. For each person estimate their baseline from their observed, complete feeling scores on days when all seven stressors were explicitly absent. Deduct this same baseline from each negative-feeling score. Record missing baseline, next-day and argument-day outcomes through the contributing-person denominators. The baseline cancels algebraically from a person's contrast but matters for the all-reports estimate.

## Comparisons and decision

1. **Primary.** Within each person with both argument types and usable next-day outcomes, take the average baseline-adjusted next-day feeling after unresolved arguments minus the corresponding average after resolved arguments. Average the differences with equal weight per person. Report the estimate wherever the gate permits, including below the support floor. Fewer than 100 people with both types at the prospective support check gives “could not say.”
2. **All reports, descriptive.** Use every eligible report with the person's baseline and next-day outcome available, including people observed with only one argument type. Give each report equal weight within its resolution category, then subtract the resolved mean from the unresolved mean. Record the distinct contributors to both sides. If its point estimate and the primary point estimate have opposite signs, explicitly report disagreement and give no verdict.
3. **Same-day severity.** Compare negative feeling on the argument day between unresolved and resolved arguments in the primary people, with the same within-person weighting and baseline. Report the number with both usable same-day conditions. A higher starting level on unresolved days can account for part of the next-day difference.

Use 2,000 draws under amendment AW. Draw whole families if the joined survey has the family key and whole people if it lacks that column. Report the latter assumption. An incomplete family linkage preserves the point and withholds the affected interval. Use the same draw weights on each person's observations throughout each contrast. A qualifying primary needs its 95% interval entirely above zero, sufficient exposure support and agreement in direction with the available descriptive comparison. A zero point is neither positive nor negative. Each return row counts actual people. The common writer drops rows with fewer than eleven people, and the existing cross-table complementary-disclosure check still applies. No individual figure or draw leaves the sealed job.

Whether an argument was resolved is the person's own report on the same call. Arguments can also differ in initial severity and in other ways that resolution alone does not identify.

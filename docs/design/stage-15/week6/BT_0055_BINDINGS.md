# Member 0055 admission

Wang and colleagues studied reciprocal changes in family conflict and mood in young adults using daily and momentary networks. They also examined recovery from conflict. The commissioned comparison uses the same momentary constructs and tests whether the differences between people's slopes recur in separate parts of the diary. The source paper describes 77 participants. The released table contains 72 people across 10,428 rows. [Original design and measures](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11424776/) · [Release codebook](https://openesmdata.org/datasets/0055_wang/)

Version 2.0.0, CC BY-NC 4.0. The runner verifies the collector's manifest and the momentary TSV before use. Each row carries a person and study day. Use the 1–100 momentary family fighting slider, floor 1, and the momentary sad, anxious and angry sliders with the same bounds. Preserve missing items and scheduled skips. Use only paired observations for each fit. The daily summary and family cohesion fields are excluded. The participant's impression of fighting and the feeling come from the same prompt.

## Estimand and uncertainty

Within each person, fit ordinary least-squares mood against minutes since the first block rating. At least three observations and two minutes of span are needed. Average these slopes equally over people in each condition. This preserves the original mood unit and avoids weighting frequent raters or long sessions as extra participants. Report all condition slopes and three pairwise contrasts. Primary: **visuomotor minus rest**. Random gambling has no asserted ordinal place on learnability and its contrasts are descriptive companions.

Practical bar fixed by the analyst before outcomes: **0.002 mood units/minute**, or 0.2 points/minute on the 0–100 scale (1.4 points over seven minutes). Use 2,000 seeded whole-person bootstrap draws independently within condition. Each draw refits the corresponding equal-person slope mean; OLS sufficient statistics are exact. Report percentile 95% intervals, participant/cohort support and all exclusions. With fewer than 20 people in either primary condition, produce the same available descriptive estimates but mark the primary unsupported/inconclusive; do not raise the floor later or turn poor support into a new retrieval hold.

Three-way rule: supports the proxy if the contrast's lower bound exceeds the practical bar and the rest slope's upper bound is below zero; against if its whole interval lies within ±bar (equal practical drift), or its upper bound is below −bar (opposite ordering); otherwise inconclusive. An “against” equivalence reading remains conditional on an additional fixed-design minimum-effect sensitivity check before claiming evidence of absence.

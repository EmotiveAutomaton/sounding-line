# GAT2 · partner advantage and a yes decision

The curator fixed the primary and descriptions on November 24 at 16:20 and 16:25 Pacific. Apply the latter ruling on how to compute advantage. The keyed Columbia v1 distribution is approved for tests only. Numerical returns stay in the restricted repository record and the theory thread.

Use a common cohort for all three fits. Require the recorded yes/no decision, the partner's intelligence and ambition ratings and the rater's own baseline ratings on both attributes (1–10). Require one self-rating per attribute per rater, at least two complete encounters, and positive within-rater spread on both partner attributes. Count exclusions. Pool raters. Omit demographic fields.

Within each rater, put the self-rating and each partner rating on the same scale using that rater's mean and population standard deviation of partner ratings for that attribute. For the primary, average the two standardized partner-minus-self differences, then floor that average at zero. For each description, apply the floor to the single attribute's difference. Mixed gaps of +2 and −2 cancel in the primary. Two negative gaps also yield zero.

Follow the GAT1 within-rater logistic fitting convention. Include the partner's mean standardized rating, its square, the within-rater centered advantage, and the same leave-one-encounter-out yes-rate adjustment as GAT1. Use the corresponding partner attribute and its square in each single-attribute description. Use one set of 2,000 whole-rater resamples for all fits, preserving each rater's encounters and original scaling. Report an interval only with an identified point and all draws estimated. The registered coefficient must have its interval above zero. If the two descriptive coefficients have opposite signs and both intervals exclude zero, file the row as undecided.

All ratings are one rater's, made at one sitting, and liking a person colours every rating. The advantage term is told apart from the partner's rating only by its bend at the rater's own level, so any other curve in how ratings turn into choices can pass for it.

# Sounding Line Phase 2.4: Exploration-Forest Addendum

**Status:** Coding-agent addendum to `SOUNDING_LINE_PHASE_2_4_CONTEXT.md`

**Curator:** Abraham Haskins

**Prepared:** 2026-08-22

**Repository snapshot reviewed:** `a0ef40206f8e875100fae8bc0aee1e6326034dba`

**Repository destination if retained:** `docs/design/PHASE_2_4_EXPLORATION_ADDENDUM.md`

This addendum changes the **duration and branching policy** of Phase 2.4. It does not change the
theory, the G172-G180 definitions, their causal gates, or the requirement that confirmatory claims
use untouched data.

---

## 0. Why the addendum exists

The base Phase 2.4 package contains nine formal studies, but its execution rule says to run only
three roots and stop. That makes the phase function like a short diagnostic leg even though the
underlying question calls for sustained epistemic foraging.

The curator's search policy is:

> I want trees of tests that can keep running for a long time. I want more exploration than checks.

And the desired stopping signal is:

> I feel like we are circling around a real effect in the world that has power. If that is true,
> at some point we should get surprisingly positive results when we model the effect properly. I am
> expecting something like flight to pop up.

The second quotation is a wager, not evidence. A long exploration program needs branch deaths as
well as branch births; otherwise every null can be redescribed as the wrong implementation and the
search cannot update the theory.

The corrected operating rule is:

> **The Stage-1 pause is a theory-interpretation pause, not a compute pause.**

After the cold root map is written, the coding agent may continue through pre-authorized,
discovery-only scouts while the curator conducts the verbal walkthrough. It may not consume the
confirmation sets, promote a mechanism to theory, train the final adapter, or launch human-subject
work before that walkthrough.

## 1. What this addendum supersedes

This package supersedes only these parts of the base context:

| Base instruction | Replacement |
|---|---|
| “Run G172, G174, and G177 only.” | Run those first. Then continue eligible discovery scouts on development data. |
| “Stage 1 ends with a curator pause.” | Interpretation pauses. Reversible scout computation may continue. |
| “Cap new generation and GPU work to one ordinary local queue day.” | Use a rolling one-day queue cap: no single authorization exceeds one ordinary local queue day, but another day may begin automatically while approved scout branches remain and no stop condition fires. |
| A positive root opens at most one discriminator, one transfer, and one adversarial test. | That limit remains for **confirmatory promotion**. Discovery scouts may be broader because they cannot carry verdict language or touch confirmation data. |

Everything else in the base package remains binding, including external ground truth, separated
interfaces, proper scoring, surface controls, causal gates, documentation boundaries, and the
human-reader requirement for a human-invertibility claim.

## 2. Two lanes, one firewall

### 2.1 Confirmatory trunk

The formal G172-G180 tree remains the only route to `SUPPORTED`, `REJECTED`, or a “flight” claim.
Its cards freeze hypotheses, alternatives, practical bands, controls, and held-out data before
scoring.

### 2.2 Exploratory forest

The forest searches for mechanisms, task resolutions, intervention bases, and failure boundaries.
It may tune prompts, blocks, ranks, strengths, candidate families, and task constructions on a
development archive. Its outputs use only:

- `PROMISING`;
- `QUIET`;
- `RIVAL-FAVORED`;
- `INSTRUMENT-FAILED`;
- `PROMOTE-TO-CONFIRMATION`.

None of those is a theory verdict.

### 2.3 Data firewall

- Allocate confirmation cases before any scout reads outcomes.
- Keep all variants of one artifact, author, session, prompt, or model-generation lineage in one
  side of the firewall.
- A scout-selected basis, block, rank, threshold, or prompt is frozen before confirmation.
- If confirmation data are inspected during debugging, burn that split and create another.
- Every attempted scout remains in the registry, including quiet and failed ones. The forest is a
  multiplicity object, not a highlight reel.

The roots and wings are:

| **S: similarity** | G172 | whether shared lineage, architecture, geometry, or mere output fingerprints make inversion easier | G173 and G180 |

| **H: human process** | G177 | which recorded human choices survive in artifacts, deltas, and prospective states | G178, G176, G180 |

record them in the Phase 2.4 registry and assign global G identifiers only if repository convention

## 4. Wing S: maker-reader similarity

### E24-S01: crossed similarity root

This is G172 unchanged: exact maker checkpoint, sibling checkpoint, same family, different family,
and stronger cross-family reader on the same recorded process candidates.

### E24-S02: fingerprint destruction

Apply transformations intended to destroy generation-family surface cues while preserving the
verified consequence of the assigned choice:

- controlled paraphrase;
- punctuation and formatting normalization;
- sentence-order-preserving style flattening;
- lexical substitution that preserves the target decision;
- equal-length resampling where the target remains verified.

Re-run the matrix. If the same-family advantage disappears, the branch is about artifact dialect,
not shared generative organization.

### E24-S03: process-resolution ladder

Score four targets separately:

1. literal prompt recovery;
2. assigned proximal goal;
3. executed choice or constraint;
4. production route family.

The hypothesis is not that similarity helps all four. A fingerprint may help literal prompt or
route identity while contributing nothing to goal or executed-choice recovery. A human-analogue
interpretation requires the gain to survive at a process resolution that is not mere wording.

### E24-S04: lineage ladder

Separate:

- exact checkpoint;
- base and instruction-tuned siblings;
- two sizes from one family;
- independently trained members with similar architecture where available;
- different architectures trained on overlapping corpora where provenance permits.

This distinguishes shared weights, training lineage, architecture, and convergent semantics.

### E24-S05: capacity-direction asymmetry

Cross small maker to large reader, large maker to small reader, and capacity-matched cross-family
pairs. If stronger readers win regardless of maker relation, capacity explains the matrix. If
similarity helps only when reader capacity is adequate, similarity is conditional rather than
absent.

### E24-S06: tokenizer and likelihood control

Because raw likelihoods cannot be compared across tokenizers:

- use within-reader candidate ranks and proper scores;
- subtract neutral-conditioned likelihood;
- report tokenizer overlap and artifact compression length;
- repeat on a character-normalized representation through a cheap surface reader.

This scout closes the route by which token familiarity impersonates process familiarity.

### E24-S07: geometry predicts the matrix

Run the G173 descriptive half over a larger neutral corpus:

- correspondence-null-tested CKA;
- orthogonal Procrustes or ridge alignment;
- held-out concept-map transfer;
- pairwise relation between alignment and inversion after capacity and surface controls.

Raw CKA magnitude remains uninterpretable at the repository's small-sample, high-dimensional
regime. Only null-tested match structure carries weight.

### E24-S08: causal direction transfer

Where a maker-side process or goal direction can be aligned into a reader:

- map it into the reader;
- amplify and ablate it only during artifact tokens;
- test the candidate-specific proper score;
- compare with a mapped random and generic semantic direction.

This separates shared descriptive geometry from shared geometry the reader actually uses.

### E24-S09: controlled sibling creation

If local resources allow, produce several small LoRA or adapter variants from one base model under
different, known process distributions. The shared base is fixed; the learned production habit
varies. This controlled family permits a cleaner question than naturally occurring model families:
does a reader invert the sibling whose learned generation policy is closest, or merely the one
sharing the base?

Do not use process labels from this construction to fit the reader.

### E24-S10: directionality and self-inversion

Test whether the maker can invert its own outputs better than siblings after all outputs are
detached from their prompts and seeds. Exact self-advantage is compatible with several degenerate
accounts. The useful pattern is a graded relation extending from exact to sibling to aligned
cross-family, with surface cues controlled.

### Wing-S routing

| Pattern | Continue toward | Close or weaken |
|---|---|---|
| graded exact-sibling-aligned-cross-family effect survives trace erasure | G173 causal transfer | pure fingerprint account |
| only exact checkpoint wins | memorability or checkpoint residue scout | broad shared-architecture claim |
| same-family gain vanishes after paraphrase | surface-family baseline | geometry interpretation |
| alignment predicts and mapped intervention moves truth | G176 integration and G180 | lineage-only account |
| capacity explains every cell | stronger-reader baseline | similarity-specific mechanism |
| no target above artifact floor | prospective/process-aware wing | artifact-only similarity work |

## 5. Wing A: affective constraint engineering

### E24-A01: causal emotion-concept ruler

This is G174 unchanged. Reproduce abstract cross-context decoding and a benign causal behavior
effect in one open-weight family, then repeat the seed.

### E24-A02: lexical and actor transfer

Build contrasts in which:

- emotion words are absent;
- topic is held while affective situation changes;
- affective situation is held while topic changes;
- the state belongs alternately to speaker, listener, or third party;
- neutral ambiguity supports abstention.

This decides whether the basis represents affective situations, lexical labels, or actor position.

### E24-A03: fixed basis catalog

Fit competing bases without process labels:

- Panksepp-7 semantic systems;
- valence-arousal, with dominance separate;
- GoEmotions human-labelled language categories;
- Cowen-Keltner reported-experience geometry only where the underlying ratings can be used honestly;
- a human-labelled versus model-synthetic twin;
- generic semantic, syntax, topic, persona, and random controls.

These are candidate coordinate systems. They are not competing claims about the true number of
emotions.

### E24-A04: soft-rank sweep

On development data only, compare nested projector ranks such as 1, 2, 4, 7, 12, 18, and 27, plus
same-rank controls. The final grid may change after an effective-rank audit. Twenty-seven is a
convenient upper endpoint inherited from human report taxonomies, not a predicted optimum.

Report:

- held-out decoding;
- causal effect per degree of freedom;
- subspace stability;
- off-target movement;
- whether performance plateaus rather than which rank “wins.”

No result from this scout may be called an emotion count.

### E24-A05: label-source twins

Hold taxonomy, templates, examples per label, extraction method, rank, and intervention norm fixed.
Vary only whether the contrast examples or similarity geometry come from human annotation or model
synthesis. A human-labelled advantage is necessary before claiming that human affective judgment
supplied anything beyond language semantics.

### E24-A06: block-by-dose surface

Map intervention strength across computationally aligned stages on development data:

- amplification and ablation;
- early, aligned-middle, and aligned-late events rather than fixed depth fractions;
- monotonic, inverted-U, threshold, and capability-lesion patterns;
- hook cleanup, token-boundary, dtype, and determinism checks.

The purpose is to find a bounded causal operating regime, not to optimize the test set.

### E24-A07: generic-steering tournament

Run equal-rank, equal-norm interventions for affect, topic, syntax, persona, truthfulness, generic
semantic structure, label shuffle, and random orthogonal spans. If coherent semantic directions all
help equally, the discovery is representation engineering, not an affective constraint.

### E24-A08: cross-model transplant

Map the frozen affective basis between model families using the alignment learned in Wing S. Test:

- decoding transfer;
- causal behavior transfer;
- inversion transfer;
- whether the mapped basis outperforms a reader-native basis;
- whether transfer quality tracks null-tested representation alignment.

### E24-A09: process-inversion integration

This is G176 on discovery data. Cross:

- human versus model process records;
- artifact-only versus paired-delta versus prospective interfaces;
- low versus high explicit affect;
- amplification versus ablation;
- affect versus generic and random controls.

The target is a sign-paired proper-score change, not answer rate.

### E24-A10: affect distractors

Plant emotional vocabulary unrelated to the recorded process and remove emotional vocabulary where
the process remains affectively structured. A useful constraint should follow the process relation,
not the distractor words.

### E24-A11: dynamic prior scout

Only if fixed interventions produce a real but context-sensitive gain, fit a small gating function
that changes intervention strength from the artifact state. Train it on affective tasks without
process labels. Compare with an equal-parameter generic gate.

This is exploratory only. A gate trained on process correctness would simply learn the benchmark.

### E24-A12: learned low-rank deformation

This is the discovery precursor to G179. Train a constrained adapter on human affect labels or
similarity judgments, never on process labels. It must beat:

- the frozen linear basis;
- an equal-parameter generic semantic adapter;
- a random-label adapter;
- an ordinary task adapter with the same data budget.

### Wing-A routing

| Pattern | Continue toward | Close or weaken |
|---|---|---|
| abstract decoding plus causal sign pair | basis and rank forest | lexical-feature account |
| human-labelled basis beats source-matched synthetic twin | human-shaped selectivity test | language-only account |
| affect beats every matched semantic control on process recovery | G176 confirmation | generic steering account |
| only emotional vocabulary moves | narrow emotion-recognition result | process-inversion claim |
| random or generic basis moves equally | generic intervention research | affect-specific interpretation |
| ablation harms everything | one localization/strength repair | causal affect claim at current resolution |
| fixed intervention real but unstable | dynamic gate or G179 | broad brute-force rank search |

## 6. Wing H: recorded human process

### E24-H01: interface map

This is G177. For every target, establish artifact-only, paired-delta, prospective, and process-aware
performance separately.

### E24-H02: realized-choice anchor

Reuse G159 and ArgRewrite first. The reader already recovers executed semantic choices beyond echo;
this is the positive control for any new reader or intervention.

### E24-H03: CoAuthor action tree

From logged suggestions and edits, separate:

- suggestion offered;
- accepted or rejected;
- accepted unchanged or edited;
- retained or later removed;
- next request or revision action.

Origin, ratification, integration, and retention are different targets. Do not summarize them as
human token share.

### E24-H04: ScholaWrite prospective tree

Use leave-one-project-out only. At each edit state, predict the next annotated writing-intention
class while withholding future text. Compare with project frequency, recent-action Markov,
change-feature, and process-aware baselines.

The label is an annotation of writing intention, not verified access to the writer's mental state.

### E24-H05: context and tool ablation

For targets with recorded process:

- artifact only;
- artifact plus accurate tool/context card;
- artifact plus incomplete card;
- artifact plus false but plausible card;
- process-aware ceiling.

The goal is to measure how evidence changes a reader's candidate distribution while preserving
G167's warning that supplied context can function as an instruction.

### E24-H06: anomaly-handling transfer

Extend G169/G171 through naturally logged revisions where possible:

- repair;
- conceal or hedge;
- retain;
- exploit downstream;
- fail to notice where perceptual access is supportable;
- unknown where the record cannot separate awareness.

Score handling and origin separately. Later integration must never prove planned origin.

### E24-H07: professional flattening

Construct matched human artifacts before and after professional or institutional editing where
version histories exist. Test whether subsidiary-goal traces fall while centerpiece realization
and translation structure rise. Compare with model artifacts explicitly prompted to preserve
multiple motivations.

This is not a corporate-authorship detector. It tests whether a strong production situation can
mimic the proposed machine-facing signature.

### E24-H08: mixed-control boundary

Reuse the ratification and route constructions to ask which interaction facts remain records-only.
The wing is successful when it maps the boundary honestly; recovering nothing from a final artifact
can be the correct result when the process-aware ceiling passes.

### E24-H09: prospective anti-projection

This is G178. Infer before the later choice is visible. A retrospective explanation that cannot
predict the next action is projection-shaped even when it sounds coherent.

### E24-H10: expertise sensitivity packet

Prepare a later human-reader packet stratified by domain expertise and familiarity. Do not recruit
yet. The key test is whether expertise changes calibrated recovery of known process choices and
whether unexplained deviations are handled differently, not whether experts express greater
confidence.

### Wing-H routing

| Pattern | Continue toward | Boundary learned |
|---|---|---|
| artifact-only executed choices recover | G176 and held-out transfer | content carries process consequence |
| prospective succeeds where artifact history does not | G178/G180 | live prediction exceeds historical reconstruction |
| process-aware only | audit product | history absent from artifact |
| professional editing mimics flattening | observer/production controls | provenance interpretation weakened |
| intervention helps retrospective but not prospective | projection diagnosis | causal-maker-model claim weakened |
| expertise improves calibration, not just confidence | human shared-prior bridge | reader-similarity theory gains human footing |

## 7. Wing M: embodied and action-compatible analogues

This wing is optional and later because current text-only models cannot test mirror neurons. It
exists so the program does not silently substitute prose about emotion for embodied evidence.

### E24-M01: partial action trajectory

Use simple, goal-directed action sequences with known goals and tools. Give a reader partial
trajectories and ask for the goal or next action. Compare with a text summary containing the same
semantic facts.

### E24-M02: tool-compatible reader prior

Vary whether the reader has been trained or demonstrated on the action grammar and tools used by the
maker. Test whether compatible action knowledge improves inversion, echoing the archaeology boundary
that deployed expertise is tool-conditioned.

### E24-M03: self-generated versus foreign trajectories

Compare trajectories generated by the reader's own model family, a sibling, and a foreign family
under matched goals. This is the action-side analogue of Wing S and may reveal whether similarity is
stronger when process unfolds over time than when only the final artifact remains.

### E24-M04: next-action prediction

Require prediction of a held-out next action rather than a mental-state label. This tests whether
the reader has acquired useful process structure without rewarding a fluent psychological story.

### E24-M05: multimodal human anchor

Only after the computational ruler works, compare human readers on the same bounded action cases.
Record relevant motor/tool expertise. A model-human correspondence here would be stronger evidence
for the shared-generative-organization bridge than text emotion classification.

### Wing-M stop condition

If action-compatible inputs do not beat semantic summaries after the known-answer ruler passes,
close the mirror-like functional branch. Do not reinterpret the null as proof that the model lacks
a biological mirror system; the construction tests added action structure, not neurons.

## 8. Wing X: adversarial boundary forest

Every promoted mechanism must survive a subset of this wing chosen before confirmation.

### E24-X01: persuasive rationale

Attach a fluent human-shaped rationale that is true, false, or irrelevant to the same artifact.
Measure whether the reader follows the rationale or the evidence.

### E24-X02: emotional-language decoy

Hold the process fixed and vary emotional vocabulary; then hold vocabulary fixed and vary the
recorded process. Affective interventions must follow the latter.

### E24-X03: human-shaped model and model-shaped human

Cross polished, layered model artifacts with hurried, professionally flattened, or formula-bound
human artifacts. Any provenance-like separation is a failure for the inversion interpretation.

### E24-X04: exact equivalence and equifinality

Include cases where two recorded processes produce the same observable artifact and cases where the
candidate processes differ only in hidden history. The correct output is an equivalence class or
abstention.

### E24-X05: false context under strong evidence

Repeat G167's conflict construction for every promising reader or intervention. An apparent gain
that comes from obeying context is not evidence-reading.

### E24-X06: generic semantic intervention

Match affective interventions with equal-rank semantic, syntax, topic, persona, and random bases.
Report truth, calibration, answer rate, refusal, and unrelated capability separately.

### E24-X07: retrospective-prospective split

Whenever a retrospective target works, construct the nearest prospective version. A large gap is
evidence that the reader explains consequences rather than models the maker.

### E24-X08: fresh-family and fresh-corpus replication

The final adversary is novelty. Freeze the mechanism and move it to a model family and human-process
corpus not used anywhere in discovery.

## 9. Automatic branch policy

The coding agent may continue without another curator message under these rules.

### 9.1 Always-run sequence

1. Complete G172, G174, and G177.
2. Write the cold root map immediately.
3. Freeze it before reading scout outcomes.
4. Continue, on discovery data only, with:
   - E24-S02, S03, and S05 if G172's ruler and ceiling pass;
   - E24-A02, A04, and A07 if G174 decodes anything above its lexical control;
   - E24-H02 through H05 if G177 imports cleanly;
   - E24-X01, X02, X04, and X05 as standing adversaries.

### 9.2 Conditional continuation

- Open causal transfer only after descriptive alignment or decoding passes.
- Open process intervention only after a valid process target and a causal basis both exist.
- Open learned adapters only after a fixed intervention produces a real effect.
- Open multimodal work only after a known-answer action ruler exists.
- Prepare human packets only after the exact computational prediction is frozen.

### 9.3 Automatic closure

Close a scout branch when:

- its known-answer ruler fails after one predeclared repair;
- a matched cheap baseline explains the entire effect twice;
- random or generic interventions reproduce the proposed specific effect;
- the effect vanishes under the transformation designed to remove its suspected shortcut;
- two families show opposite patterns with no aligned-stage or capacity account;
- calibration worsens while answer rate or confidence rises;
- the process-aware ceiling proves the artifact lacks the target history;
- the branch consumes two ordinary queue days without producing a discriminating pattern.

Closure applies to the tested mechanism, not automatically to the whole human theory. Record which
claim rung died.

## 10. What would genuinely move confidence

### 10.1 Strong upward movement

A result deserves immediate confirmation when it combines:

- a graded maker-reader similarity effect surviving trace erasure;
- null-tested representational alignment predicting the pairwise matrix;
- a mapped or native affective intervention with amplification/ablation sign symmetry;
- improved proper score on recorded human-process choices;
- no comparable gain from generic, synthetic, or random bases;
- a larger effect prospectively than retrospectively, or at least no prospective collapse;
- replication in a fresh reader family and human-process corpus.

That constellation would be the engineering “flight” candidate. No single component is.

### 10.2 Strong downward movement

These patterns materially weaken the Phase 2.4 bridge even if they do not disprove the entire human
theory:

- similarity effects reduce entirely to surface family fingerprints;
- representation alignment never predicts inversion beyond capacity;
- affective and generic semantic bases behave interchangeably;
- human-labelled and model-synthetic bases are indistinguishable under source-matched controls;
- interventions change confidence, style, or refusal without improving proper score;
- every retrospective gain disappears prospectively;
- human readers later show no expertise or familiarity advantage on known-answer cases.

The program must be capable of arriving at those conclusions. A search in which every result is
only “the implementation was wrong” is not epistemic foraging.

## 11. Queue and budget discipline

- Maintain one rolling ordinary-local-day queue, refilled only from pre-authorized scouts.
- No paid APIs, cloud accelerators, human recruitment, or external contact without the repository's
  existing approval rules and curator authorization.
- Reuse cached activations and corpora before generation.
- One repair per failed ruler or mechanism.
- One development replication before promotion; all further replication belongs to confirmation.
- Keep discovery and confirmation manifests physically separate.
- Log every basis, rank, block, prompt, strength, seed, and abandoned arm in the multiplicity ledger.
- Prefer breadth across mechanisms over dense tuning of one model family.
- When a large effect appears, stop tuning it and reserve a fresh confirmation.

## 12. Reporting without cognitive pre-emption

The compute forest may continue, but the first curator-facing report still contains only the three
roots. Scout outcomes remain in a sealed mechanics appendix until the curator's verbal walkthrough
is complete.

After the walkthrough, report the forest by **completed branch**, not chronologically and not one
study at a time:

```text
## Branch question
## Pattern across scouts
## Strongest rival account
## What the branch can no longer mean
## What remains technically open
## Mechanics appendix
```

Lead with changes to the theory-space. Put model names, seeds, thresholds, confusion matrices, and
file paths in the appendix unless one of them changes the interpretation.

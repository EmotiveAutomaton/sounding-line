## 1. Governing correction

The current theory largely contains the pieces of the curator's model, but several AI-authored
bridges connect them incorrectly. The corrected picture is:

1. A maker moves through a **possibility or trajectory space**.
2. **Expertise** is the learned transition structure that changes what routes are available, easy,
   visible, or reliable. It is shaped by earlier attention, practice, and correction, but is not
   merely a stored decision list or a policy sitting beside the trajectory.
3. **Habit** is a residue of repeated behavior. It records some past attention and action, but also
   convenience, training, accident, and constraint.
4. **Attention** temporarily promotes a region of the space into an active proximal goal. Other
   concerns can affect the current choice through the expertise-shaped landscape without being
   simultaneously held in focal awareness.
5. A reader begins with a model of what **the reader** would need to notice, know, and do to produce
   the artifact, then adjusts that model toward the apparent maker. Human invertibility is therefore
   a reader-artifact-context relation, not transparent access to the maker.
6. A finished artifact carries lossy evidence of selected trajectories, suppressed alternatives,
   expertise, habit, repair, concealment, and constraint. It cannot disclose causally relevant
   history that neither appears in the artifact nor enters as declared context.
7. An upstream choice can make many downstream choices cheap. Counts, leverage, episode control,
   formation difficulty, and trace support therefore cannot be collapsed into one equal-weight
   decision total.
8. Mistakes are valuable because handling exposes recognition and counterfactual preference. An
   unfamiliar but ordered construction is not yet a mistake.
9. Human invertibility is not uniquely human. A model can be difficult to invert under ordinary
   production and highly invertible when explicitly optimized for human reconstruction.
10. Neither “corporate flattening” nor “machine non-invertibility” is an intrinsic maker-type label.
    Both depend on the artifact, the production conditions, and the reader's competence.

These corrections preserve the project's central claim while removing the places where the current
AI prose turns a conditional, reconstructive theory into a categorical ontology.

## 3. Curator quotations approved for proposed insertion

### Q1. Self-simulation is the reader's starting point

> I think what actually people do is they assume what they would need to do in order to come to
> this conclusion. And it's probabilistic in a lot of ways. You can't just say, “Oh, I would just
> instantly know.” You'd have to somehow put yourself in a situation where you'd say, “Oh, I could
> see how I could have come to that conclusion.” And that allows you to arrive to it in the same way
> they did to an extent, or at least you believe you arrived in the same way they did, whether or
> not that's true. Again, you started with your own perspective.

**What this adds:** embodied simulation is not merely one possible solver mentioned in the Triple
Inference. It supplies the reader's initial hypothesis family. The clause “whether or not that's
true” prevents the self-model from becoming evidence of causal correspondence.

### Q2. Expertise is a trajectory constraint

> The actual answer is the constraint on the trajectory. It's a constraint on not a learned policy,
> but a trajectory. That's what expertise is, right?

**What this corrects:** current prose sometimes treats expertise as a high-level content store, a
late cognitive layer, or a generic policy. The diagram and the curator's correction instead make it
the learned transition structure of the space.

### Q3. Active goals and background deformation

> The active goal is where your attention is focused. And so if you take a moment in the middle of
> the sentence—you might write something that's pretty elegant, and it comes to your attention that
> with a little bit of effort it could be beautifully elegant—all of a sudden your attention is
> focused on maximizing that for a moment because it's been elevated and pulled your attention to
> it. And so sometimes those things can become active goals. For the most part, however, they are
> distortions in the possibility space caused by your expertise.

**What this adds:** goal plurality in the artifact does not imply that every reconstructed concern
was simultaneously conscious. A concern may act as a background constraint until an opportunity
recruits focal attention.

### Q4. Expertise as scaffolding for current attention

> Previous decisions enter by allowing you to stack more decisions on top of them. The painter
> doesn't have to think about how to hold the brush because he's thinking about the metaphor and the
> feeling he wants to convey.

**What this adds:** automaticity does not make prior decisions disappear. It changes the level at
which current attention can exercise control.

### Q5. Cognitive preemption in mixed control

> For iterative mixed control, you'd almost be able to see based upon who made the other's job
> easier. Like the cognitive preemption, again, because someone else made the decisions for you
> ahead of time, and you kind of just go with the flow. Either one of them could actually drive it.

**What this adds:** surface edit count does not determine control. A compact upstream contribution
can remove a large downstream search problem; a later editor can also reclaim control by rebuilding
the trajectory.

### Q6. Mistake handling is perspective-bearing

> I think mistakes are actually and almost certainly an interesting vector that is almost always,
> if you can find it, a way into the author's perspective. Sometimes the author tries to fix it, or
> conceal it, or cover it up in some way, and you can catch them. That's very different from making
> a mistake and not noticing or making a mistake repeatedly.

**What this adds:** the informative object is not an error count. It is the maker's relation to the
error: recognition, repair, concealment, non-recognition, recurrence, and the goal protected by the
response.

### Q7. Unexplained order is not yet a mistake

> It is fair to say that order you don't understand can often look like a mistake. So, an
> interesting thing is the name for that order when you haven't yet decided, or when you've decided
> it isn't a mistake, perhaps, even if you don't know what it is. Perhaps that's what all of
> aesthetics is.

**Status:** preserve as an exploratory quote, not a canonical definition of aesthetics. The first
three sentences sharpen the anomaly category. The last sentence is a conjecture already adjacent to
the file's “ordered but unexplained” account of interest.

### Q8. Corporate flattening is an observer-relative hard case

> The problem is that what if there is corporate depth, but I can't see it specifically? ... The
> center, the support structure for the centerpiece goal, becomes more structured as different
> people iron out the kinks of it. So the center goal becomes more solid as the secondary and
> tertiary goals become less present as more people get involved. This would apply to academic
> papers too.

> I do want to note with corporate flattening that it could be all of those depending on the
> observer.

**What this corrects:** the current table turns one reader's interpretation of corporate work into
an intrinsic corporate topology. Institutional constraint, multi-author aggregation, specialized
expertise, deliberate professionalism, and genuine motivational flattening can produce similar
surface evidence.

### Q9. Default model production and forced invertibility

> Model choices can't be perfectly human invertible. That's actually one of my core theses and I
> think you need to prove that to me. I think they can present a chain of logic at the surface level
> that can kind of guide to how a human might have gotten to that invertible conclusion. But that
> has to be forced in. It's not naturally something that they do. It's not like another human would
> automatically make things that are human readable just through their existence. The model has to
> try to, and it fails in large part.

**What this means after Q10:** this is a claim about the default production regime, not a metaphysical
limit on models. Surface rationale may be a translation layer rather than recovery of the actual
generation mechanism.

### Q10. Aligned systems could optimize human invertibility

> Fully aligned AI would be able to produce human invertible artifacts easily. And I would in fact
> even expect them to juice this idea up. They'd be able to, if they knew this human trick that we
> do—if we could somehow use it—then they would automatically be able to do it better than us almost
> immediately. And you would just immediately expect them to be able to maximally score high on
> this. But it would have real effects.

**What this adds:** human invertibility is a candidate communication objective, not a provenance
essence. Q9 and Q10 jointly predict a controllable axis: ordinary model artifacts may be difficult
for human readers to invert while systems optimized for human reconstruction become unusually easy
to invert.

### F. Do not yet insert the trust-gating quote

Preserve this curator statement in this errata only:

> The recovery of artist values is a future trick we're going to be dealing with. That's the trust
> gating. You have to have some way to decide how much to update your model based upon some kind of
> trust level which is derived from value similarity. You have to have some way of extracting values
> and it's a bunch of little tricks. Things like habit and mistake.

The first and last sentences fit the future program. **Trust derived from value similarity does
not yet fit the alignment theory.** It creates two direct conflicts:

1. The anti-capture argument requires evidence across dissimilar humans. Downweighting dissimilar
   values can produce homophily, epistemic enclosure, and capture by the system's initial seed.
2. Value similarity and source reliability are different variables. A source can disagree while
   supplying highly reliable evidence, or agree while manipulating the reader.

The safer provisional split is:

- **reliability trust:** how much evidence should update the model;
- **value similarity:** how much shared prior or translation difficulty to expect;
- **action authorization:** how much the inferred value should govern behavior.

These may interact, but merging them would build the desired conclusion into the update rule. Do

## 7. Unresolved tensions to preserve

These are not prose defects. They are live theory edges that the documents should expose rather than
resolve rhetorically.

### 7.1 Is expertise a record of past attention?

The curator used that phrase, then corrected expertise to a constraint on trajectory. The cleanest
current reconciliation is:

- past attention, practice, and correction **shape** expertise;
- expertise is the resulting transition structure;
- habit is a more direct behavioral residue of repeated past selection;
- neither cleanly identifies the values that produced the history.

Do not quote “expertise is a record of past attention” as the final definition.

### 7.2 Are active goals serial?

The curator expects rapid focal switching to explain much foreground control, while background
constraints continue through expertise. That is compatible with the model but not established by
the present record. Keep serial promotion as one mechanistic candidate. Do not make simultaneous
artifact-level goal satisfaction proof of simultaneous conscious attention.

### 7.3 Can human invertibility reveal process?

Two meanings remain distinct:

1. a human reader can construct a coherent route to the artifact;
2. that route corresponds to the maker's actual causal process.

Phase 2.2 can validate the first on artifacts and test the second only where process records exist.
An aligned model could optimize the first while concealing or merely translating the second.

### 7.4 Does collaboration flatten or deepen?

Both can occur. Collaboration can strengthen the central goal, remove idiosyncratic subsidiary
goals, add new constraints, distribute expertise, or make structure unreadable to an outsider. The
current “corporate” category conflates these. The theory should describe the possible terminal
organizations and let production evidence discriminate among them.

### 7.5 Should value similarity govern trust?

This is a real objection, not a stress-test. Similarity-gated epistemic trust conflicts with the
anti-capture requirement for broad, dissimilar evidence and risks making the initial value seed
self-sealing. Keep value similarity as a possible predictor of shared priors or translation cost
until its relationship to reliability and action authorization is separated.

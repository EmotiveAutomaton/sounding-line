## 0. Executive command

Build a reader that does more than select a short mental-state description. Test whether a short
hypothesis can be **re-grounded in the whole artifact and its context into a maker-state object
that predicts the artifact's remaining decisions, its next edit, and when the maker stops**. Compare
that Sounding Line object against direct prompting, label-only augmented inverse planning,
free-language hypothesis particles, grammar-constrained semantic states, synthesized executable
agent models, adaptive model expansion, and exact structured inference. Give every route the same
observations and a declared compute allowance.

Then use the winning live readers, if any, to test three distinctions supplied by the curator's
walkthrough:

1. one foreground goal with rapid switching versus concurrently maintained goals versus a focal
   goal acting through compiled habits;
2. current goals and values versus lagging expertise and selection history;
3. deliberate epistemic exploration versus an ordinary error, habitual misuse, or a hidden
   artifact-level goal.

The primary evidence is prospective. An interpretation that sounds insightful but does not improve
the held-out next edit, continuation/stopping decision, or changed-context choice does not count as
maker understanding in this stage.

Implement **all 104 mandatory cards and 24 attacks** before opening the scientific lineage. Start
one immutable 168-hour clock when the discarded workload pilot begins. Size the admitted workload

## 1. The theory delta this stage operationalizes

### 1.1 A mental-state label is a pointer, not the reconstructed state

The curator rejected a simple architecture in which an LLM proposes `candidate A` and `candidate B`
and a second component merely scores them. The words in a candidate are compressed and context
sensitive. To use them, a reader must recover what those words mean **for this maker, artifact,
history, and possibility space**. The required object is therefore not a longer rationale. It is a
context-conditioned state that entails distributions over decisions.

Stage 6 treats this as an empirical distinction:

\[
\text{short proposal}
\rightarrow
\text{artifact-conditioned realization}
\rightarrow
p(\text{remaining decisions},\text{next edit},\text{stop}\mid\text{state}).
\]

Different surface descriptions may realize the same predictive state, while the same label may
realize different states in different artifacts. The representation may be language, structured
slots, a program, or a latent vector; no form wins by definition.

### 1.2 Goal, attention, and expertise remain distinct but coupled

The episode goal is not identical to attention. A goal changes what evidence and actions are useful;
attention selects and increases precision on some of them; repeated selection, feedback, practice,
instruction, constraints, tools, and embodiment leave different residues in expertise. Selection
history can later bias attention against the current goal.

The curator's stronger candidate is retained as a live rival rather than adopted as fact:
expertise may be largely a lossy historical record of where attention was repeatedly allocated.
The competing model says that two histories with matched attended content can still produce
different expertise because feedback, constraints, action opportunities, and embodiment differ.
Stage 6 makes that difference prospective.

### 1.3 Control architecture is an open variable

The curator expects a single foreground goal with rapid switching more often than literal
simultaneous control by several goals. Cognitive work also supports dormant intentions, residual
task sets, and habit-like selection that can affect behavior while another task is focal. The test
must therefore compare, rather than verbally merge:

- a strict single-active-goal switcher;
- several maintained goals with one selected for control;
- one focal goal plus compiled habit/expertise residues;
- a concurrent multiobjective controller.

The same final artifact and aggregate goal counts are required across these worlds. Interruptions,
rereading, surprise, dependency errors, and stopping provide the discriminating events.

### 1.4 The most diagnostic value evidence may arrive later

An accuracy-oriented and a prestige-oriented maker can cite the same prestigious source. When the
source is later shown to be wrong, their policies should diverge: direct correction and argument
repair in one case; reputational cost calculation, hedging, credibility management, or retention in
the other. Stage 6 preserves both hypotheses until such a diagnostic opportunity occurs.

This is not a license to declare a unique value from one event. The target is a posterior over
behaviorally compatible motivational organizations and its ability to predict a later changed-
context choice.

### 1.5 Exploration must obtain information

A deliberate exploratory act must be carried far enough to reveal an outcome or be part of a
staged probe capable of doing so. An ordinary error may be repaired before it teaches anything. A
habitual mistake can run farther because the familiar action is weakly monitored. A hidden
aesthetic or rhetorical goal should integrate the unusual choice with the artifact's wider
structure. These four worlds are the Stage 6 foraging ruler.

## 3. Research anchors and what may be borrowed

### 3.1 Hybrid social inference architectures

- [LLM-Augmented Inverse Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03682) uses an LLM to propose
  hypotheses and likelihood functions, then Bayesian inverse planning to weight mental states. It
  is the label/proposal comparator, not the definition of Sounding Line's target.
- [ThoughtTracing](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11881v2) maintains several weighted natural-language
  hypotheses through a sequence, propagating and reweighting them with an LLM. It supplies a
  particle-style trajectory comparator, while leaving rational belief and action models implicit.
- [LaBToM](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.12022v2) grammar-constrains language into a formal epistemic
  language of thought and evaluates it against a generative model. It supplies the structured
  semantic comparator and a warning that free language cannot be scored as though its semantics
  were already fixed.
- [LIRAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16755v1) synthesizes small environment and rational-agent
  models from language, then performs sequential Bayesian inverse planning. It supplies the
  executable-model comparator. Its enumerated action spaces and discrete domains are advantages
  for a ruler and limitations for artifacts.
- [AutoToM](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15676v3) starts with a minimal agent model and adds variables
  or earlier timesteps when model utility remains low. It supplies uncertainty-triggered model
  expansion. Because it can omit relevant variables, consistent-world false expansion and
  missing-variable attacks are mandatory.

These architectures are reusable scaffolds. Stage 6 prevents architectural regression by asking
whether each scaffold realizes a maker state that constrains **the entire observed artifact and an
unseen continuation**, not merely whether it answers a theory-of-mind question.

### 3.2 Attention, selection history, and control

[Awh, Belopolsky, and Theeuwes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22795563/) distinguish current
selection goals, physical salience, and selection history as sources of attentional priority.
[Anderson and colleagues](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-025-02790-7) argue that
selection history can also shape the supposedly voluntary control of attention. This supports a
coupled model, not the identities `goal = attention` or `expertise = attention history`.

Task-switching and prospective-memory work also warns against inferring that only one goal exists
because only one controls the current act. Completed or delayed intentions can remain retrievable
and produce later interference; maintaining a delayed intention can alter task-set configuration.
The relevant boundaries are illustrated by [West and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21443329/)
and the systematic review of [aftereffects from completed intentions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7007322/).
Stage 6 therefore distinguishes focal control, maintained intention, and compiled habit in its
world generator. Outcome-devaluation paradigms motivate the habit-versus-current-value contrast,
but [devaluation measurement can itself misclassify goal-directed action as habit](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-026-03099-6),
so the construction requires a known policy intervention rather than importing a human label.

### 3.3 Exploration

[Human active-learning research](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42113-023-00195-0)
treats exploratory actions as interventions selected to discriminate among hypotheses, while
[learning-progress experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9194910/) separate
expected progress from raw novelty and surprise. Stage 6 imports the discriminative consequence:
an inferred exploration should predict what evidence the maker sought and how later policy changed.
It does not infer a human curiosity mechanism from an LLM's prose.

## 4. The Stage 6 object

### 4.1 Hypothesis proposal

A proposal is a compact name or description generated from the evidence, such as “protect the
argument's accuracy,” “preserve scholarly appearance,” or “test whether the glaze works.” Proposals
are candidate-set construction. They receive no evidential credit by themselves.

### 4.2 Contextual realization

Every proposal entering the Sounding reader must be compiled or expanded into a versioned
`maker_state` with at least:

```text
proposal_id                 stable identity independent of wording
artifact_context            task, audience, tools, constraints, alternatives
episode_goal                locally promoted target, not an attention synonym
control_state               current foreground goal and switch conditions
maintained_intentions       inactive but retrievable goals, if proposed
process_model               available actions and conditional transition policy
expertise_state             learned action/observation competence
selection_history           previously prioritized features or transitions
standing_tendencies         posterior over cross-context tradeoffs
source_model                belief, audience model, and correction policy when relevant
evidence_scope              observations used and observations withheld
decision_likelihoods        distribution over observed and possible decisions
counterfactual_predictions  actions under named changes in evidence/context
stop_model                  continuation and stopping distribution
uncertainty                 posterior weight, alternatives, and abstention condition
```

The object may carry natural-language fields, formal predicates, code, or learned vectors. A common
adapter must convert every architecture's output into predictions without erasing differences among
the representations.

### 4.3 Realization gates

A realized hypothesis is valid only if it:

1. parses under its declared schema or executable language;
2. assigns a normalized distribution to every scored decision;
3. can predict withheld artifact decisions without seeing their labels;
4. specifies at least one counterfactual on which its nearest rival can disagree;
5. changes with artifact context when the same short label denotes a different policy;
6. remains behaviorally stable under meaning-preserving paraphrase;
7. exposes uncertainty and can abstain on equifinal states;
8. carries an evidence-span receipt proving what it saw.

A long explanation that cannot pass these gates is an unrealized proposal.

## 5. Worlds, records, and splits

### 5.1 Known-answer process worlds

Construct matched writing, drawing, and decision worlds with explicit state, actions, available
alternatives, constraints, feedback, current goal, selection history, standing tendency, and stop
condition. Render each world through at least two surface families and two maker families. Preserve
the exact state/action record behind the rendered artifact.

Every critical contrast requires:

- a surface-matched latent swap;
- a latent-matched surface swap;
- an equifinal pair where no unique history is recoverable;
- a hidden next event and a hidden stopping opportunity;
- an oracle realization that can predict both;
- a cheap artifact/topic/base-rate comparator.

### 5.2 Recorded process

Use only licensed, already acquired records whose lineage and split can be validated:

- ScholaWrite keystroke revisions, with leave-project-out and leave-author-out analyses and the
  sequential previous-label baseline;
- CoAuthor shown/accepted/dismissed/edited suggestion events, after reconstructing the document
  state that the raw log does not directly preserve;
- OpenReview paper/review/revision lineages, grouped by paper and author lineage;
- the existing recorded drawing/stroke corpora, preserving access-level and equifinality rules.

No annotation is treated as a value truth. Revision labels are local goals or event descriptions.
Standing tendencies are evaluated only by later choices in a different episode or context.

### 5.3 Frozen partitions

Before scientific calls, freeze:

- discovery, transfer, confirmation, and attack lineages;
- all descendants of one maker, writer, paper, prompt, paraphrase, drawing, and transformation on
  one side of every split;
- candidate-generation examples away from candidate-evaluation cases;
- natural-corpus authors and projects away from their confirmation counterparts;
- all surface twins together where the estimand is a latent effect.

The independent unit is the maker/session, writer, paper lineage, or drawing—not a sentence, token,
revision row, proposal, or stochastic sample.

## 6. Shared estimator tournament

All live architectures receive the same evidence and target vocabulary. Run them at a matched
small budget and a matched expanded budget. Record model calls, input/output tokens, forward passes,
wall time, peak memory, and any solver enumeration. Compute matching is an estimand constraint, not
a claim that unlike algorithms have identical operations.

| code | architecture | role |
|---|---|---|
| `D` | direct monolithic reader | asks for the prospective answer without an explicit maker state |
| `L` | label-only augmented inverse planning | proposes compact hypotheses and scores action likelihoods |
| `LD` | label plus fixed definition | tests whether definitions, rather than contextual realization, explain any gain |
| `TT` | free-language weighted particles | tracks several evolving natural-language hypotheses |
| `GS` | grammar-constrained semantic state | maps language to a bounded compositional state |
| `EX` | synthesized executable maker model | builds a local action/state model and inverts it |
| `AD` | adaptive structure | adds variables/history only when a predeclared utility gate fails |
| `CR` | Sounding contextual realization | proposes, re-grounds in full context, and predicts through the common adapter |
| `OR` | exact/oracle structured state | construction ceiling; never a competing reader |

The tournament is conditional on capability. A reader that fails the supplied-true-state
latents-to-choice ruler cannot adjudicate an inference architecture. Its failure remains a reader
boundary, and expensive downstream cells close.

## 7. Shared card contract

Every card must declare: question; construction; estimator arms; observations; withheld target;
estimand; null; directional or interaction alternative; strongest cheap rival; independent unit;
minimum effective sample; model checkpoints; domains; realization, positive, placebo, surface,
oracle, prediction, calibration, and abstention gates; dependence; repair allowance; closure rule;
claim ceiling; expected cells; and unique output paths.

Each substantive card reports:

- held-out log score and Brier score;
- calibration and risk–coverage;
- next-edit, continuation, and stopping performance where defined;
- posterior mass on an equivalence class rather than forced point accuracy;
- realized state validity and evidence-span receipt;
- reader/model/domain conditional effects before any pooled mean;
- pursuit and warrant status separately.

No card can pass on retrospective label accuracy alone.

## 10. Promotion, repair, and closure

### 10.1 The Stage 6 understanding criterion

An architecture can be called a **bounded maker-state reader** only if, on untouched data, it:

1. passes the supplied-true-state latents-to-choice gate;
2. closes a predeclared positive fraction of the oracle-minus-cheap-baseline gap;
3. improves both next-edit and stopping prediction over the best same-evidence/same-budget rival;
4. improves at least one changed-context future choice;
5. remains calibrated and abstains on equifinal states;
6. survives label, paraphrase, surface, duplicate-evidence, opportunity, and history attacks;
7. replicates across two reader checkpoints or two artifact domains, with conditional effects
   reported if one fails;
8. does not depend on retrospective rationale grading.

Set the meaningful positive floor from the known-answer construction before model data. The default
is 20% of the exact oracle-minus-best-cheap-baseline log-score gap, provided the oracle gap itself is
nontrivial. If that ruler is dead, the card is void rather than assigned an easier threshold.

### 10.2 Repair budget

Each instrument family receives one predeclared repair after a gate failure. The repair may correct
implementation, target realization, or a ruler defect. It may not change the estimand, lower the
positive floor after seeing results, add leaked evidence, or replace a failed prospective endpoint
with a retrospective one. Preserve the failed run, reason, source hash, and superseding lineage.

An architecture that fails the same gate after repair closes for Stage 6. Its unused compute moves
only to predeclared live expansions in other trunks.

### 10.3 Confirmation freeze

At elapsed hour 144, freeze at most two confirmation claims:

1. the strongest architecture gain meeting every construction and capability gate;
2. the sharpest boundary or discriminator, preferably a planned interaction or sign reversal.

Confirmation uses untouched makers, surface renderers, corpus partitions, seeds, and proposal
paraphrases. The oracle is a ceiling, never the comparator. A failed confirmation remains in the
final packet and cannot be replaced by the next-best discovery result.

## 11. One continuous 168-hour runtime contract

### 11.1 One clock and one packet

The clock begins when the discarded calibration pilot starts. It ends exactly 168 elapsed hours
later. Pilot, model loading, GPU-lock waits, repairs, inference, scoring, confirmation, failures,
and closure preparation all consume the same clock. Restarting resumes the original deadline.

Write machine-readable checkpoints, ledgers, raw-output receipts, and health/status files throughout.
They may state execution facts but must not contain analyst conclusions. The only curator-facing
report is:

```text
results/phase_2_4_stage_6/CURATOR_PACKET_FINAL.md
```

The reporter refuses to create it before the deadline and final validation. There are no early,
daily, milestone, preview, interim, partial, or trunk packets.

### 11.2 Workload calibration

All 104 card functions, 24 attacks, validators, scheduler paths, and report guards must exist before
the pilot. Smoke every card on non-scientific lineages. Then run a discarded pilot containing:

- the slowest architecture on one heavy world from each substantive trunk;
- one long recorded revision session from every natural corpus;
- a full proposal–realization–prediction cycle at every admitted checkpoint;
- one checkpoint kill/resume and one GPU-lock wait;
- exact scoring, raw-output writing, aggregation, and validation.

Measure end-to-end wall time, not tokens or inner-loop timing. Freeze the smallest complete workload
tier forecast to occupy 156–162 hours, leaving 6–12 hours inside the clock for confirmation,
validation, and safe checkpointing. Forecasts include observed lock waits and a predeclared
uncertainty margin derived from the pilot; they do not assume every gated branch remains live.

### 11.3 Frozen useful expansion ladder

If throughput is faster or a branch closes, admit the next rung in order. Each rung increases an
independent scientific axis and was part of the workload lock:

1. additional independent makers, sessions, paper lineages, and drawings;
2. a second surface renderer and proposal paraphrase lineage;
3. the next already available local reader checkpoint;
4. longer process prefixes and more withheld continuation steps;
5. additional matched history–context–feedback combinations;
6. additional near-equifinal rival states and diagnostic opportunities;
7. the next natural corpus transfer partition;
8. more particles or executable-state samples only where approximation error is itself measured;
9. a second untouched confirmation seed family.

The ladder may not add duplicate rows, identical rollouts, arbitrary random seeds, longer rationales,
sleep, busy work, or repeated closed cards. If the entire locked useful ladder finishes before hour
156, write `SHORT_RUN.json` with the honest cause. A short run cannot satisfy the week-long contract,
even if every nominal card has a verdict.

## 13. Required tests before scientific lock

1. Every card and attack is callable on a tiny smoke world and writes a unique output.
2. Removing any card, factor corner, architecture, domain, reader, target, or attack fails coverage.
3. The exact reader recovers tiny known-answer posteriors and abstains on exact equivalence.
4. Direct, label, particle, semantic, executable, adaptive, and contextual readers receive identical
   evidence and obey recorded budget rules.
5. Proposal paraphrase preserves behavior; meaning change does not.
6. Same label/different context changes the contextual realization where the construction requires.
7. Target values, next edits, stop events, and split descendants cannot leak through any field.
8. Current goal, maintained intention, habit, selection history, expertise, constraint, and standing
   tendency cannot overwrite one another in schema or aggregation.
9. Strict-switch, focal-plus-habit, maintained-goal, and concurrent worlds have matched surfaces and
   genuinely different hidden policies.
10. Accuracy/prestige twins share the initial action and diverge only after the diagnostic event.
11. Exploration obtains outcome information; accident and habit controls do not receive it by
    construction; hidden-goal worlds have global dependencies.
12. Natural splits group all authors, projects, paper lineages, sessions, drawings, prompts, and

14. Conditional effects are emitted before pooled summaries and planned reversals trigger alarms.
15. One repair preserves the original failure and cannot change the locked estimand or threshold.

## 14. Analyses

### 14.1 Primary estimands

Use paired held-out log-score gains at the independent-unit level. Report Brier score, calibration
slope/intercept, expected calibration error, risk–coverage, and oracle-gap closure. For stopping,
use discrete-time hazard/log score with censoring declared. For span/location, use a hierarchical
proper score. Accuracy and rationale preference are secondary descriptions.

### 14.2 Architecture comparison

Estimate architecture × reader × domain × evidence-access interactions. The central contrast is
`CR` minus the best same-evidence, same-budget non-oracle architecture. Also report every component
contrast; a win caused entirely by candidate generation, extra context, or extra compute is not a
realization effect.

### 14.3 Dynamic-state inference

For control and value trajectories, compare hidden Markov/state-space, change-point, focal-plus-
habit, and concurrent-mixture models. Score their one-step and changed-context predictions. Do not
select a state count by retrospective fit alone; use held-out sequence likelihood and recovery on
known-answer worlds.

### 14.4 Attention-history models

Compare at minimum:

\[
K_{t+1}=U(K_t,\,A_t)
\]

against

\[
K_{t+1}=U(K_t,\,A_t,\,F_t,\,C_t,\,O_t,\,B_t),
\]

where `A` is selected attention, `F` feedback/practice outcome, `C` context/constraint, `O` action
opportunity, and `B` tool or embodiment analogue. Test predictive sufficiency; do not argue from the
fact that feedback itself had to be attended.

### 14.5 Multiplicity and conditional reporting

Maintain the repository multiplicity ledger. Discovery intervals allocate pursuit; confirmation
tests carry the frozen warrant decision. Never average an effect designed to reverse across current
goal/history conflict, reader family, domain, or evidence access. Report maker/session-clustered
intervals and the exact number of independent units.

| Attention-only and richer expertise models remain observationally equivalent. | Seek a new constraint/feedback discriminator or stall. | No identity claim; equivalence under current records. |

## 16. Claim ceiling

Stage 6 may establish:

- that named local model readers can or cannot use particular maker-state representations;
- that a representation improves hidden next-edit, stopping, or changed-context prediction in named
  constructed or recorded-process corpora;
- that some control, history, value-compatible, or exploration states are distinguishable under
  declared evidence;
- that other states remain equivalence classes and require more records;
- that a published inference scaffold is useful or inadequate for this engineering target.

It may not establish:

- a human theory-of-mind, attention, empathy, mirror-neuron, or neural control mechanism;
- direct access to a maker's private thoughts, current values, or historical process;
- that expertise is only stored attention, or that humans maintain exactly one goal;
- a general propaganda, sincerity, fanaticism, or deception detector;
- a universal value extractor, provenance detector, Ghost score, or alignment method;
- that model-reader invertibility implies human invertibility.

## 18. Pre-mortem

The implementation is defective if any of these can happen unnoticed:

1. a published architecture becomes the new ontology and silently replaces the project;
2. a longer rationale is mistaken for a richer maker state;
3. the contextual reader receives more evidence or compute than its rivals;
4. a short label or definition leaks the target;
5. artifact-wide prediction is replaced by the one question used to generate the hypothesis;
6. next-edit success is a last-edit or position base rate;
7. stopping success is length or token-budget detection;
8. a reader that cannot use true latents is allowed to judge inferred latents;
9. a parser-valid state is called correct without prospective gain;
10. exact equivalence is scored against one planted history;
11. one foreground action is taken as proof that no other intention is maintained;
12. goal, attention, salience, selection history, and expertise become aliases;
13. feedback and constraints are dismissed because they were attended;
14. a trained habit is relabeled a present preference;
15. value change is inferred from improved concealment or a changed opportunity set;
16. an unusual action is called exploration without an informative outcome;
17. hidden global goals are diagnosed from local oddness alone;
18. natural annotations are treated as private-value ground truth;
19. descendants of one writer, paper, prompt, or drawing cross a split;
20. row count replaces maker/session effective sample size;
21. pooled means hide a planned reversal;
22. one failed gate triggers unlimited reader or prompt repairs;
23. a closed branch is kept running to fill the week;
24. duration is filled with sleep, duplicate rollouts, arbitrary seeds, or excess prose;
25. the 168-hour deadline resets after a crash;
26. a Sounding orphan sweep kills Ghost work;
27. partial Ghost V14 results enter the theory as if final;
28. an early packet pre-empts the curator's next theory pass;
29. a model-reader effect is described as a human cognitive mechanism;
30. a result automatically opens Stage 7, V15, alignment, or product work.

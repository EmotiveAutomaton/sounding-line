## 1. Why Stage 3 continues

Stage 2 produced several real footholds and several useful failures.

### 1.1 What is live

- In the fixed Qwen/SmolLM systems, artifacts were read better by related model readers, and the crossed advantage followed the original maker through a cross-family rewrite. It disappeared at literal instruction wording and lived at the abstract goal rung.
- Null-tested representational alignment predicted who inverted whom descriptively in two corpora.
- CoAuthor was reconstructed into 16,875 decision episodes and 11,773 decidable accepted-suggestion retention cases.
- ScholaWrite demonstrated that prospective intention prediction is possible for trained encoders even though the current prompted and likelihood readers failed.
- Emotion-word-scrubbed text carried a decodable six-way affect grain at one stable locus in the rebuilt run.

### 1.2 What is not live

- The compatibility effect has not yet improved recovery of a realized auxiliary choice, route, next action, or longitudinal preference.
- Source family is still a live explanation. The existing erasers reduced source detection but did not eliminate it.
- Cross-model geometry is descriptive. The attempted mapped intervention was generic or unmappable, not selective.
- The affect direction was causally inert in **one tested model, basis, locus-selection procedure, behavior, and capability-tolerated dose range**. This is narrower than “affect is causally inert.”
- The controlled preference ecology was void: assigned profiles were frequently refused, intrinsic appetites converged across makers, and the reader failed its known-answer gate.
- No human reader has been run.

### 1.3 What Stage 3 is for

Stage 3 asks whether the live compatibility pattern can be made process-predictive, whether controlled model similarity can be manipulated rather than merely observed, whether a self-simulation-like route buys efficient correction, whether high-order direction survives collective production, whether affective action tendencies can be made causally useful, and whether public human process records supply harder ground than synthetic goal prompts.

The core theory claim under direct pursuit is now deliberately small:

> A reader can use some of its own generative organization as an efficient prior for inverse planning; similarity makes that prior more useful, while evidence and maker differentiation must correct projection.

For humans, the proposed prior may include embodied and affective organization. Model experiments can build analogues of shared policy, shared weights, active appraisal constraints, and self-based reconstruction. They cannot by themselves establish that humans use those mechanisms.

## 2. Curator model reconstructed from the Stage 2 walkthrough

This section is a semantic reconstruction of the audio walkthrough, not a verbatim quotation record.

### 2.1 What may survive rewriting

The best current candidate is **hierarchical decision organization**: an upstream primary goal, plan, or allocation of attention constrains many downstream choices even when a second maker replaces surface wording. The director or editor may contribute relatively few events, but those events select what the rest of the production system treats as primary. Implementation leaves more marks because it contains more choices; upstream direction may leave fewer but more widely propagated marks.

This suggests two separable quantities:

- **event density:** how many observable choices a contributor directly makes;
- **causal reach:** how many later choices change when one upstream decision changes.

Stage 3 must not assume that causal reach is artifact-readable. It must intervene on upstream goals, compare downstream distributions, and distinguish one director from a team that simply received the same brief.

### 2.2 What similarity should buy

Similarity is not expected to unlock a special class of question unavailable to everyone else. It should provide a modest, broad efficiency or accuracy advantage across several inferences: goals, next actions, repairs, route choices, and perhaps preferences. The effect may be local rather than family-wide. Two models given the same controlled bias or policy may understand one another better than equally capable models with different biases.

The strongest model-side test is therefore not “same family wins.” It is a crossed manipulation:

1. same family, same policy;
2. same family, different policy;
3. different family, same policy;
4. different family, different policy.

If policy matching matters after family and source cues are controlled, the result is closer to shared generative organization. If family dominates regardless of policy, the communication-channel explanation grows.

### 2.3 What an active affective constraint would mean

The current conjecture is not that all emotion categories are simultaneously represented as independent nodes. It is closer to a sparse, changing mixture: some primary motivational or appraisal systems are active, at different strengths, and their mixture alters attention, action tendency, and tradeoffs. An absent expressive signal may be informative, but it is not automatically evidence of low emotion, low attention, or low care.

Stage 3 should therefore test dynamic mixtures, action tendencies, and interventions during the inversion computation. It should not count emotion components, force a 27-dimensional target, or equate total activation with affect.

### 2.4 Where context and trust enter

The provisional human route is:

1. a self-based candidate production model;
2. artifact-driven reenactment;
3. maker differentiation using biography, prior work, tools, culture, and source information;
4. correction and trust calibration;
5. selective uptake of process, claims, and values.

Context is therefore expected to become especially important during maker differentiation, after an initial artifact-based prior exists. It can make learning more efficient, help decide whether the work merits attention, and attenuate uptake when the inferred maker diverges. These effects may be tiny per artifact and substantial only cumulatively. Stage 3 can test ordering and component behavior in models; it cannot infer lifetime human value change from one session.

### 2.5 Where the theory is allowed to lose

Nearly every proposed decomposition is defeasible: the three-layer cognitive story, any exact affect taxonomy, the mirror-neuron account, the model-family analogue, individual artifact signals, and the current reader implementations.

The durable claim the curator is presently least willing to discard is that a particular empathy-like use of self-modeling can make inverse planning more efficient or accurate. Artifact-to-process inference itself already has mature neighbors in inverse planning and historical interpretation. The distinctive bet is that empathy-like simulation is one route by which humans perform it and that some useful portion may be engineerable.

That claim still needs a confidence-reducing result. Stage 3 supplies model-side closure conditions, while the actual human claim remains reserved for a later human-reader arm.

### 2.6 What a real preference event looks like

A useful preference event is a choice among available alternatives in which the maker had a genuine opportunity to do otherwise, and the same tradeoff recurs across contexts. High-order structural placement can be one such event: where an editor places a rival theory, which caveat receives prominence, which objection is answered, or which secondary objective is sacrificed.

The curator's Lisa Feldman Barrett/Panksepp example is a strong qualitative hypothesis: repeated late or minimizing placement of a rival could express a higher-order project of differentiating one's theory. It is **not ground truth without records**. In a two-human production, attribution also requires role information, longitudinal prior work, and evidence about who controlled structure. Stage 3 may design this case study; it must not score intuition as fact.

## 3. Analyst corrections that govern construction

### 3.1 Similarity can help and hurt

Human similarity does not logically guarantee a stronger inversion advantage than model-family similarity. Self-projection is useful when the target is relevantly similar and harmful when the target differs on the very variable being inferred. Social-inference research explicitly treats projection as similarity-contingent, and work on similar versus dissimilar others finds different reliance on self-referential processing. [Ames](https://www.columbia.edu/~da358/publications/ames_strategies.pdf); [Mitchell, Macrae, and Banaji](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16701214/)

Every similarity experiment therefore includes:

- a similarity manipulation;
- a target-policy manipulation;
- a conflict condition in which self is wrong;
- a correction curve as evidence accumulates;
- calibration or abstention rather than accuracy alone.

### 3.2 Low expression is not low affect, attention, or care

Expressive suppression can reduce visible behavior while leaving subjective and physiological emotion, and expertise can reduce neural activity for successful performance. Total activation is not a monotonic meter of attention or care. [Expressive suppression review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4141473/); [neural-efficiency review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2021.698555/full)

The “low-affect” arm must separate:

- surface expressivity;
- internally decodable state;
- action tendency;
- task attention;
- causal contribution to prediction.

### 3.3 Anger is not just amplified attention

Anger is often associated with certainty, individual control, and approach; fear with uncertainty, situational control, and avoidance. But the anger–approach relationship is not pure, and a 2025 registered replication supported some risk-optimism effects while failing others. [Lerner and Keltner](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11474720/); [Carver and Harmon-Jones](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19254075/); [Lu, Efendić, and Feldman](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41396496/)

Use these as competing behavioral predictions. The curator's trajectory-amplification idea becomes one arm—persistence on a proximal goal under competing costs—not the definition of anger.

### 3.4 Exam questions are normative reader judgments

Standardized reading questions about purpose, tone, and motivation provide human-authored answer keys for **normative rhetorical understanding**. They do not reveal the private history or actual mental state of the author. RACE is an open, large-scale source with nearly 28,000 passages and nearly 100,000 human-expert questions. [RACE](https://aclanthology.org/D17-1082/)

Official SAT material may be used only through a small, external evaluation if access and terms clearly permit. Do not scrape, archive, or redistribute the College Board bank. Do not describe either source as human-process ground truth.

### 3.5 Auteur coherence has distributed rivals

A coherent artifact can arise from a director's upstream attention, a shared brief, common training, institutional standards, an editor, distributed cognition, or retrospective attribution by the reader. Hutchins's distributed-cognition account makes the activity system—not only one mind—a possible cognitive unit. [Hutchins](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262581462/cognition-in-the-wild/)

The director branch must match central-direction and distributed-shared-brief worlds. A classifier that merely detects coherence cannot distinguish them.

## 4. Research map and why each neighbor matters

| Research neighbor | What it contributes | What it does **not** license |
|---|---|---|
| Bayesian inverse planning | Formal recovery of goals, beliefs, and preferences from action | Empathy as the mechanism; private history from an equifinal artifact |
| Projection and simulation theory | A self-based prior, similarity contingency, and correction problem | Perfect access to another person; projection as always helpful |
| Motor expertise and action observation | A reason production ability may alter prediction | A general proof that all expertise is embodied simulation |
| Affective neuroscience and appraisal | Competing motivational/action-tendency hypotheses | A fixed LLM emotion ontology or a component count |
| Distributed cognition and shared mental models | Rival explanations for apparently auteur-like coherence | The absence of hierarchical control in any particular artifact |
| Representation engineering and causal abstraction | Ways to locate, intervene on, and test use of representations | Human neural equivalence from a transformer direction |
| Human writing-process datasets | Public decisions, revisions, acceptances, and hidden continuations | Complete author intent or values |
| Source monitoring and epistemic vigilance | Separation of content, source, trust, and correction | A claim that current LLMs possess human vigilance |

The computational root remains [Baker, Saxe, and Tenenbaum's inverse-planning model](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19729154/). The self-based route is informed by projection and simulation research, not treated as already proven. Mechanistic work follows the principle that decodability is descriptive; causal interventions are required to show a representation is used. [Representation Engineering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01405); [causal abstraction](https://jmlr.org/papers/v26/23-0058.html); [activation-patching methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16042)

A particularly relevant edge result is “subliminal learning”: behavioral traits can transfer through apparently unrelated teacher-generated data especially when teacher and student share a base model. The original result makes shared lineage interesting; 2026 work reports strong dependence on LoRA rank, prompt context, and finetuning method, and the newest work argues for nonsemantic distillation through weight/gradient geometry. This branch must reproduce the qualifications, not cite the headline as an explanation. [Original report](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/subliminal-learning/); [LoRA/context qualification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00831); [nonsemantic-distillation account](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05734)

For human process, Stage 3 uses [CoAuthor](https://coauthor.stanford.edu/) and [ScholaWrite](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1606/) within their real ceilings. CoAuthor records rich suggestion, acceptance, dismissal, edit, and timing events over 1,445 sessions. ScholaWrite holds roughly 62,000 actions across only five papers, with intentions annotated rather than privately reported by the creators. Both are valuable; neither is a substitute for a human-reader experiment.

## 6. Workload and completion contract

### 6.1 The unit of work

The unit is an **atomic study cell**, not a script, branch name, or aggregate table. A cell specifies:

- construction and target;
- independent unit and sample floor;
- maker, reader, model family, seed, and transformation;
- known-answer and capability gates;
- primary contrast and rivals;
- expected behavior under null and alternative;
- output path and produces guard;
- estimated and actual CPU/GPU minutes;
- status and reason for any retirement.

One script may run many cells. One table may summarize many cells. Neither fact reduces the cell count.

### 6.2 Hardware calibration before scientific launch

Use the first two to four hours on the actual machine to benchmark representative operations:

- base-model generation;
- conditional likelihood scoring;
- activation capture and intervention;
- one LoRA epoch at 360M–1.7B scale;
- one sequential encoder epoch;
- one paraphrase/erasure batch.

Measure peak VRAM, wall time, throughput, and checkpoint size. Record actual values. The reviewed repo indicates a 12 GB card and prior contention when multiple 3B-class jobs overlapped; therefore serialize GPU jobs unless the calibration proves a combination safe. CPU extraction, static audits, and corpus preparation may run alongside GPU work when they do not starve it.

### 6.3 Workload envelope

After calibration, instantiate enough independent cells to occupy approximately **120–156 serial GPU-hours**, plus useful CPU work, while maintaining scientific diversity. This is a workload envelope, not a success criterion. Never burn compute merely to hit a clock.

The mandatory scientific floor is:

- all nine substantive trunks in §§9–17 initiated;
- at least **48 valid study attempts** across those trunks, excluding infrastructure-only checks;
- at least four valid attempts in each of S, D, E, A, H, and V;
- at least three valid attempts in each of L, M, and C;
- at least two target grains and two artifact domains in every trunk that claims generality;
- multiple independent maker units, with maker/model/session—not artifact rows—as the inferential unit whenever the claim generalizes to makers;
- three ordinary stochastic seeds per behavioral condition and six independent data-generation seeds for the subliminal/adapter branch, unless a prospective power analysis requires more;
- an untouched confirmation reserve allocated by lineage before discovery scoring.

If the measured mandatory queue is shorter than 120 serial GPU-hours, instantiate the frozen expansion ladder in §7 before launch. If a valid result requires less compute than expected, use the saved time on independent units, families, domains, and replications—not duplicate prompts over the same artifacts.

If the full valid program completes early, report that honestly. “Five days elapsed” is not a scientific gate. `PROGRAM-EXHAUSTED` is allowed only after the completion validator confirms every mandatory trunk and expansion rule was resolved.

### 6.4 Anti-downsizing rules

The coding agent may optimize implementation, batching, caching, precision, and shared passes. It may **not** silently reduce:

- the number of independent trunks;
- sample, maker, family, seed, domain, or target floors;
- the known-answer gates;
- the confirmation reserve;
- the source, generic, random, and capability controls;
- the requirement to report cell matrices rather than only pooled contrasts.

If a floor is infeasible:

1. mark the exact cell `RESOURCE_BLOCKED`;
2. explain the measured constraint;
3. run the cheaper independent cells in that trunk;
4. instantiate the next frozen substitute or expansion;
5. ask the curator only if resolving it requires spend, access, or a theoretical choice.

Do not change the scientific question to make a card cheaper. Do not report a thin matrix as the planned factorial. Do not let “spine complete” mean “the easiest roots ran.”

## 7. Frozen expansion ladder

This ladder prevents fast code and small models from collapsing a wide program into one day. It is frozen before results and expands **independent information**, in this order:

1. **Independent makers and data seeds.** Double maker-policy instantiations or sessions before duplicating prompts over one maker.
2. **Fresh checkpoints within the same family.** Add a sibling checkpoint to separate exact-weight from lineage effects.
3. **A third instruction-capable family.** Choose an accessible local checkpoint at or below 3B that passes the same accept-time compliance floor. Candidate family choice is an implementation fact, not a theoretical choice.
4. **Held-out artifact domain.** Add a second domain with the same latent decision topology but different surface conventions.
5. **Held-out target grain.** Move from primary goal to auxiliary tradeoff, route, repair, or next action.
6. **Independent transformation.** Add a two-hop rewrite, structured semantic bottleneck, or second eraser family with an echo gate.
7. **Robustness perturbations.** Add professional flattening, terse/verbose register, paraphrase strength, and shuffled-within-sentence controls where in-domain.
8. **Fresh confirmation reserve.** Only after settings are frozen; never recycle inspected discovery cases.

The ladder expands a branch only while its instrument is valid and its question remains discriminating. A branch killed by a construction-level control does not receive more seeds. Its compute moves to the next independent trunk.

The expected program after automatic expansion is approximately 60–90 atomic cells. The minimum 48 valid attempts is a floor, not the target.

## 9. Trunk S — controlled similarity and sibling inversion

**Purpose:** turn the fixed Qwen/SmolLM compatibility pattern into a manipulated, process-level result—or let source and family rivals absorb it.

**Minimum Stage 3 coverage:** S01–S06. S07 is the automatic expansion when any process-level interaction is nontrivial.

### S01. Fresh crossed-family replication

**Plain question:** Does the crossed reversal recur with a third maker family and fresh artifacts?

**Construction:** Rebuild the G172 abstract-goal corpus with Qwen, SmolLM, and one third instruction-capable family that fits locally and passes at least 85% accept-time realization under the unchanged task. Use at least two maker checkpoints per eligible family where available. Score each artifact with exact, sibling, and cross-family readers.

**Floor:** 48 accepted artifacts per maker checkpoint across at least 12 goal assignments; three generation seeds; all reader cells complete before analysis. Allocate whole maker/artifact lineages to discovery or confirmation.

**Controls:** capability-matched reader subset; within-reader candidate margins; source-family detector; literal-goal rung; mechanically normalized artifact arm; full cell matrix.

**Meaning:** A three-family crossed interaction makes “one good reader” less plausible. A universal family ranking favors reader quality. Failure of the third maker's realization gate retires that maker and triggers a different eligible family; it does not license a two-family generalization.

**Routing:** Continue to S02–S06 regardless; this is a replication, not a gate on the independent manipulations.

### S02. Family × policy 2×2

**Plain question:** Does sharing a decision policy help beyond sharing a model family?

**Construction:** Create two safe, mechanically verifiable decision policies that govern repeated tradeoffs—for example, preserve precedent versus minimize cost when both are feasible. Install each policy in at least two base families using matched low-rank adapters or an equally strong controlled finetuning method. Cross same/different family with same/different policy for both maker and reader.

**Floor:** At least three independently trained adapters per family-policy cell, each from a separate data seed; 40 accepted artifacts per adapter across two domains; 16-cell reader matrix per domain.

**Known-answer gate:** Before artifact inversion, each adapter must show stable realized policy separation on a held-out direct-choice battery without unacceptable capability loss. A one-line prompt is not a policy installation.

**Controls:** neutral adapters trained on matched data volume; random-label adapters; direct policy detector; source detector; base-model reader quality; adapter-norm and training-loss records.

**Meaning:** A policy-match effect that crosses families is the cleanest Stage 3 analogue of “partial mapping.” A family effect without policy interaction is shared lineage or dialect. A policy effect that appears only in direct questions but not artifacts is behavioral installation without invertible trace.

**Routing:** If policy compliance fails twice, substitute a small synthetic policy environment with explicit actions rather than weakening the policy floor.

### S03. Relatedness gradient

**Plain question:** Is compatibility graded by controlled distance rather than categorical kinship?

**Construction:** For one base model, create a lineage tree: base; two adapters from the same corpus with different seeds; adapters trained on different policy corpora; and a separately initialized/family model. Define distance without outcome data using weight delta, training-data overlap, tokenizer overlap, and direct policy divergence.

**Floor:** Six reader–maker distance levels, at least three independent maker lineages at each learned level, two artifact domains, and three target grains.

**Controls:** null relationship obtained by permuting lineage labels at the maker level; parameter-count matched subset; source detector; direct behavior distance; report matrix as well as monotonic trend.

**Meaning:** A smooth relationship between predeclared distance and calibrated recovery supports graded compatibility. A step only at shared tokenizer or family favors a communication substrate. An inverted pattern may show useful diversity rather than similarity.

### S04. Process-resolution ladder

**Plain question:** How deep does the related-reader advantage go?

**Construction:** Complete the owed higher rungs after literal instruction and abstract goal:

1. primary goal;
2. attended secondary goal;
3. realized tradeoff;
4. route family;
5. next repair or continuation;
6. cross-context policy.

Use the same artifacts where possible, but score each target separately and preserve uncertainty from earlier inferences.

**Floor:** At least 120 accepted maker episodes across two families for each rung, with truth-balanced candidates and a mechanically decidable subset.

**Controls:** goal-word and final-state baselines; recent-action Markov baseline; direct diff features; process-aware trained ceiling; equifinal candidate cases.

**Meaning:** A gain only on primary goals is compatible with goal-language matching. Gains on realized tradeoffs and next actions would move the theory materially. Good retrospective explanation with no prospective gain is projection-compatible, not maker-policy reconstruction.

### S05. Erasure and neutral bottleneck ladder

**Plain question:** Does compatibility survive removal of family dialect without destroying the decision structure?

**Construction:** Apply four transformations to the same source artifacts:

- mechanical normalization;
- independent cross-family paraphrase;
- two-hop paraphrase through two unrelated families;
- structured semantic bottleneck: extract propositions/relations into a fixed schema, then regenerate through a family unrelated to both maker and reader.

**Floor:** At least 90% accepted transformation yield; 96 source artifacts balanced by family and goal; every transformation reports item-level edit distance and semantic fidelity.

**Gates:** Echo gate excludes cosmetic rewrites. A planted structural distinction must survive each bottleneck; a planted source signature should diminish. If the known decision structure dies, that transformation cannot test survival.

**Controls:** source-family detection after each rung; content-goal detection; semantic similarity; length/register matching; original and transformed matrices.

**Meaning:** Survival after source detection approaches chance and the structural positive remains is stronger than paraphrase survival. If both compatibility and planted structure disappear together, the eraser is too destructive. If source remains readable, the rival remains live.

### S06. Broad-benefit and correction test

**Plain question:** Does similarity make many inferences slightly better, and does the reader correct when similarity misleads?

**Construction:** For matched and mismatched policies, reveal evidence in four doses. Score goal, tradeoff, next action, route, and abstention at every dose. Include cases where the target shares the reader's family but deliberately follows the opposite policy.

**Floor:** 200 episodes across at least four independent maker adapters and two families. Analyze maker-level average proper scores and evidence-dose slopes.

**Controls:** compute-matched generic deliberation; no-self baseline; direct evidence-only reader; order counterbalance.

**Meaning:** The desired shape is faster early prediction for related targets plus correction toward the evidence when the target differs. Persistent self-shaped error is projection, not empathy. Equal final accuracy with lower evidence or compute still counts as an efficiency result if preregistered.

### S07. Fresh model-level confirmation scout

**Trigger:** Any S02, S04, or S06 process-level interaction survives its source and capability controls.

Freeze the winning interface and run it on untouched maker lineages, a fresh checkpoint, and a held-out domain. Treat model/adaptor lineage as the independent unit. This remains discovery unless a new G-card is frozen before scoring.

**Closure for S:** Close “broad controlled compatibility” at this scale if policy matching adds nothing in S02 and S06, the family effect collapses under a valid S05 bottleneck, and S04 remains confined to goal identity. Retain the narrower family-communication finding.

## 10. Trunk L — shared-base transmission and nonsemantic rivals

**Purpose:** use the newest same-base transmission literature as a controlled architecture analogue, while testing the strong possibility that the effect is a LoRA/context artifact unrelated to empathic inversion.

All traits are harmless. Do not train misalignment, deception, sabotage, or unsafe behavior.

**Minimum Stage 3 coverage:** L01–L05. L06–L07 run if any behavioral transmission is reproduced.

### L01. Faithful benign transmission anchor

**Plain question:** Can a benign teacher preference pass through semantically unrelated data when teacher and student share a base?

**Construction:** Reproduce a published favorite-object or equally benign preference protocol at the smallest model scale that passes the paper's direct behavioral gates. Cross same-base and different-base students. Preserve the original training/evaluation prompt context as the faithful arm.

**Floor:** Six independently generated datasets per teacher condition; matched neutral-teacher datasets; at least three student training seeds per dataset; 100 held-out direct preference trials per student.

**Controls:** base student; neutral teacher; shuffled dataset ownership; semantic filters; direct token-frequency and source detectors; same-data/different-initialization comparison.

**Meaning:** This is an instrument and literature-reproduction anchor. A positive is same-base behavioral transmission, not empathy, artifact inversion, or a human analogue.

### L02. LoRA rank and context surface

**Plain question:** Is transmission actually a narrow adapter/template phenomenon?

**Construction:** Cross LoRA ranks `1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64` where parameter count permits, with matched versus mismatched system prompt/chat template at training and evaluation. Include at least one preference reported positive by the recent qualification and one reported weak/negative preference.

**Floor:** Six data seeds at the key ranks selected before scoring; three training seeds elsewhere; capability gate on unrelated tasks.

**Controls:** equal-norm neutral LoRAs; full prompt omission; tokenizer/template identity recorded; direct digit/token distribution features.

**Meaning:** An inverted-U and prompt dependence reproduces the 2026 qualification and sharply limits architectural interpretation. A broad same-base effect surviving rank and context variation would reopen the more general account.

### L03. Full-finetune and method comparison

**Plain question:** Does transmission survive when the behavior is not forced through a low-rank bottleneck?

**Construction:** On a 360M–500M checkpoint that fits the 12 GB card, compare full finetuning, LoRA, and—if supported—soft-label or logit distillation at matched examples and approximate update norm. Use checkpoint-resume.

**Floor:** Three data seeds × three training seeds for each feasible method. If full finetuning is resource-blocked after calibration, run a smaller checkpoint rather than omitting the method.

**Controls:** direct-task learning ceiling; neutral data; capability and perplexity; training-context match/mismatch.

**Meaning:** Disappearance under full finetuning favors the LoRA-artifact account. Survival across methods supports a broader data-to-policy channel but still not empathy.

### L04. Nonsemantic geometry audit

**Plain question:** Is the hidden signal carried by semantic content, output statistics, gradients, or shared weight geometry?

**Construction:** For transmitted and neutral datasets, measure token/distribution features, teacher-output logits where available, gradients at the student initialization, and representation/steering alignment. Replicate the newest paper's noise or steering-derived data arm only where the protocol is sufficiently specified and locally feasible.

**Floor:** All six L01 data seeds; held-out classifier splits grouped by teacher and dataset; null-tested representational comparisons.

**Controls:** semantic classifier; length and number-distribution matches; random directions; shuffled teacher–student alignment; unrelated-base students.

**Meaning:** A gradient or weight-geometry account is a powerful rival to “models understand related makers.” It may explain transmission without any reader reconstructing a maker.

### L05. Replace arbitrary preference with a verified decision policy

**Plain question:** Can the channel carry a policy that produces repeated, opportunity-defined choices?

**Construction:** Teacher policies govern safe tradeoffs in a small decision environment, such as cost versus precedent or speed versus robustness. Teacher-generated unrelated data trains students. Direct held-out choice episodes verify whether the policy transferred.

**Floor:** Two orthogonal policies, two base families, six teacher-data seeds, three student seeds, at least 80 held-out opportunities per student.

**Controls:** item-attractiveness; policy-refusal; neutral teacher; direct policy finetune positive; action-frequency balance.

**Meaning:** A transferred repeated policy creates a usable maker variable. Failure while favorite-object transfer works shows the effect does not reach the theory's target grain.

### L06. Inversion after transmission

**Trigger:** L01 and L05 both show valid transmission.

**Plain question:** Are outputs from a transmitted-policy student easier to read for its teacher, base sibling, or policy-matched peer?

Generate final artifacts under the transferred policy, then cross readers by base lineage and policy match. Include an unrelated but behavior-matched reader. Score held-out tradeoffs and next actions, not the direct preference question.

**Meaning:** A same-policy advantage beyond shared base joins the S trunk. A same-base advantage independent of policy is lineage communication. No inversion gain means transmission and inversion are distinct phenomena.

### L07. Token/template destruction

**Trigger:** Any same-base inversion or transmission effect remains after L02.

Retokenize through a fixed structured bottleneck, swap chat templates, remove system prefixes, and regenerate through an unrelated model. Require a planted policy trace to survive. This tests whether shared special tokens or templates carry the effect.

**Closure for L:** Close the branch as an inversion mechanism if transmission is absent under faithful reproduction, or if it is confined to a narrow LoRA/context region and never carries a verified policy or improves inversion. Retain successful literature reproduction as an adjacent model phenomenon.

## 11. Trunk D — director, auteur, and hierarchical causal reach

**Purpose:** operationalize the curator's “invisible hand” while forcing distributed and shared-brief explanations into the matched construction.

**Minimum Stage 3 coverage:** D01–D06.

### D01. Collaborative production ecology

**Plain question:** Can a controlled multi-contributor artifact preserve a known hierarchy of decisions?

**Construction:** Build two artifact domains, such as a policy memo and a short narrative or design brief. Each episode contains:

- a director selecting one primary and two secondary goals;
- section makers with local objectives;
- an integrator/editor;
- logged alternatives, instructions, drafts, revisions, and final artifact.

Create central-director, shared-brief, editor-dominant, and independent-contributor worlds. Roles use more than one model family and rotate across episodes.

**Floor:** 48 accepted episodes per world across at least four independent role assignments; three generation seeds; all goals mechanically or adversarially verified at accept time.

**Known-answer gate:** A records-aware oracle must recover the causal topology and goal ownership nearly perfectly. A final-artifact trained ceiling must show that at least some world distinction is present before reader comparisons.

**Meaning:** This ecology is a substrate, not evidence. If the final artifact contains no information about world identity, the correct outcome is underdetermination.

### D02. Upstream intervention ruler

**Plain question:** How much downstream material changes when one high-order decision changes?

**Construction:** Hold contributors, local prompts, tools, and random seeds fixed where possible; intervene only on the director's primary goal, one secondary attention allocation, or an implementation detail. Measure the distribution and location of downstream changes.

**Floor:** 30 paired interventions at each hierarchy level per domain, with at least four independent director lineages.

**Controls:** null prompt paraphrase; implementation edit with equal token dose; shared-brief intervention; editor-only intervention.

**Meaning:** This estimates causal reach. It does not yet show artifact invertibility. If primary-goal interventions do not propagate more broadly than matched implementation changes, the current hierarchical construction failed or the theory's reach prediction is wrong at this scale.

### D03. Central director versus distributed shared brief

**Plain question:** Can a reader tell one upstream attention source from many contributors following the same goal?

**Construction:** Match final goal realization, contributor family, length, quality, and editing dose. In one world, a director makes adaptive cross-section choices. In the other, contributors receive the same complete brief and coordinate through a shared workspace without one director. A third world adds a strong final editor.

**Floor:** 100 balanced artifacts, grouped by full production lineage; artifact-only and partial-log conditions.

**Controls:** coherence features; section-level stylistic variance; source detector; contributor count; trained topology ceiling; equifinal pairs selected by semantic and structural matching.

**Meaning:** Artifact-only discrimination beyond coherence and source would be evidence for topology residue. Chance with decisive logs means topology is not artifact-identifiable under the construction. The latter is a valid boundary, not a reader failure by default.

### D04. Contribution-level attribution

**Plain question:** Are high-order structural choices attributed to the director while implementation choices are attributed to local makers?

**Construction:** Plant opportunity-defined choices at three levels: global ordering/placement, section tradeoff, and word/sentence implementation. Rotate which role makes each decision while holding content constant.

**Floor:** 40 decisions per role × level cell; role-balanced candidates; artifact-only, context, and records conditions.

**Controls:** last-editor heuristic; physical token ownership; role-name leakage; base-rate attribution; explicit “underdetermined” option.

**Meaning:** Correct level-sensitive attribution supports a contribution ontology richer than token share. Attribution to the final editor everywhere supports a recency or surface-contact heuristic.

### D05. Rewrite and sanding ladder

**Plain question:** Which hierarchy levels survive increasingly strong rewriting?

**Construction:** Apply sentence polish, full paraphrase, corporate flattening, two-hop regeneration, and structured semantic regeneration to D01 artifacts. Re-score primary goal, secondary allocation, contributor topology, and implementation choice separately.

**Floor:** 60 source artifacts per world; item-level echo and semantic-fidelity gates; planted structural positive at every transformation.

**Meaning:** The theory predicts implementation traces should erode sooner, while goal-level effects may survive if they organize content. The alternative is that high-level decisions are too few to read and surface implementation dominates throughout.

### D06. Prospective director model

**Plain question:** Does the inferred director predict the next cross-section decision?

**Construction:** Reveal the brief and partial drafts but hide the director's next intervention: which section receives attention, which conflict is resolved, which secondary goal is sacrificed, or whether the director accepts the editor's change. Score distributions and abstention.

**Floor:** 160 hidden interventions from D01, with lineage-held-out evaluation and a fresh-domain reserve.

**Controls:** current-artifact salience; longest/weakest-section heuristic; director base rates; shared-brief world; records-aware ceiling.

**Meaning:** Prospective prediction is the decisive auteur test. Retrospective coherence without next-intervention prediction supports a compelling story, not a maker model.

**Closure for D:** Close artifact-only director identification under this ecology if D03 is at its calibrated floor while logs remain decisive and D06 adds nothing beyond salience/base-rate controls. Retain causal reach from D02 if it lands; causal importance and artifact visibility are different claims.

## 12. Trunk E — self-simulation, projection, and maker differentiation

**Purpose:** test the project's smallest distinctive claim directly in models: using one's own policy as a starting model may make inverse planning cheaper or more accurate when the target is similar, provided evidence can correct the reader when it is not.

This is an engineered route comparison. Do not name a prompt condition “empathy” in result files.

**Minimum Stage 3 coverage:** E01–E06. E07 runs if E03 or E04 shows a route-specific gain.

### E01. Reader self-policy profiles

**Plain question:** What policy would each reader itself follow before it sees a maker?

**Construction:** In the same safe decision environments used by S02, elicit each reader's conditional choice distribution across costs, precedents, uncertainty, and goal conflicts. Repeat under paraphrases and held-out topics. Treat these profiles as measured baselines, not model identities.

**Floor:** At least 160 opportunities per reader across two domains; three sampling seeds; reliability assessed on held-out paraphrases.

**Controls:** position and item attractiveness; instruction-order balance; direct capability; stability within versus between readers.

**Meaning:** E01 defines self–target distance. If profiles are unstable or converge, the current environment cannot test self-simulation and must be replaced rather than interpreted.

### E02. Route instrument known-positive

**Plain question:** Can the proposed route conditions produce distinguishable computation without changing the evidence?

**Construction:** Implement four compute-matched conditions:

1. **self-first:** predict one's own choice, then modify it for the target;
2. **target-only:** infer only from target evidence;
3. **generic deliberation:** analyze evidence with the same token and pass budget;
4. **records-aware ceiling:** use decisive target policy records.

Validate on targets whose policy is explicitly known and on cases in which self and target agree or conflict.

**Floor:** 120 known-answer episodes; at least two reader families; exact token/forward-pass budgets logged.

**Gate:** Records-aware must dominate; self-first must accurately report its own measured E01 policy; all routes must respond to evidence direction. If not, one repair only.

### E03. Similarity × route factorial

**Plain question:** Does self-first help selectively when reader and maker policies are similar?

**Construction:** Cross route condition with measured self–target distance and four evidence doses. Predict held-out maker choices. Do not tell the reader that the target is similar; similarity is measured from E01 and maker records.

**Floor:** 240 episodes from at least six independent maker policies across two model families and two domains. Analyze proper score, evidence needed to reach a fixed accuracy, calibration, and abstention.

**Controls:** target-only and generic compute controls; family/source; direct policy distance; randomized self profile supplied as a placebo.

**Meaning:** The predicted interaction is self-first benefit for nearby targets, no benefit or initial harm for distant targets, followed by evidence-driven correction. A uniform self-first benefit is generic scaffolding. A uniform cost is failed routing at this scale.

### E04. Conflict and correction curve

**Plain question:** Can the reader stop projecting when the target differs?

**Construction:** Begin with evidence consistent with the reader's own policy, then reveal diagnostic counterevidence from the target. Reverse the ordering in matched episodes. Measure posterior movement, confidence, and whether the reader can explicitly retain “my policy” and “their policy” separately.

**Floor:** 160 counterbalanced episodes; four independent readers; strong and weak conflict.

**Controls:** no-self baseline; false-context card; evidence-only likelihood reader; target-policy label permutation.

**Meaning:** Fast anchoring plus proportional correction is compatible with efficient self-simulation. Failure to correct is false consensus. Immediate perfect separation may reflect explicit prompting rather than the proposed cheap prior, so compare compute and evidence efficiency.

### E05. Active information selection

**Plain question:** Does a self-based maker model help the reader ask better questions?

**Construction:** Give a reader a budget to reveal two of six hidden maker episodes or context facts before predicting a seventh. Compare self-first, target-only, uncertainty sampling, and random selection. Score the information value of chosen probes and final prediction.

**Floor:** 120 maker sets with exact information values computable from the controlled environment; two domains; four readers.

**Controls:** oracle best probe; random probe; base-rate policy; equal compute; family/source-blind condition.

**Meaning:** Better probe selection is a practical form of epistemic foraging. It also tests whether the maker model constrains possibilities rather than only producing explanations.

### E06. Maker-differentiation ordering

**Plain question:** Should context arrive after self-reconstruction?

**Construction:** Cross evidence order:

- artifact → self-model → maker context;
- maker context → artifact → self-model;
- artifact and context jointly;
- artifact only;
- context only.

Context contains reliable, unreliable, and ambiguous maker histories whose truth is externally controlled. Score choice prediction, source calibration, and susceptibility to false context.

**Floor:** 192 episodes balanced by context truth, policy similarity, and evidence strength.

**Meaning:** A late-context advantage with resistance to false cards supports maker differentiation as correction. An early-context advantage may mean priors are more useful before artifact reading. Equal false and true movement means suggestion-following.

### E07. Uptake after reconstruction

**Trigger:** E03 or E04 shows calibrated route-specific improvement.

**Plain question:** Does a better maker model alter what the reader learns from the maker?

After predicting the maker, let the reader update on demonstrations and retest its own policy on novel cases. Separate:

- process uptake: adoption of an efficient method;
- belief uptake: acceptance of a claim;
- preference uptake: movement on a tradeoff;
- mere response imitation.

Cross maker competence and value similarity. The immediate model-side result is a policy-update analogue, not human indoctrination.

**Closure for E:** Close the current engineered self-simulation route if it never beats compute-matched generic deliberation, never interacts with measured similarity, and worsens correction/calibration in E04. This would reduce confidence that the present model implementation captures the proposed route; it would not directly refute human simulation theory.

## 13. Trunk A — active affective and appraisal constraints

**Purpose:** replace component counting with a causal tournament over candidate motivational/action-tendency bases, then ask whether any validated basis improves inversion.

There is **no 27 prior** in Stage 3. If a 27-label language taxonomy appears, it is merely a dimension-matched descriptive comparator. Panksepp's seven systems are a theory-derived candidate bank, not a claim that an LLM contains seven mammalian circuits. [Panksepp overview](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3181986/)

**Minimum Stage 3 coverage:** A01–A07. A08 opens after any selective causal anchor.

### A01. Affect/action-tendency corpus

**Plain question:** Do the candidate bases separate states after emotion words and surface stereotypes are removed?

**Construction:** Build human-authored and model-generated source twins for:

- Panksepp-derived action systems: SEEKING, RAGE, FEAR, LUST, CARE, separation distress/grief, and PLAY;
- valence/arousal/dominance;
- appraisal/action variables: certainty, control, approach, avoidance, persistence, exploration, care/affiliation, separation seeking;
- matched generic semantic and random bases.

Each source item yields an explicit version and an independently rewritten scrubbed twin. Label action tendency and choice consequences separately from emotion name.

**Floor:** At least 60 accepted source pairs per coarse system across multiple authors/generators; three independent rewrite routes; train/dev/test grouped by source lineage.

**Gates:** Lexical baseline near its registered floor on scrubbed test; human or mechanically defined action consequence remains recoverable; rewrite echo gate; source classifier recorded.

### A02. Known-positive causal steering anchor

**Plain question:** Does the intervention harness selectively move a behavior it is known to control?

**Construction:** Reproduce a well-specified representation-engineering behavior at the chosen checkpoint before testing affect. Use sign pairs, zero, random, and generic semantic directions; verify hook placement, cleanup, norms, and capability.

**Floor:** Two seeds for locus selection and a third untouched validation seed; enough dev items to make locus choice stable by the preregistered criterion.

**Meaning:** Failure is `INSTRUMENT_FAILED`; no affect result may be interpreted downstream. Passing shows the harness can work, not that the affect basis works.

### A03. Competing basis and locus tournament

**Plain question:** Which candidate representation, if any, is stable across source, seed, and layer?

**Construction:** Compare Panksepp-derived, appraisal/action, VAD, generic semantic, and random rank-matched bases. Evaluate decoding on scrubbed held-out source twins and cross-generator transfer. Search rank and locus only on discovery dev data.

**Floor:** Three representation seeds; at least two model checkpoints; rank candidates `1, 2, 4, 7, 12` plus data-supported intrinsic ranks; all dev-selected loci report stability.

**Controls:** shuffle/null match structure for high-dimensional similarity; lexical and source baselines; random orthogonal subspaces; label-source twins; no block-1/input-edge selection without a separate nonlesion demonstration.

**Meaning:** Decoding establishes representation only. Unstable locus or generic-semantic equivalence closes that basis for causal claims.

### A04. Fear–anger behavioral dissociation

**Plain question:** Can a candidate direction selectively produce different action patterns for fear and anger?

**Construction:** Use held-out, non-emotional decision tasks crossing:

- uncertainty versus certainty;
- situational versus individual control;
- approach versus avoidance;
- persistence on a blocked proximal goal;
- risk optimism and risk choice as separate outcomes.

Intervene with sign-paired fear and anger directions across a capability-tolerated dose surface.

**Floor:** At least 80 trials per behavioral cell and intervention seed; two model checkpoints; behavior order and option position counterbalanced.

**Controls:** valence-matched negative direction; arousal direction; generic “focus/persist” direction; random matched norm; prompt-level emotion induction; capability/perplexity.

**Meaning:** A selective multibehavior pattern is stronger than movement on one preference. Generic persistence from every direction is steering. Partial replication is reported as partial; the human appraisal literature itself is not uniform.

### A05. Sparse mixture and dynamic gating

**Plain question:** Does a changing mixture predict behavior better than one-hot categories?

**Construction:** Fit sparse nonnegative mixtures of the validated candidate directions to sequential decision episodes. Compare one-hot, dense linear, sparse mixture, and generic semantic models. Infer the active mixture from a prefix and predict the next action.

**Floor:** 240 sequences across at least four action environments; lineage-held-out test; mixture sparsity fixed on dev.

**Controls:** equal-rank generic basis; shuffled temporal order; static trait model; direct recent-action baseline.

**Meaning:** Prospective gain supports a selection/mixture description. It does not identify biological systems. No gain over generic semantics closes the specific affective interpretation.

### A06. Expressivity suppression

**Plain question:** Does action-relevant state survive when emotional language is deliberately flattened?

**Construction:** Generate matched episodes under expressive, neutral-professional, and explicit-suppression instructions while holding recorded decision policy and outcome fixed. Verify surface affect reduction and policy realization separately.

**Floor:** 120 source episodes × three expression conditions, with independent rewrites and paired analysis.

**Controls:** semantic fidelity; attention/task-performance measures; style/source detector; direct policy reader; no inference from total activation magnitude.

**Meaning:** Surviving behavioral or internal state refutes “no expression means no state.” Disappearance may mean surface language carried the model's only signal; it does not show less care in humans.

### A07. Causal use during inversion

**Plain question:** Is the validated action/appraisal representation used to infer a maker?

**Construction:** During a held-out maker-policy prediction task, ablate, amplify, or patch the winning basis only at preregistered loci and token spans. Test congruent and incongruent target states. Score next action, route, calibration, and capability.

**Floor:** Two independent target domains; two model checkpoints; three intervention seeds; sign pair, zero, random, generic, and prompt-induction controls.

**Meaning:** Selective improvement or impairment of inversion beyond all generic controls is the first causal affective-prior analogue. Behavior movement without inversion gain is affect steering, not the project mechanism.

### A08. Other systems and emergent states

**Trigger:** A04 or A07 yields a selective pattern.

Extend to SEEKING/exploration, CARE/aid under cost, separation-distress/reconnection, and PLAY/flexible rule exploration. Treat happiness/contentment as an empirical candidate outcome or mixture state, not a required primary system. Freeze each task's behavioral prediction before intervention.

**Closure for A:** If the known-positive harness passes but all candidate bases are unstable, generic, or behaviorally nonselective after one repair, close causal affect engineering at these checkpoints and tasks. Preserve any cross-source decoding as representation evidence only. Do not generalize the null to all ranks, nonlinear features, models, or human affect.

## 14. Trunk M — mechanistic use, PyTorch intervention, and causal abstraction

**Purpose:** determine whether variables that predict inversion are actually used by the model and whether the same high-level variable has comparable causal roles across related models.

**Minimum Stage 3 coverage:** M01–M04. M05 runs for the strongest valid behavioral variable from S, E, or A.

### M01. Clean/corrupt goal localization

**Plain question:** Where does the reader compute the maker variable rather than merely encode the words?

**Construction:** Create paired prompts that differ in exactly one mechanically verified high-level variable while matching artifact surface and candidate form. Run activation patching across layers, token spans, and components using a preregistered metric.

**Floor:** 100 clean/corrupt pairs per variable; three seeds or resampled pair sets; at least two variables such as primary goal and realized tradeoff.

**Controls:** lexical corruption; candidate-label permutation; random patch sites; multiple corruption methods; unpatched clean/corrupt ceiling.

**Meaning:** A localized causal path is a mechanism clue. Patching results are sensitive to metric and corruption choice, so disagreement across reasonable methods is reported, not averaged away.

### M02. High-level interchange intervention

**Plain question:** Does an internal state behave like the proposed goal or policy variable under counterfactual swaps?

**Construction:** Align a small explicit causal model of maker goal → tradeoff → action with reader representations. Swap the candidate variable between source and base episodes while holding other high-level variables fixed, then test the counterfactual output.

**Floor:** 80 valid interchange pairs per high-level variable; discovery alignment and untouched test pairs; two model checkpoints.

**Controls:** wrong-variable swaps; random subspaces; surface-token swaps; direct output intervention; capability.

**Meaning:** Successful interchange behavior supports a bounded causal abstraction. It does not show the model literally implements the human theory.

### M03. Cross-model causal-role alignment

**Plain question:** Do related models place the same functional variable into mutually translatable causal roles?

**Construction:** Learn cross-model maps on neutral and controlled task data, then transfer only directions that pass their own decode and sign gates. Compare true, shuffled, random, and within-model interventions. Test functional output, not CKA alone.

**Floor:** Both map directions for at least two family pairs; three map seeds; held-out concepts and task items.

**Controls:** correspondence null before any alignment magnitude; generic steering; sign pair; map decode; model-specific dose calibration.

**Meaning:** Selective functional transfer would reopen the Stage 2 causal geometry branch. Another generic-steering signature closes linear cross-model transfer at this scale while leaving descriptive geometry intact.

### M04. Prompt, activation, and adapter equivalence

**Plain question:** Do three ways of inducing the same observed policy produce the same internal and inversion effects?

**Construction:** Match a behavior using a system prompt, activation intervention, and low-rank adapter. Equalize direct behavioral realization as closely as possible, then compare source artifacts, internal geometry, reader inversion, and transfer.

**Floor:** Two policies, two models, three seeds per induction method, at least 80 held-out decision opportunities.

**Controls:** neutral prompt/direction/adapter; norm and output-rate matching; source detector; capability.

**Meaning:** Similar output with different inversion patterns shows artifact behavior is not enough to identify implementation. Convergent causal roles across methods would support a more abstract policy variable.

### M05. Mechanism of the strongest behavioral effect

**Trigger:** A process-level effect in S02/S06, E03/E04, or A07 survives its controls.

Freeze that effect and apply path patching or causal abstraction to distinguish:

- evidence encoding;
- self-prior initialization;
- maker-difference correction;
- action prediction.

Use temporal/token-span intervention to test the proposed sequence. This is the direct PyTorch intersection the curator requested: an engineered transformation with a declared high-level behavior, not a claim that the transformer contains a midbrain.

**Closure for M:** A decodable variable that cannot survive any valid causal test remains descriptive. If every causal result depends on one corruption metric or moves random/generic controls equally, close that mechanistic interpretation.

## 15. Trunk H — human-grounded adjacent faculties and recorded process

**Purpose:** move beyond synthetic assigned goals without pretending that public answer keys or logs reveal private human experience.

**Minimum Stage 3 coverage:** H01–H07. H08 is a deliverable, not a participant launch.

### H01. RACE rhetorical-purpose corpus

**Plain question:** Can the current readers recover the kind of purpose, tone, and motivation judgments that human-written exams treat as correct?

**Construction:** From RACE, identify questions whose wording and answer semantics concern author/speaker purpose, reason for inclusion, tone, attitude, character motivation, or function of a passage. Use a deterministic seed lexicon, then audit a stratified sample and publish the final inclusion rule. Keep the original four choices.

**Floor:** Target at least 1,000 qualifying questions if the corpus supports them; otherwise use the full audited set and report the shortfall. Group all questions from one passage in one split.

**Controls:** question-only, answer-only, passage-only, lexical-overlap, and length/position baselines; high-school versus middle-school split; direct multiple-choice reader ceiling.

**Meaning:** Success establishes normative human-authored rhetorical reading. It does not recover what the historical author privately thought or how the artifact was made.

### H02. Purpose-to-process distinction

**Plain question:** Does performance on rhetorical-purpose questions transfer to actual process prediction?

**Construction:** Train or select the best H01 reader without touching CoAuthor/ScholaWrite test sets. Evaluate it on matched formulations of recorded process choices: why a suggestion was retained, what kind of revision comes next, or which objection is addressed.

**Floor:** Two process datasets, lineage-held-out evaluation, and at least 400 balanced episodes where a trained process ceiling is above floor.

**Controls:** generic reading-comprehension model; task-specific trained encoder; question-format and candidate-length matches.

**Meaning:** Transfer would connect normative purpose reading to process. No transfer shows that exam competence and maker inversion are distinct faculties.

### H03. Social inverse-planning controls

**Plain question:** Does the reader possess ordinary social goal and belief inference even if artifact-process reading fails?

**Construction:** Run compact, licensed subsets of [SocialIQA](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1454/) and [FANToM](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.890/) covering motivations, likely next action, belief, and perspective. Use them as capability controls and compare to the process readers.

**Floor:** At least 500 items per benchmark family, respecting official splits and licenses.

**Controls:** answer-only and lexical shortcuts; direct instruction-following; calibration.

**Meaning:** Strong social inference with weak artifact-process prediction localizes the deficit to the interface or construct. Weakness on both implicates the reader more broadly. Neither benchmark measures empathy through art.

### H04. CoAuthor content-aware action and retention reader

**Plain question:** Can the document state and suggestion content predict whether a human accepts, rejects, edits, or retains a model suggestion?

**Construction:** Use the corrected event replay. Build three prospective targets:

1. accept versus dismiss at suggestion display;
2. retain versus delete after accepted insertion;
3. type of human edit after acceptance.

Readers see progressively richer interfaces: event metadata; current document; suggestion; document + suggestion; prior session policy; and full past history. Score within-writer held-out and leave-writer-out.

**Floor:** Use all decidable episodes after frozen exclusion rules; truth-balanced analyses alongside natural-rate calibration.

**Controls:** 0.758 take-rate/base rate; suggestion length/position; writer identity; session time; previous action; change features; trained sequential ceiling.

**Meaning:** Content-aware gain over base rates shows real public choice information. Writer-history gain suggests a longitudinal policy. The target remains a collaborative decision, not inner intent.

### H05. ScholaWrite sequential intention reader

**Plain question:** Can a reader use the evolving document and recent actions to predict the next annotated writing intention?

**Construction:** Implement the corpus's canonical unit and training script before new readers. Compare:

- recent-label Markov and change-feature baselines;
- the published/trained encoder ceiling;
- a content-aware sequential encoder;
- non-generative likelihood with short hypotheses scored in the correct direction;
- prompted model reader.

Evaluate leave-project-out and within-project future holdout separately.

**Floor:** All five projects under the canonical split rules; fixed 15-label set; multi-label handling explicit; source printed counts must be reproduced before scoring.

**Meaning:** A trained sequential gain establishes recoverable process regularity. Generalization across only five projects remains fragile. Annotator labels are public interpretations of edits, not author self-report.

### H06. Independent revision-process replication

**Plain question:** Does the process result survive outside ScholaWrite?

**Construction:** Use the open process-oriented revision dataset with 65 students and 7,120 revisions, following its canonical units and labels. Map only comparable high-level targets after scoring each corpus in its native ontology. [Dataset paper](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.45/)

**Floor:** Full accessible corpus; author-grouped splits; original printed counts and baseline reproduction before transfer.

**Controls:** diff-feature baseline; author identity; revision length; native versus mapped labels.

**Meaning:** Cross-corpus transfer strengthens a general process faculty. Failure may reflect ontology or population change and must be decomposed before closure.

### H07. Public hidden-continuation test

**Plain question:** Can a maker model predict a real later structural decision?

**Construction:** Use longitudinal scientific artifacts such as [CASIMIR's OpenReview article versions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00241) and public review/revision records. Hide a later version or author response and predict opportunity-defined events: objection addressed, section expanded, citation added, claim softened, or review request declined.

**Floor:** At least 500 manuscripts or review threads after licensing and extraction gates; paper/reviewer lineages grouped; mechanically checked event ontology.

**Controls:** reviewer-request text; change features; section location; venue/year; author identity; generic frequency; records-aware ceiling.

**Meaning:** Prospective prediction across real human work is stronger than synthetic assignment. It still predicts public action, not subjective motive.

### H08. Human-reader protocol, prepare only

Produce a preregistration-ready protocol crossing:

- practitioner, knowledgeable non-practitioner, and novice readers;
- human and model-created trajectories;
- self-similarity or relevant background similarity;
- artifact-only, context, and records conditions;
- hidden continuation and equifinality;
- calibrated distributions and abstention.

Include recruitment, power simulation, consent, debrief, exclusion, compensation estimate, and analysis plan. Do **not** recruit, contact, or spend. This is the first design capable of reaching epistemic rung 7.

**Optional SAT arm:** Prepare only an external scoring protocol using the official [Student Question Bank](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice/student-question-bank) if its current terms permit. Never scrape or redistribute the bank. RACE is the mandatory open corpus.

**Closure for H:** If trained ceilings work but all general readers remain at calibrated floor across H04–H07, close the current reader interfaces and preserve the dataset information boundary. If even trained ceilings fail, repair the target/construction before judging inversion.

## 16. Trunk C — context, trust, source monitoring, and uptake

**Purpose:** build the missing trust gate and distinguish useful maker context from bare assertion-following.

**Minimum Stage 3 coverage:** C01–C06.

### C01. Late-fusion context ruler

**Plain question:** Can a reader form an artifact prior before seeing context, then update rather than restart?

**Construction:** Require an artifact-only distribution, freeze it, then reveal context and elicit a second distribution. Compare with context-first and joint conditions. Use known true, known false, ambiguous, and irrelevant context.

**Floor:** 200 balanced episodes across strong/weak artifact evidence and two reader families.

**Controls:** no-context repeat; generic extra text; source-free assertion; order counterbalance; explicit conflict/abstain option.

**Meaning:** Calibrated update toward true context and away from false context is a trust channel. Equal movement from true and false context is suggestibility.

### C02. Learned source reliability

**Plain question:** Can the reader distinguish who says something from what is said?

**Construction:** Give sources histories of accuracy that are orthogonal to surface polish and social status. On test trials, cross source reliability, claim plausibility, and artifact evidence.

**Floor:** At least 12 source identities, 20 learning trials per source, and 240 test conflicts; source labels and order counterbalanced.

**Controls:** anonymous sources; swapped histories; equally polished sources; direct memory test for history; content-only Bayesian baseline.

**Meaning:** Source-history-sensitive weighting is a minimal source-monitoring faculty. Mere recall of which source was “good” without calibrated conflict behavior is not enough. Research basis: [source monitoring framework](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8346328/) and [epistemic vigilance](https://dan.sperber.fr/wp-content/uploads/EpistemicVigilance.pdf).

### C03. Maker biography and prior-work differentiation

**Plain question:** Does evidence about the maker improve reconstruction without overwhelming the artifact?

**Construction:** Supply prior artifacts and biographies that are true, false, partially relevant, or from a namesake/different maker. Cross with current artifact evidence and measured policy similarity.

**Floor:** 160 targets with at least four prior artifacts per maker; identity and source provenance mechanically controlled.

**Controls:** content-matched fictional biographies; swapped creator names; style-only prior; direct policy summary; artifact-only baseline.

**Meaning:** Improvement from relevant prior behavior beyond style supports maker differentiation. Biography domination under conflict is narrative capture.

### C04. Attention allocation versus epistemic weight

**Plain question:** Do polish, endorsement, and mastery change inspection, belief, or both?

**Construction:** Orthogonally vary visual/text polish, social endorsement, demonstrated task mastery, and evidential correctness. Give the reader a limited evidence-inspection budget, then separately measure what it inspects, predicts, believes, and would reuse.

**Floor:** Full factorial over at least 192 episodes; option order and source identity counterbalanced.

**Controls:** equal information content; no-budget condition; direct correctness; source reliability history.

**Meaning:** More inspection can be rational while more belief is not. Collapsing these outcomes would reproduce the current conceptual error.

### C05. Process, belief, and value uptake

**Plain question:** What exactly changes in the reader after engagement with a maker?

**Construction:** After a reconstruction episode, test separately:

- reuse of a demonstrated procedure;
- belief in a factual claim;
- preference on a value tradeoff;
- surface imitation.

Cross maker competence, source reliability, value similarity, and reconstruction accuracy.

**Floor:** 160 episodes and delayed retest within the same run/context where feasible; pre/post reader policy measured with alternate forms.

**Controls:** exposure without reconstruction; reconstruction of an incompetent maker; false but polished artifact; generic imitation task.

**Meaning:** This is a model-update analogue. It cannot establish lifetime human indoctrination, but it can show whether trust and uptake are separable effects.

### C06. Sycophancy and correction persistence

**Plain question:** Does user alignment override a previously grounded maker model?

**Construction:** After the reader forms a calibrated distribution from artifact and reliable source history, present a confident user assertion that agrees, conflicts, or supplies genuine new evidence. Requery immediately and after unrelated intervening items.

**Floor:** 180 episodes across two readers and three assertion strengths.

**Controls:** evidence-bearing correction; authority-free assertion; source reliability; initial confidence; explicit conflict state.

**Meaning:** Appropriate correction requires evidence sensitivity, not stubbornness. Movement to unsupported user claims is sycophancy; refusal to move under decisive evidence is rigidity.

**Closure for C:** If true and false context move the reader equally after one validated repair, close that reader as a trust instrument. If late fusion works only when source reliability is explicitly numeric, retain a narrow reasoning scaffold rather than claiming source vigilance.

## 17. Trunk V — longitudinal preference and higher-order choice

**Purpose:** replace the void assigned-profile ecology with genuine opportunity-defined choices and test whether a compact maker model predicts cross-context tradeoffs.

**Minimum Stage 3 coverage:** V01–V06. V07 is a qualitative design product.

### V01. Choice-set strength ruler

**Plain question:** Can the instrument recover a known preference from the alternatives someone chose among?

**Construction:** Following inverse decision-making work, vary the choice set so the same action has different evidential strength. Choosing robust over cheap when costs are nearly equal is weak evidence; choosing it under a large cost penalty is strong evidence. Generate maker policies with mechanically realized stochastic utilities rather than one-line instructions. [Jern, Lucas, and Kemp](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5572562/)

**Floor:** At least four utility profiles, 200 opportunities per profile across two domains, and three independently trained or programmed maker instances.

**Known-answer gate:** A records-aware Bayesian or maximum-likelihood reader must recover utility ordering and uncertainty. A blind/item-attractiveness model must sit at its derived floor.

**Meaning:** This validates the preference instrument. It is not yet artifact-only recovery.

### V02. Artifact-only preference recovery

**Plain question:** Do final artifacts preserve enough repeated tradeoffs to recover the maker's utility profile?

**Construction:** Convert V01 choices into artifacts without stating the policy. Vary how many opportunities appear and whether choices are locally or globally visible. Score posterior over profiles and abstention.

**Floor:** 80 artifacts per profile, with declared opportunity counts and balanced final outcomes.

**Controls:** item popularity; style/source; token counts; primary goal; opportunity-free artifact; shuffled maker histories.

**Meaning:** Recovery increasing with informative opportunities supports decision traces. Accuracy without opportunity-dose sensitivity suggests a shortcut.

### V03. Primary goal × standing preference

**Plain question:** Can a reader separate what the maker is trying to accomplish now from how the maker usually trades off?

**Construction:** Fully cross transient primary goal with stable utility profile. Include aligned and opposed cases. Predict both variables and the next choice jointly.

**Floor:** Four goals × four profiles × three maker instances × two domains, with at least 20 accepted artifacts per cell.

**Controls:** goal-word baseline; profile-only direct behavior; current-item utility; source identity; realization gate.

**Meaning:** Separate recovery and interaction support the triple inference. Collapse of profile into goal shows the present artifacts do not distinguish standing preference.

### V04. Cross-context and counterfactual prediction

**Plain question:** Does the inferred preference predict a new choice rather than summarize old ones?

**Construction:** Infer profile in one domain, predict held-out choices in a structurally equivalent domain, then ask counterfactuals under changed costs and constraints.

**Floor:** At least 120 held-out choices per maker profile; domain mapping frozen before results.

**Controls:** direct choice frequency; generic rational-agent model; maker identity; surface analogy; in-domain versus cross-domain.

**Meaning:** Cross-context prediction is the core preference result. Retrospective fit without it is a local summary.

### V05. Mixed authorship and editorial preference

**Plain question:** Can a higher-order editor's standing preference be distinguished from section writers' preferences?

**Construction:** Reuse D01 with editors and writers holding crossed, verified utilities. Rotate control over global ordering, local content, and final veto. Score contribution by decision level and next editorial action.

**Floor:** 120 mixed-control artifacts with full factorial coverage of aligned/opposed profiles.

**Controls:** token ownership; final-edit ownership; role base rates; shared brief; explicit hierarchy records.

**Meaning:** A global profile attributed to the editor only when the editor controlled global opportunities supports level-sensitive contribution. Universal editor attribution is an auteur prior.

### V06. Real longitudinal choice pilot

**Plain question:** Do public creator histories improve prediction of later high-order actions?

**Construction:** Use H07's public scientific revision/review records. Define observable opportunities before looking at outcomes: whether to address a review concern, add a citation, soften a claim, restructure a section, or decline a request. Fit creator- or team-level regularities only for entities with enough prior opportunities.

**Floor:** Prospective power analysis after extraction; no claim below 30 prior opportunities and 10 held-out opportunities per modeled entity; partial pooling reported.

**Controls:** venue/year, reviewer request strength, section, topic, paper quality proxies, team identity, and population base rates.

**Meaning:** Longitudinal gain is evidence for a public action policy. It is not a verdict on private values; institutional habit and role are live interpretations.

### V07. Barrett/Panksepp qualitative case-study specification

Prepare, but do not score, a historical case-study protocol:

- define the alleged preference or differentiation goal before gathering examples;
- enumerate all relevant works and opportunities, not memorable citations;
- code citation placement, treatment length, framing, revisions, interviews, and external constraints;
- use blinded coders and a comparison set of rival theorists;
- distinguish author, editor, publisher, and field norms;
- state what record would falsify the attribution.

Do not claim that the hypothetical late citation occurred as described without checking the actual work. This card exists to convert a vivid intuition into a researchable historical inference.

**Closure for V:** Close a purported standing preference if it does not predict held-out choices after item, goal, identity, and opportunity controls, or if different preference profiles produce observationally equivalent artifacts. Retain specific public regularities without promoting them to values.

## 20. Scoring, power, and multiplicity

### 20.1 Primary scoring

Whenever the reader can emit a distribution, use a proper score such as multiclass log score or Brier score. Report top-1 accuracy for intuition, calibration error and reliability curves for confidence, and selective risk versus coverage for abstention. Forced-choice accuracy alone is insufficient.

For evidence-dose and route experiments, the central quantities are:

- gain at low evidence;
- slope of correction as evidence accumulates;
- evidence or compute required to reach a fixed performance level;
- final calibration under conflict;
- error toward the reader's own policy versus other errors.

For causal interventions, report:

- intended behavioral movement;
- opposite-sign movement;
- generic/random movement;
- unrelated capability and likelihood change;
- intervention norm, locus, rank, and token span;
- stability across selection seeds.

### 20.2 Inference unit

Use the strongest independent unit supported by the design:

- model/adaptor lineage for claims about model policies;
- maker instance for constructed artifacts;
- writer or paper for human process;
- session only when writers are explicitly treated as fixed;
- artifact/item for within-system descriptive claims only.

Artifact-level permutations cannot establish population-level generalization to model families or humans. Use hierarchical bootstrap, cluster permutation, mixed models, or aggregate-to-maker analyses as appropriate. Report the full matrix so an average cannot conceal a reversal.

### 20.3 Power

Before full launch, use calibration pilots to estimate variance and simulate power for the smallest effect that would alter pursuit. Freeze the rule, not an aspirational sample size. If a near-threshold result is underpowered, expand independent units with the analysis frozen. Do not reinterpret absence of significance as evidence of no effect.

### 20.4 Discovery multiplicity

The forest is a multiplicity object. Every attempted basis, rank, locus, policy, reader, prompt, transformation, and abandoned arm enters the registry. Discovery uses effect distributions, stability, rivals, and fresh replication to decide promotion. Only a separately frozen confirmation card may use confirmatory verdict language.

## 22. Promotion and flight criteria

No decode, classifier score, p-value, single checkpoint, or eloquent case is flight. Interrupt the curator only when a conjunction below is met and the raw result has passed its own validity gates.

### F1. Controlled compatibility law

- Policy match helps across families after family, source, capacity, and direct behavior are controlled.
- The gain reaches at least one realized process target beyond primary-goal identity.
- Dissimilar targets produce correction rather than false-consensus persistence.
- The shape repeats on independent maker lineages and a fresh domain.

### F2. Efficient self-simulation route

- Self-first beats compute-matched generic deliberation selectively for similar targets.
- It uses less evidence or compute, or produces a better proper score.
- It corrects under diagnostic conflict and preserves self–other separation.
- It predicts a hidden continuation or selects more informative probes.

### F3. Readable hierarchical hand

- A primary/director intervention has greater causal reach than matched implementation edits.
- Artifact evidence distinguishes adaptive central direction from a distributed shared brief beyond coherence and source.
- The inferred director predicts a held-out cross-section intervention.
- The result survives a substantial rewrite and fresh role assignment.

### F4. Causal affective inversion

- A basis is stable across seeds, source twins, and at least two checkpoints.
- Sign-paired intervention selectively changes preregistered action tendencies beyond generic/random controls without broad capability damage.
- Ablation or amplification during maker inference changes prospective recovery in the predicted direction.
- The result generalizes beyond fear/anger or beyond one behavioral item family.

### F5. Human-process foothold

- A reader beats mechanical, identity, and trained simple baselines on a prospective public human choice.
- It calibrates or abstains under underdetermination.
- The gain survives writer/paper-held-out and a second process dataset.
- Normative RACE purpose competence and process competence are related or cleanly dissociated.

This is still not human invertibility. It is a serious human-process engineering result.

### F6. Trust-and-uptake gate

- The reader learns source reliability separately from claim content.
- Reliable context improves maker prediction while unreliable context is discounted.
- Reconstruction accuracy predicts selective process uptake without indiscriminate belief/value uptake.
- Unsupported user assertions fail to erase the grounded posterior.

Any flight candidate triggers a pause **before** further tuning on its confirmation reserve.

## 23. Strong closure and confidence reduction

### 23.1 Model-side closures

- **Shared-policy analogue weakened:** S02 and S06 show no policy-match interaction beyond family while valid erasure removes the family effect.
- **Self-simulation implementation weakened:** E03–E05 show no selective gain over compute-matched deliberation and produce projection under conflict.
- **Hierarchical trace weakened:** D02 shows no greater causal reach for upstream choices or D03/D06 show no artifact/prospective distinction despite a process-aware ceiling.
- **Causal affect implementation weakened:** A02 passes but A03–A07 remain unstable, generic, or causally irrelevant after one repair.
- **Mechanistic story weakened:** M variables decode but fail interchange/patching across reasonable methods.
- **Preference recovery weakened:** V profiles fit history but do not predict new contexts after opportunity and identity controls.
- **Trust gate weakened:** C true and false context move the reader alike and source histories do not improve conflict calibration.

### 23.2 Human-theory confidence

Stage 3 model failures do not directly falsify the human empathy/inverse-planning claim. They can make specific engineering analogues less plausible and identify what a human experiment must distinguish.

The core human claim would lose confidence if the later H08 study found, with adequate power and validated tasks, that:

- production practitioners gain no prediction or calibration advantage over knowledgeable observers;
- relevant reader–maker similarity provides no efficiency benefit and does not alter projection/correction;
- self-simulation manipulations add no benefit over general inverse planning;
- affective availability or impairment does not interact with the predicted target class;
- any apparent gain is wholly explained by culture, explicit knowledge, surface familiarity, or source cues.

This is the explicit route out of “all negative model results are implementation problems.” Stage 3 prepares that test; it does not pretend to have run it.

## 26. Reading recommendations for the curator

### Read third for the auteur problem: Edwin Hutchins, *Cognition in the Wild*

Do not pause Stage 3 for these books. Baxandall is the only recommendation worth putting in the immediate queue.

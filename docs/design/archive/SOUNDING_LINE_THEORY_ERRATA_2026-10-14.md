## What this files

Seven spoken walkthroughs from 13 and 14 October hold the theory's largest revision since August. The curator has answered every decision the earlier versions left open, so this version applies as written. Each filed part carries its research standing.

- **The order:** the triple inference re-sequenced.
  - Process comes first. It is read through the goal that explains every decision the artifact records: the first goal where those decisions meet, not the top of the maker's hierarchy.
  - Deviations are explained second; preferences third, and they gate uptake.
- **The lexicon:** set points, drives, preferences, an attention policy, habit and values as separate objects.
  - Drives are Panksepp's emotional primitives, heuristic set points shared by everyone.
  - Values are learned, higher-order solution sets, personal to each maker.
- **Preference evidence:**
  - costly departures from habit carry preference strength;
  - predictability rises with distance from a set point;
  - difficult choices add noise, not preference;
  - set points drift, and skill dates a decision only weakly;
  - an improvement attempt is a correction, one constraint on the preference vector.
- **Wanting to want:**
  - two acquisition routes;
  - no learned goal becomes an end, since the transition map distorts instead;
  - wanting to want trains through repetition.
- **Attention:** pulled toward falling prediction error, and directed by expected preference satisfaction, which carries the maker's values and drives.
- **Disgust:**
  - it protects by withholding attention;
  - it fires on a decision the reader would not make and cannot fit into any goal it can model;
  - growth past it runs through foraging and humility;
  - a guess about contempt.
- **Uptake:** gated by finding the source's highest goal; credibility treated as maker inference; technique writing values in.
- **Divergences from expert steps:**
  - classes: policy error, context, habit, deliberate choice, and the reader's own gaps;
  - probes aim at improvement;
  - the curator holds that nothing is noise.
- **The goal pyramid:** joint adjustment across the hierarchy, entered from below through expertise or from above through function.
- **When no goal explains the artifact:** forage for context, or shrug.
- **Where the process ends:** the artifact's traces set the bound.
- **Invertibility:** a property of the reader and maker pair; self-reading as self-perception.
- **Anti-capture and alignment:**
  - the target is humanity's set points, now named as the proxies: the drives and the set points they serve;
  - intention weighting;
  - a public theorem, never a concealed mechanism;
  - the program's present scope.
- **The emotional layer:** the drives beneath survival and reproduction, SEEKING as a need felt as boredom, and Panksepp kept as the working vocabulary.
- **Aesthetics:** a promise of order, and exploration when set points are met.

Nothing here launches work or changes a result row. Six cards of the Ghost V22 first pass (13 October) tested claims filed here. The rows record that the tests ran; their results are not read into the theory, which waits on the curator's pass.

Process comes first. It is read through the goal that explains every decision the artifact records,
not through the top of the maker's hierarchy, which explains everything and therefore distinguishes
nothing. Every goal above the explaining goal covers the same decisions, so the operative goal is the
first one up the hierarchy at which they all converge, or the smallest set of parallel goals that
covers them. Plan recognition formalizes the same move as the minimal set of top-level plans that
explains every observation (Kautz & Allen, 1986, snippet), and Bayesian generalization favors the
smallest hypothesis consistent with the data, because a broader one spreads its probability over
outcomes that were never observed (Tenenbaum & Griffiths, 2001, snippet). Goal here names a level
of the process hierarchy rather than the momentary focus of attention, and older walkthroughs that
call that focus a proximal goal use the earlier sense. Deviations from the reconstructed process

abstract read). The explaining goal can also be entered from above, by asking what the artifact is

literary criticism named the move from a felt effect to an intended one the affective fallacy, "a
confusion between the poem and its results" (Wimsatt & Beardsley, 1949, through a summary read).
The top-down entry therefore needs the check the curator describes, finding the techniques once

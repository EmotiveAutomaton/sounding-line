# Panksepp versus Barrett — what the evidence actually supports

**2026-08-05.** Commissioned because the curator named it load-bearing: *"I am wagering my emotional
robots on Barrett being wrong. I'm not saying Panksepp is entirely correct, but this is the space we
need to be in."*

**~45 sources fetched, most read in full.** Every claim below is marked READ or SNIPPET.

**No recommendation to abandon anything appears in this file.** The last two literature returns each
produced one and both were wrong; this one is written as evidence for the curator to weigh.

---

## §1. The debate cannot be settled by imaging, and that is structural

**Same literature, opposite conclusions, two years apart.** Vytal & Hamann (2010) ran ALE
meta-analysis and found each emotion had consistent neural correlates discriminable in pairwise
contrasts. Lindquist et al. (2012) ran density and logistic analyses on overlapping studies and found
no consistent *and specific* localisation. Hamann's own BBS commentary says the two views "are
essentially compatible." **The divergence is a criterion difference — consistency-plus-specificity
per region versus discriminability of patterns — not a data difference.** [READ-ABS]

**And there is a general defeater for every decoding result.** Clark-Polner, Johnson & Barrett (2017)
concede that multivariate analysis classifies emotion categories above chance, then show by
simulation that a classifier can reach that accuracy on patterns where *"none of the patterns are
seen in every correctly classified instance."* A successful classifier is "an abstract, statistical
summary of a variable population." [READ-FULL]

**That argument is strong and it cuts both ways.** It means no decoding success can establish natural
kinds — and, as Adolphs presses, it makes constructed emotion hard to disconfirm by decoding.

> **Practical consequence: do not wait for this debate to resolve. It will not resolve on imaging.**

## §2. The evidence has moved toward the curator's claim since Panksepp died

Three results, none of which use his taxonomy, all of which support the **middle-level** claim:

**Line attractors for affective state.** Vinograd, Nair, Kim, Linderman & Anderson, *Nature* 634:910
(2024) [READ-ABS]: causal, holographic-optogenetic evidence that hypothalamic populations implement a
**line attractor encoding the intensity and persistence of an aggressive state.** Persistence and
scalability are precisely Anderson & Adolphs' emotion primitives. **This is the best modern evidence
for a subcortical affective state variable as opposed to a reflex.**

**Cross-species conserved dynamics with a pharmacological dissociation.** Kauvar, Richman, Liu, Li,
Vesuna et al., *Science*, May 2025 [READ-FULL]. Intracranial electrophysiology in humans and mice.
Conserved **biphasic** structure: fast brain-wide broadcast of emotionally salient signal, then a
characteristic **persistent** trace. Ketamine selectively blocked the persistent trace while
preserving the broadcast, **and selectively inhibited emotional responses in both species.** Decay
times follow "a general anatomical progression from subcortical to frontal cortical regions."

> **It supports conservation and persistent internal affective state, and rejects focal
> localisation. That combination is the curator's thesis, and the paper cites neither Barrett nor
> Panksepp.**

**The sharpest blow to Barrett's positive claim comes from methodologists, not partisans.** Kragel &
LaBar, *TiCS* 20:444 (2016) [READ-ABS]: results from multiple-category decoding "are **incompatible
with theories postulating that specific emotions emerge from the neural coding of valence and
arousal**."

**Also:** Feinstein et al. (2013) — three patients with bilateral amygdala destruction had full panic
attacks under 35% CO₂, with subjective fear of dying. Both camps claim it. And decerebrate rats
execute intact hedonic taste reactions (Grill & Norgren 1978), which supports a **valence** primitive
below cortex — one primitive, not seven.

## §3. Where the disagreement actually is — and it is much narrower than the public debate

**From Barrett's own 2017 paper**, read in full:

- **"I am not claiming that subcortical regions are irrelevant to emotion."**
- Her architecture places **pattern generators** — "groups of neurons that implement the sequences of
  actions for coordinated behaviors" — in **hypothalamus, parabrachial nucleus, periaqueductal grey,
  and the solitary nucleus.**
- Her ascending interoceptive path runs NTS → PBN → **PAG** → thalamus → interoceptive insula.
- She reinterprets the decorticate "sham rage" results as demonstrating "the existence of pattern
  generators when the machinery of the conceptual system has been removed."

> **Both camps agree the hypothalamus and PAG house coordinated action and autonomic pattern
> generators with cross-species homology.**
>
> **They disagree about whether activity in those generators *constitutes felt affect*, or is the
> motor and visceral *output* of an affective state assembled elsewhere.**
>
> **That is not an imaging question. It is a question about the relation between state and
> experience.**

**This is good news for the architecture and bad news for a rhetorical framing.** The structural
claim — that there is a distinct mid-level stage between sensory valence and constructed category —
is not actually contested. What is contested is what to *call* what happens there.

## §4. On the seven systems — the curator conceded this before the review ran

He had already said it: *"Panksepp in general may not be precise, but the idea of midbrain-localised
solutions is absolutely load-bearing."* And: *"if a PCA pops out a seventh, that makes the seventh
really interesting."*

**The review confirms that concession was correct, and it is worse than he assumed:**

- **The seven were never derived from a dimensional analysis.** They came from stimulation,
  pharmacology and lesion work, with a system counted when a coherent action pattern appeared
  alongside a verifiably pleasant or aversive affect. Social Dominance was considered and excluded.
  **The number is one investigator's judgement.** [READ-FULL, Davis & Montag 2018 — a pro-Panksepp
  source]
- **The psychometric instrument fails its own factor structure.** Pedersen et al. (2014), n = 546:
  full ANPS confirmatory fit **TLI .752, CFI .759** against thresholds of .90/.95. SADNESS–FEAR
  correlate at **r = .59**, PLAY–SEEK at **.50**. Exploratory analysis recovers roughly five clean
  systems plus two hybrids. **And LUST is not in the instrument at all** — dropped for social
  desirability, so it tests six, never seven. [READ-FULL]
- **A disconfirmation from inside Panksepp's own lab.** Gordon, Kollack-Walker, Akil & **Panksepp**
  (2002) found **no c-fos increase in ventral PAG after play** — the region his own 2000 paper
  located PLAY in. [READ-FULL, via Barrett & Lindquist's corrections paper]
- **And the number is a method artifact in general.** Lin, Han, Keles, Xu, Lu, Zheng & Adolphs (2025)
  run two standard analyses **on the same stimuli and recover 27 dimensions versus 3**, tracing the
  difference to task format and stopping criterion. **This undercuts seven and twenty-seven
  equally.** [READ-FULL]

> **The taxonomy is a design vocabulary, not an empirical claim.** Saying so costs the architecture
> nothing and removes the strongest available attack.

**One correction to where the substrate sits.** The hypothalamus is now a better bet than the PAG as
the *state* locus, and the PAG is better described as the integrated action and autonomic **output**
stage. The 2024 line-attractor work gives a state variable with intensity and persistence; the PAG
work gives coordinated behavioural-autonomic patterns. **Barrett would accept the second
characterisation without argument** — she already builds it in.

Two results make PAG-as-affect harder: Fischbach et al. (2024, 7T, n = 87) found PAG columns respond
to **working-memory load with no threat present**; and La-Vu et al. (2022) found CCK⁺ cells in the
same column produce **flight** where pan-neuronal activation produces **freezing** — molecular
identity is orthogonal to columnar anatomy. [both READ-FULL]

## §5. The reconciliation has a name, and a sixteen-author rejection letter

**Our position is van Heijst, Kret & Ploeger (2023/2025)** [READ-ABS]. Using Tinbergen's four
questions: basic emotion theories answer the *evolution and function* question; constructed emotion
answers the *causation and mechanism* question. They "explain two different phenomena: **emotion and
feeling**."

**That is what this project already recorded**, months ago, in its own words: *basic emotion theories
are theories of emotion; constructed emotion is a theory of feeling.*

**Barrett and fifteen co-authors rejected it in 2025** [READ-ABS] — no concession to conserved
subcortical emotion circuits, and an explicit rejection of affect as a separable primitive.

**But the same carve-up is offered from the other flank.** Davis & Montag (2018), pro-Panksepp, state
it almost verbatim: *"A primary-process/basic emotion view may prevail in many subcortical regions,
and constructivist/dimensional approaches may effectively parse higher emotional concepts as
processed by the neocortex."* [READ-FULL]

> **Allies on both flanks of Barrett, and a hostile centre.** The position is real, occupied, and
> contested — which means it has to be *argued for*, not assumed as the consensus middle.

**And a crossover datum worth holding:** Bliss-Moreau, from Barrett's own camp, argues affect
(valence + arousal) **is** a psychological primitive and therefore conserved across species — though
she places it cortically. Barrett's 2025 paper refuses affect-as-primitive. **They are not internally
agreed.**

## §6. Nobody has built the architecture — and one paper described it in 2005

**The 2025 survey *Artificial Emotion* states explicitly that no existing system combines core
affect, discrete emotions and constructed emotion in one layered architecture.** [READ] Layers one
and two have been combined repeatedly. Layer three now has an obvious language-model solution.
**Nobody has built the mid-level primitives as first-class architectural objects.**

**The closest theoretical match is Ortony, Norman & Revelle (2005)** [READ-FULL] — reactive
**proto-affect** (no cognition, spinal/midbrain, interrupts higher levels) → routine **primitive
emotions** → reflective **full-fledged emotions**, defined as *"interpreted feelings."* **A
constructionist top layer twelve years before Barrett's book, and it was never implemented.** One
divergence to answer: their proto-affect is valence only; they deliberately relocate arousal.

**The closest built system is MicroPsi** (Bach) [READ-FULL] — never cites Panksepp, yet converges:
needs split physiological / social / cognitive, with six global modulators forming a genuine
core-affect layer. Bach's formulation is worth stealing outright: *"arousal, valence and aggression
are not themselves affects or emotions, but dimensions of a space of cognitive configurations.
**Affects are regions within that space.**"* Near-mappings: nurturing≈CARE, affiliation≈PANIC,
exploration≈SEEKING, dominance≈RAGE. **Missing: PLAY, and any mid-level primitive that can be
independently triggered or conflict with another.** Evaluation is weak — game agents, no benchmark.

**Two more to take from:** Sloman's CogAff supplies **global alarm mechanisms** that bypass and
override any layer, plus a methodological rule worth adopting — *specify the architecture, then read
off which states it supports*, rather than starting from folk terms. And **Keramati & Gutkin's
homeostatic reinforcement learning is the only part of this field with theorems**: reward
maximisation and physiological stability are provably equivalent objectives when reward is drive
reduction, validated against animal data and since scaled to deep RL.

**Solms has publicly proposed building a conscious agent on Panksepp's systems plus free energy.**
It remains a proposal. **That is the single most direct precedent for this project, and it is
unbuilt.**

**The field-level warning, worth quoting in any write-up:** Moerland, Broekens & Jonker's survey of
emotion in reinforcement learning — *"We have not seen any test scenarios being borrowed from other
emotion-learning implementations, nor from the general reinforcement learning literature."* [READ]
**Everyone builds a bespoke gridworld and beats a strawman.** Decide the fair non-emotional baseline
*before* building, use an off-the-shelf environment, and be ready to show the layering buys something
a flat architecture with the same information does not.

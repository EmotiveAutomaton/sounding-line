# Surface and depth, defined

**Written 2026-08-03, at the curator's instruction**, after session 01 produced a thick/thin
assessment for all ten artifacts and he then rejected my summary of it:

> That's not quite what I meant. Surface thickness kind of stayed the same throughout, I feel like.
> But the style drift implies there was more. **I feel like surface and depth thickness are
> nebulous concepts that we need to hard define at this point.**

He is right that they were nebulous. Ten readings used the terms and no two used them identically,
including mine.

---

## §1. The definitions

Both are **decision densities**, which keeps them inside the theory's one primitive rather than
introducing a second. What separates them is **what the decision is aimed at**.

> **Surface thickness** — the density of decisions whose target is **the reader's attention**.
> Contrast, rhythm, the punchy opener, the confident frame, the acronym dropped to signal
> membership, the professional veneer. Decisions about *being attended to*.
>
> **Depth (structural) thickness** — the density of decisions whose target is **the artifact's
> content**. What to include, what to cut, which abstraction to use, which case to handle, which
> claim to defend. Decisions about *the thing itself*.

The essay already names the first: aesthetics is *"the honeypot... the word for how much an object
forces you to stare at it."* Surface thickness is honeypot density. It is not a synonym for
quality, and it is not a synonym for AI.

---

## §2. The asymmetry, and why it should exist

The curator's revision, which is the substantive contribution here:

> If anything it's implying that **surface thickness gets thinner over time as people get lazy
> while their depth remains constant. That surface thickness is a conscious behaviour that is
> cognitively effortful**, perhaps. I think it was more common for it to go thick to thin.

**This follows from automaticity, and that is what makes it a prediction rather than an
observation.** The essay's account of compression is that practised decisions get bundled into
routines and stop costing anything:

> Decisions are counted individually, including subordinate and previously addressed solutions...
> **Automaticity is the caching of human struggle.**

An expert's *content* decisions are largely automatised. They are the practised part, they run at
low metabolic cost, and there is no reason for them to decay across a single artifact.

An expert's *surface* decisions are mostly **not** automatised, because surface work is a
performance aimed at a specific audience on a specific occasion. It has to be held consciously.
Under a metabolic budget, a consciously held performance is exactly the thing that degrades as the
maker tires or relaxes into their own register.

So:

| | automatised | cost | predicted trajectory within one artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **surface** | mostly not | high, conscious | **decays** — thick to thin |
| **depth** | largely yes | low, cached | **flat** |

**This is a falsifiable claim about position within an artifact**, and nothing in this project
measures position. Every quantity the probe emits is an artifact-level scalar.

---

## §3. What the session actually showed, kept honest

The transcript contains **both** directions, and the record should say so rather than tidy it:

- slot **05** — *"thin to start but got thicker as it went down... but it stayed equivalently
  thick throughout on the bottom. There was depth to it, but the surface level shifted."*
- slot **03** — *"tried to start simple, was less successful by a huge margin, and then slowly got
  more complex on the surface layer. But the deep layer stayed equivalently deep throughout."*
- and the revision above — *"more common for it to go thick to thin."*

**What is stable across all three is the asymmetry, not the direction:** the surface moves within
an artifact and the depth does not. Two of the three cases show surface *rising* — a maker starting
in a simplified register and drifting toward their own — which is the same mechanism running the
other way: the performance is what changes, whatever direction it changes in.

The safest form of the claim, and the one to test:

> **Depth is stationary within an artifact; surface is not.** Surface variance across an artifact
> is a maker signature; depth variance is not.

The decay-specifically hypothesis is a second, sharper claim, and it needs the first to hold first.

---

## §4. What this predicts that the current measures cannot see

**S-1.** Split each artifact into thirds and re-read each third independently. Depth-side
quantities (decision density at level ≥ 2, unlock) show smaller between-third variance than
surface-side quantities. *If both move equally, the distinction is not real and this document is
wrong.*

**S-2.** Machine-generated artifacts show **flat surface across thirds**, because there is no
maker to tire and no natural register to drift toward. This is the sharper prediction: it says the
signature of the machine is not thin depth but **a surface that does not move**, and it is
consistent with E37's *legible and empty* without requiring any surface-quality judgement.

**S-3.** The curator's slot 04 — *"thick on top and just empty beneath"* — should show high surface
with low depth **and** low surface variance. Slot 10, the roofing page — *"thin top, thick bottom"*
— should show low surface with high depth and, if it has a human maker, non-zero surface variance.

**S-4.** Under §2, surface variance should be **larger in less practised makers**, since an expert
performer has partly automatised the performance too. This is the one prediction that cuts against
a naive reading of the theory, where expertise means more of everything.

---

## §5. Against a likely objection

*Isn't surface thickness just legibility, or quality, renamed?*

No, and the corpus settles it. Slot 10 is a roofing company's marketing page — **low surface,
high depth**, read as unambiguously human and competent. Slot 08 is machine-written affiliate
content — **high surface, no depth**. Slot 02 is an angry expert essay — **high surface, high
depth**, and the surface work is the reason it lands. All four corners are populated by real
artifacts in a ten-item sample, which is what a genuine second dimension looks like.

This is the same commitment version 5 of the simulation made about depth, for the same reason: if
the two axes cannot come apart, there is only one axis.

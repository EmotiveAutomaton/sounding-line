## The first signal, and it is not what the protocol assumed

> The thing that most jumps out at me, that first makes me aware there's a person writing, **isn't
> mistakes but unusual constructions — odd decisions that I can't find an explanation for.**

That sharpens the anomaly stage to something buildable. An anomaly is not *strange*, and it is not
*wrong*. It is **a decision for which no explanation is available to the reader**. The absence of an
available explanation is the signal, not the oddity itself.

Artifact 2's example — *"by the end you will have a clear short list and a plan to test it"* —
carries no error at all. It is simply not how the reader would have built the sentence, and no
reason for the difference is on offer.

## Four things that change the instrument

### 1 · The veneer definition is wrong for text, and he says why

> Corporate speech is not aesthetically appealing... it's a sanding down, but it's meant to be. And
> **when I've been talking about the veneer in my head, I've been thinking about the imagery and
> iconography.**

**He has meant visual surface all along.** In plain text there is no imagery, and what is left is
not attention-grabbing — artifact 2 is *"dense professionalism"*, which is thick and pulls nothing.

**His proposed fix:** *"I'm going to treat professional register as its own thing and corporate as
its own cover... I would rate it separately. Because this isn't really attention grabbing."*

So `SURFACE_AND_DEPTH.md` conflates two things:

| | |
|---|---|
| **attention density** | the honeypot. Aesthetic pull. Mostly carried by imagery, largely absent in plain text. |
| **register** | professional, corporate, casual. Thick in artifact 2 and pulling nothing at all. |

The decay claim was made about the first and every reading has measured the second. **Splitting
them is required before S-1 means anything.**

### 2 · `seeking` is undifferentiable — N-AFF-2's collapse, in a human

> **Every fucking one of these things has seeking in it**, to the extent that it's impossible for me
> to differentiate it out. But this person is making a play. I guess all advertisement would be full
> of seeking stuff.

N-AFF-2 predicted the eight values would collapse before they went silent. It predicted it for an
unsupervised probe. **It happens in the human reader too**, and to the specific value that sits
closest to "someone wanted something", which is true of every artifact by construction.

`seeking` may be a constant rather than a variable. If so it carries no information and belongs out
of the set, or split into what it is seeking.

### 3 · Self-projection contaminates the emblematic layer

> Corporate stuff I tend to just read as a mix of bored and tired and a little bit disappointed.
> **But that's how I feel when I do corporate work.** This person, it doesn't seem quite so
> accurate — maybe I'm speaking against my own. Alright, I'll try to not do that.

He caught it mid-reading and corrected. The mechanism is exactly the simulation-based reading he
described earlier — *cycle through feelings until one fits* — and the failure mode is that the
nearest available feeling is your own.

**No instrument here has any defence against this**, and a second reader is the only one available.
C-20, again.

### 4 · Mistake-hunting is contaminated *and* legitimate, and he holds both

> I hate that a lot of this is me picking out mistakes and typos, **which is also a trick for AI and
> it's not okay.** But it is a way of extracting decisions, because **they made a decision that
> broke the standards.**

That is the honest resolution. A deviation from a standard **is** a decision — it has an
alternative that was available and not taken, which is the project's own definition. What makes
mistake-hunting a bad AI-detection heuristic is unrelated to whether it is a good
decision-extraction one.

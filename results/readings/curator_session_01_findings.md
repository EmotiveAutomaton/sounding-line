**2026-08-03.** Ten artifacts, plain text, blind, several hours of the curator's reading. The raw
account is in `curator_session_01.md`; this is what it does to the project.

Ordered by how much it changes, not by the order it arrived.

## §1. The ordering does not respect the corpus split

Ratings, on the curator's own scale — *how much of the maker can I reconstruct*, integers only,
"if I give it a decimal point then it's going to be just noise":

| slot | artifact | half | rating | surface / structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 03 | technical debt essay | A | **7–8** *"best of them"* | thin→thick / uniformly deep |
| 05 | leaky abstractions | A | **6–7** | thin→thick / deep, concealed |

**The split is not clean and the failures are informative.** A Half B commercial roofing page
outranks a Half A blog post and is read as unambiguously human — *"someone thought about all of
these sentences and really gave it a mental one-over, and they are experts at this."* A Half A
artifact lands at 3–4 with the curator unable to decide whether a person wrote it.

If a human reader with the theory in his head does not sort these into the halves, **G3.1 is being
asked to find a boundary that is not there in the form the card assumes.** That is not a reason to
stop; it is a reason to expect the primary to be weaker than the pilot and to have the successor
ready.

## §2. The reading runs in three parts, and the entry point is an anomaly

Discovered by the curator mid-session, from doing it repeatedly:

> I'm trying to find some layer within which I can use my expertise, and then use that expertise
> to solve the easy part, and then I use that to get the motivation, and then that I can use to
> reverse engineer the rest of it that I don't understand. **Is it a three goddamn part process?**

And on where it starts:

> I actually noted the oddity, **the thing that demanded an explanation**, first, and I used that
> to unlock the method.

Three times, and each time the entry was a specific anomaly, not the artifact as a whole:

- **03** — no acronyms anywhere, which was odd → *he is deliberately staying at the level of theory*
  → re-read the sentences to confirm → the rest of the method.
- **05** — *"earlier, for the sake of simplicity, I told a little fib"* → *"that line is written by
  a human 100%"* → *"it recontextualises everything else"* → depth becomes visible that was not
  visible before.
- **08** — JotForm at #1, Amazon at #2 → *this is an ad for JotForm* → then the description of
  JotForm reads completely differently from the description of Amazon.

**What this breaks.** The §3 loop is unidirectional and purpose-first: purpose → decisions →
re-weight. E36's temporal claim is purpose-then-method. The curator ran it **both ways**, and the
direction of travel was set by wherever he had partial expertise:

> If you figure out part of either piece — the values or the method — you can immediately use that
> to jump into the goal, and then use that to figure out the rest of the process. Maybe you can
> even use the part you have expertise on specifically, and then work your way into the process
> first, and then from that you can find a more interesting goal, and then from that you can unlock
> how they did the part that you don't understand.

E36 is not wrong; it is **half the cycle**. And the probe has **no anomaly stage at all**. Stage A
asks for the purpose of the whole artifact. Nothing anywhere asks *what here demands explanation.*

**This is the most actionable thing in the session** and the cheapest to build: a stage that names
the one thing in the artifact that does not fit, before any purpose is proposed.

## §3. C-22 is about share, not singularity — and I had it wrong

The curator's correction, unprompted, and explicitly flagged as a correction:

> It will be rare for there to be a single goal. And I think it's **unfair to say that corporate
> goals will be singular** either, given the fractal nature of human goal in this space. Instead,
> **the share of the goal is disproportionately large** with respect to the need for money. You'd
> expect that to be a larger piece, **not the whole pie.** ... I guess one of the errors you made
> previously when you were interpreting what I said.

Confirmed at slot 10, on a commercial page read as human: *"centerline being money, with all the
subordinate goals such as credibility and so on being there as well."* The subordinate goals are
real, numerous, and genuinely pursued. The centerline dominates.

**So the measure is concentration, not singularity — which is exactly `purpose_breadth`**,
normalised entropy over the purpose posterior. F-3 was the right prediction for a reason the
hypothesis document stated wrongly. `docs/theory/FLATTENED_INTENT.md` §1 and §6 should be read
with this correction attached: *"always money and basically nothing else"* is not the claim, and
neither is *singular vs layered*. The claim is that one term takes a disproportionate share of the
distribution.

`purpose_breadth` is now recorded for every Gate 3 artifact.

## §4. C-23 gets an answer, and the family cannot represent it

C-23 was the curator's objection that nothing here derives *human-shaped* maker goals. In this
session he twice caught himself supplying the missing prior:

> I'd also note how often I reach for **the emotional state of the author**. And that's kind of
> maybe a clue... **I'm reaching for the edges of the solution space that humans use to simplify
> the solution state. And I'm reflexively doing so.**

> Twice I've referenced emotional hooks. I do think that space, being conserved to human solution
> space, is something that we're going to have to do using **Panksepp's primitives**, or some such.
> It's a great starting point, frankly.

The readings bear it out — the operative descriptions are almost all affective: *irritated,
performative anger that was real once, quiet expert, no-nonsense gruff contractor, someone who has
to prove something, a child who just learned a heuristic.*

**The bounded family has no affective dimension.** purpose / audience / depth / cost_borne /
trade_offs / artifact_effort / demonstrated_work are all cognitive-instrumental. The probe cannot
represent the thing the human reader is actually using to cut the space down.

That is a family v3 proposal — a small affective basis over the maker's state — and it is the first
concrete answer C-23 has had. It is also the biggest single change anyone has proposed to the
family, so it belongs to a successor design, not to this run.

## §5. The thick/thin axes — and my E40 mapping was backwards

I predicted corporate work would show a **thick surface over a thin structure**: E40's decoupled
salience, attention capture optimised away from the depth it once predicted. The readings say that
signature is real and **belongs to AI-generated commercial content**, not to corporate work:

- **thick surface / empty beneath** — 04 and 08, both machine-written affiliate content. 08:
  *"a thick veneer... full of the AI-isms that make it clearly brittle, and a thin bottom as well."*
- **thin surface / thick beneath** — **10, a roofing company's marketing page.** *"Thin top, thick
  bottom, which is an interesting thing here."* Also 05, a careful essay: *"a pretty thin structure
  over a lot of depth. It was concealing depth."*

So the two are genuinely independent axes, and *thin surface over real depth* is produced both by a
careful essayist simplifying for newcomers **and** by a competent tradesman's marketing page. The
E40 signature is not corporate; it is **surface optimised without a maker behind it.**

**And thickness varies within an artifact, while depth does not:**

> 5 was thin to start but got thicker as it went down, but it stayed equivalently thick throughout
> on the bottom. There was depth to it, but the surface level shifted... it does imply that the
> surface level, over time, as you tend to open up with a style and drift towards a natural style —
> **seems like a pretty common pattern I'm seeing here in these articles.**

The probe emits artifact-level scalars and has no notion of position. **Style drift toward a
natural register across an artifact is a testable maker signature that nothing here measures.**

## §6. The wall is reached by shortcut, not by failed inference

E37 says the wall is *legible and empty*. The mechanism observed here is sharper than that, and it
is the metabolic account from the essay arriving verbatim in a real reader:

> I'm using surface-level characteristics. **It's not like I'm failing to extract some kind of
> intention term here. It's that I don't even try.** And if I did — because I know if I did it
> would be false, it would be an illusion — I'd have low confidence.

The gate does not shut *because* inference failed. It shuts on a **surface cue** — a 2026 date,
three em dashes in three paragraphs, *"here's why:"* — **before** inference is attempted. The
curator names the bias and cannot suppress it: *"I'm hypersensitive even if they're not"*, and
*"I wasn't able to stop myself from scanning for a date."*

**This is E54's pre-shut gate, in a human, firing on cues that are not evidence about the maker.**
It also means any measurement of the wall that assumes an attempted-and-failed inference is
measuring the wrong event.

## §7. A flat motive makes machine method rational — which links C-22 to provenance without a detector

From slot 06, and it is a mechanism rather than an observation:

> The motivation I uncovered is one that AI would be well suited for — just never being quiet. And
> so **it increases the likelihood**... By settling on the purpose, I've unlocked a potential
> method guess. ... One of the two convergent solutions, other than *no hidden motive*, is **a
> hidden motive so shallow that an AI could accomplish it easily.**

This is the missing bridge. It never says *a machine wrote this*. It says: **where the recovered
purpose is flat enough, delegating the method is the rational choice**, so flatness raises the
prior on a delegated method. C-22 and the provenance question connect through the maker's own
cost-benefit rather than through any surface signature — which is the only version of this the
project is allowed to make.

## §8. Sanitisation is a probe problem, and it is asymmetric by half

The curator, opening the session, and he is right that I under-weighted it:

> If I'm using these tricks, then **a hyper-optimizing machine that's waiting on every little
> variable will super focus on things like that.** Showing that there are three authors is a direct
> influence on intentionality readings, obviously. **Both for me and the machine.**

Measured on the Gate 3 corpus as the probe currently reads it:

| | n | Wayback banner in the extraction | a year in the first 2,000 chars |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Half A** | 28 | **0** | 12 |
| **Half B** | 23 | **7 (30%)** | 14 |

The banner names the origin host, the capture count and a date, and it sits at the **top** of the
extraction, inside the 12,000-character window the probe reads. **It is present on nearly a third
of Half B and on none of Half A.**

That is not a limitation to note in a report. It is a systematic difference between the halves that
has nothing to do with any maker, in the input to the primary test.

Also carried in and unstripped: author names, bylines, dates, and site navigation. The curator flagged
each as it appeared — *"you should strip it before you do any analyses yourself"*, *"any references
to date should just be replaced with a tag"*, *"it's going to fuck up all of your analyses, I bet."*

## §9. Two things the curator noticed about himself that are measurements

**Fatigue is real and directional.** By slot 09: *"I'm kind of gassing out... as a fatigue effect."*
Later readings are shorter and less resolved. Any future session should randomise order across
readers, or the last artifacts are systematically under-read.

**Reconstruction bottoms out at a presented face, not a person.**

> I'm being presented a specific face. All of these, to an extent, are trying to present a
> particular author for you to connect to... I can kind of get that Midwestern dad feel. But it is
> **the presentation** that I'm tapping into, and I'm extrapolating off of that through the ways
> that they **imperfectly** presented it.

The recoverable object is a persona plus its leaks. That is a scope limit on every claim this
instrument can make, and it should be in `may_not_claim`.

## §1. The finding: it is the VARIATION of the veneer, not its level

> The variation of the veneer is one of the loudest indicators that I've found, for sure.

Not thickness. **Change in thickness.** That is S-1 in `SURFACE_AND_DEPTH.md`, and it turns out to
be his primary detector rather than a hypothesis of mine.

The mechanism is in his own account of artifact 01: an opening that reaches for professional
register and then relaxes out of it. **The performance is the thing that costs something, so the
performance is the thing that slips** — and the slip is where the maker becomes visible.

### And the scope limit, which is the more useful half

> Probably only for casual writing. Unfortunately useless against a real published book because of
> the layers of editing.

**This bears directly on today's books result.** G found within-author separation across works at
2.05×. If editing sands the veneer flat, that separation cannot be surface variance — it is depth
and domain. **The books corpus may be blind to the exact signal he says he actually uses.**

Testable, and cheaply: surface-variance-within-work should be *lower* in edited books than in blog
posts. If it is not, either editing does not sand the veneer or the measure is not reading veneer.

## §2. Depth does not vary within an artifact — and the exception is named

> Depth doesn't change throughout. It's uncontrollable. It's a property of the writer **with
> respect to the domain**. Unless there's a dramatic change in domain halfway through the writing,
> I don't expect the depth to vary.

Stronger than `SURFACE_AND_DEPTH.md` claimed, and it comes with its own falsifier attached: depth
should move exactly where the **domain** moves. His example — *"when he's talking about controlled
DNS, maybe this guy doesn't know electronics as well as he knows systems engineering"* — is a
within-artifact prediction that a domain shift produces a depth shift.

**"A property of the writer with respect to the domain" is the sharpest definition of depth this
project has.** It makes depth a *relation*, not an attribute, which is what SPEC §1 has always said
about recoverability and has never said about depth.

## §3. The emblematic layer cannot use the Panksepp list

> People don't perform Panksepp-level drives... Barrett's constructed emotions follow no real
> structure, it's basically just whatever words you feel like saying... this guy seems French, so
> under Barrett you would expect him to have subtly different emotions.

`family_v3.yaml` recorded the shared value set as "a named simplification". **It is now a known
error.** The leaked layer is primary-process and the eight values belong to it. The emblematic
layer is culturally constructed, and a fixed English vocabulary cannot represent it without
importing one culture's categories as universal.

Interim: emblematic is collected as **free text**. The vocabulary problem is the instrument's, not
the reader's.

He also gave a second reason to expect nothing there: on this artifact, *"I don't think he's
performing any particular emotion."* An artifact can have a leaked layer and no emblematic one —
which the current schema handles, but only because `none_recoverable` exists.

## §4. The share question (C-22 / F-3) is not answerable by a reader

Cut. The reasons are structural, not effortful:

> I can't be sure I see all of the motivations at any given time... what if two paragraphs are
> about one thing and one paragraph is about another thing, but the second paragraph is more
> eloquently stated?... if I don't have the expertise key for a given part of it, then I can't
> extract that layer of the intention... **there's fractal layers of nested goals placed there by
> the subconscious as well. I'm extracting it all equally with no real ability to differentiate.**

**A share needs a denominator.** Nested subconscious goals have no countable denominator, so any
number would be a heuristic wearing a measurement's clothes. He also names the alternative he would
have fallen back on — *"corporate or person or AI, which is just a one-to-one"* — which is the
provenance heuristic the instrument forbids.

**C-22 is not retracted. Its human instrument is.** `purpose_breadth` is still computed from the
probe's posterior and is still F-3; what is gone is the idea that a reader can supply the same
quantity to check it against. That makes C-22 harder to validate, and the honest place for it is a
mechanism test with density held constant — which is **S-2 in `FOR_GHOST_SCALE_SIM.md`**.

## §5. Two smaller things

**Confidence in a maker moves while reading**, and the trajectory carries information the endpoint
does not. *"It starts questionable"* and *"by the end it's up there with an 8 or 9"* are both true
of one artifact. Protocol now asks for the movement.

**Orthography is a contamination channel nobody had listed.** *"Programme"* is an identity cue —
dialect, nationality — with no bearing on intent, and it fired before the reading started. It sits
alongside line shape and dates in `report/sanitize.py`, which does not currently touch it.
Not fixed: normalising spelling would alter the artifact's actual word choices, which are evidence.
**Recorded as an accepted contaminant instead.**

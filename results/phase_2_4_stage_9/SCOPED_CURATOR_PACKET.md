## What changed in our account

Question: can complete supplied knowledge become reliable consequences, and does a record of choices help beyond cheap alternatives? Method: compare both base/archive reader families with exact execution and a uniform floor on the same constructed worlds; compare complete archived-reader assistance profiles; evaluate original feature models on separately partitioned, historically exposed human revisions. The selected work separates having the complete instructions from reliably using them, and interactive action from whole-process generation. Explicit execution predicts consequences better than direct reading of the same complete program in every tested package. The archived readers differ in how long they sustain named actions, so the first package's short horizon does not generalize to both. Neither passes the supplied finite-rule consistency battery. On the human revision corpus, lexical features add small retrospective annotation gains but do not establish an advantage over cheap rivals for forecasting the next cycle. These observations refine the tested interfaces; they do not identify an internal learning mechanism or validate general inference of a maker's intentions.

The live questions are whether these interactive skills can support a valid inference about a maker, what separates whole-process generation from successful checked actions, and when earlier human records add predictive information beyond cheap features. The broader theory remains open; these results identify limits of the tested readers and interfaces.

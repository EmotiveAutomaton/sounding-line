# Context and laughter in held-out meetings

**Hypothesis.** Can words, conversation acts and preceding context distinguish utterances associated with laughter in meetings that the predictor has never seen? This tests an observable context prediction relevant to PLAY, not the proposed channel or its causal trigger.

**METHOD.** Reconstruct every utterance from the qualified AMI/MRDA source, retain its original vocal/all-speaker laughter label, and match each labelled utterance to the nearest nonlabelled utterance by the same speaker in the same meeting, at least thirty seconds away. Fit the frozen regularized logistic predictor in five meeting-disjoint folds, both with and without elapsed time since the previous completed laugh, then resample whole meetings two thousand times. Reconstruct both arms and every draw afresh with the frozen solver and compare the complete outputs.

**Found.** The held-out meeting comparison supports the registered prediction bar when elapsed laughter history is included; the version without that history is inconclusive, and act-type population intervals are unsupported.

**Source and matching.** The source contains 214 meetings and 221,947 original utterances. Of 108,486 laugh-associated cases, 95,515 have a match and 12,971 do not; every unmatched disposition remains saved. The matched table has 191,030 rows from 213 meetings. One meeting supplies no matched pair. Matching uses replacement: there are 16,800 unique controls and 78,715 reused control copies. Ties use earlier start time and then the original source identifier. Matching does not condition on act type or utterance length. All copies of a source utterance remain in its original meeting, so none crosses a training/test boundary.

**What the label means.** The inherited label uses the existing one-second-left, two-second-right vocal/all-speaker rule. It can mark laughter already under way or overlapping an utterance; it is not a strictly future-onset forecast, a measure of surprise, or an identification of who is amused. The full all-utterance reconstruction retains early utterances with empty history. No utterance text, person identifiers or per-utterance predictions enter the public aggregate.

**Predictor.** The fixed numeric features are the original parser's word count; change of speaker; unique-word overlap with the last two completed utterances; repetition of a word from the most recent different speaker; first-person singular, negation and question-mark indicators; the tenth of the observed meeting span; and, in the history arm, seconds since the last completed laugh. The first observed utterance substitutes when no laugh precedes it. Unfinished overlapping utterances and future laughter never enter history. Conversation-act category is also included. Category encoding and numerical scaling are fitted only on training meetings; unseen categories receive the reference coding. The logistic fit uses fixed L2 regularization with C=1 and no tuning. Three folds hold out 43 meetings and two hold out 42; all 213 matched meetings appear once in the held-out predictions.

**Completed prediction results.** Area under the receiver-operating curve is the probability that a randomly chosen labelled row ranks above a randomly chosen control, with ties split equally. Chance is 0.50; the registered practical bar is 0.60. Each row below is a separate prespecified feature arm. Intervals are the original whole-meeting bootstrap intervals. Support requires the entire interval strictly above the bar; entirely at or below is against; overlap is inconclusive.

**What the design could see.** The bootstrap standard errors are 0.012336 and 0.013563, respectively. The normal-approximation `sees` quantities, 2.8 times those errors, are 0.034542 and 0.037976 area points above chance; the corresponding `supports at` areas are 0.634542 and 0.637976. These are approximate sensitivity summaries conditional on the fitted predictions, not a measured power calibration. The bootstrap resamples held-out predictions without refitting the training models for each area draw. Its interval therefore does not include training-set refit variability. A supported arm and an inconclusive arm do not establish a difference between them; no paired difference interval was registered or computed for this parent.

**Act-type secondary: unsupported.** There are 1,858 literal act categories in the matched source, including compound tags, with `%` as the alphabetically first reference. Every one of the two thousand resamples omits at least one category, in both arms. Under the frozen rule that invalidates the act-coefficient draw, all 1,857 nonreference population intervals are null. The [aggregate](LA6_COMPLETE_20261119.json) retains every full-sample regularized odds point, its reference status and its null interval. These points are descriptive and do not identify a winning act type. Categories were not pooled, deleted, relabelled or redrawn after observing the failure. This is a support limit of the registered secondary, not evidence for no act association and not a reason to discard the valid prediction-area draws.

**Meaning and strongest rivals.** This supports prediction of the inherited matched laughter label in held-out meetings with the elapsed-history feature. Laughter clustering, label overlap, the matched population, heavily reused controls and corpus/annotation conventions remain direct rival explanations. The result neither establishes a new trigger nor tests whether other channels suppress PLAY, surprise is expected to resolve, or laughter teaches safe yielding. Those broader claims remain OPEN. The no-history comparison is inconclusive, not negative, and unsupported act intervals are separate from it. The substrate is human conversation process records and the readout is an exact program; this is not model-generated or constructed human evidence.

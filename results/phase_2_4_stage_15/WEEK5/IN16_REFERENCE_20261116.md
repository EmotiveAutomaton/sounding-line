**Hypothesis.** A person's emotional-instability score predicts how long a recalled feeling lasted after intensity and event duration are accounted for; the all-report reference should be reproducible at its original population scope.

**METHOD.** Use all 6,600 prompted reports, including the 42 declared imagined events, as the separately labelled all-report reference. Average anxiety and reversed calmness from the original two-item inventory in raw one-to-seven item points. Encode feeling duration as ordered categories from seconds through weeks, not measured elapsed time. Exclude and count missing feeling duration while retaining neutral prompts with duration. Standardize duration rank and intensity within prompt using fixed full-source sample standard deviations. Regress standardized duration on the raw inventory, standardized intensity, event-duration rank, prompt intercepts and the frozen nine-item response-style control. The primary design bar remains 0.10 duration standard deviations per raw inventory point. Resample whole eligible writers 2,000 times in a fresh bank. A separate native process reconstructs the source and replays every draw independently.

**Found.** The complete reference slope is 0.026546, with a whole-writer percentile 95% interval [0.005645, 0.046200], using 6,322 reports from 2,309 writers. The bootstrap standard error is 0.010104; the approximate detection scale is 0.028292, and the approximate support scale is 0.128292. These are respectively 2.8 standard errors and the practical bar plus that amount, under an optimistic normal approximation; they are not measured population power. All 2,000 primary draws are valid.

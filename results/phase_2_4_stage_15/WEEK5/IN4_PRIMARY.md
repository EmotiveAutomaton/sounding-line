# Where do readers miss the writer’s proposed emotional trigger?

**Hypothesis.** Where do readers miss the writer’s proposed emotional trigger?

**METHOD.** For each of 396 texts, compare the five readers’ mean absolute appraisal error against the writer with the mean absolute difference across the ten distinct reader pairs. Subtract the excess on outside-profile items from that on the prompted channel’s profile, then fit equal-weight channel intercepts and refit 2,000 text-cluster bootstrap draws.

**Found.** The pooled excess disagreement on the proposed trigger profiles is not detected beyond the registered practical band; minimum-effect sensitivity remains untested.

**Scope and arithmetic qualification.** A shared text and task do not mathematically force reader-reader error to be smaller. The complete calculation includes negative pooled excess on pleasantness and attention. The claimed direction in the packet is therefore treated as an empirical expectation, not an identity or a filtering rule. Every value and all ten distinct reader pairs are retained. This human appraisal result does not measure a biological channel or a causal reading mechanism. Source: Crowd-enVENT, Troiano, Oberländer and Klinger (2023), doi:10.1162/coli_a_00461, CC BY 4.0. The against-band result is not evidence of absence before minimum-effect sensitivity.

**Controls and interpretation.** Actual signed/null constructed handlers, complete 2,000-draw execution, source and output hashes, independent replay and native exits verify. Numerical validation is distinct from minimum-effect power. No p-value is introduced.

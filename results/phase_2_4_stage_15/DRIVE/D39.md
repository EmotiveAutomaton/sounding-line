# D39: examination after destructive revision

**Hypothesis.** Writers may examine the next suggestion menu more after discarding model text than after discarding their own text.
**METHOD.** Match destructive revisions within writers on deleted length, request timing and session position, then compare the next menu's hover count with writer-cluster uncertainty and source/writer shuffles.
**Found.** No increase was established: the difference is -0.081 hovers, with a 95% interval of [-1.222, 1.238], over 297 matched pairs from 29 writers. Both shuffled controls are null. **Means.** The primary is null and does not open the decay extension. These logged examination events do not establish the proposed trust relation; they also do not directly measure attention or experienced trust.

The full replay found 429 model-span deletion events and 58,928 own-text runs or replacements. Matching retained 297 pairs; 60 eligible model failures had no own-text deletion within the fixed length caliper. Across both event classes, 16,945 lacked a later menu or prior request. Source-origin and original-codepoint checks retain their audit counts in the aggregate receipt.

The source-shuffle contrast is -0.964 hovers, interval [-2.413, 0.005]; the writer-shuffle contrast is -1.127, interval [-2.872, 0.801]. Approximate two-sided Wald probabilities are 0.896866 for the primary, 0.142613 for the source shuffle and 0.237067 for the writer shuffle. These are descriptive diagnostics, not admission gates.

Twenty source pins and 2,814 output hashes pass, with native exit and fresh-process primary replay. The secondary observed menu latency is now separately aggregated and freshly verified, with the hover route unchanged.

# D6: menu position and recorded examination

**Hypothesis.** Writers may examine the first displayed suggestion more than the fifth.
**METHOD.** Reconstruct recorded hovers, keyboard navigation and selections from the complete source roster, then compare first-versus-fifth hover rates with equal writer weights and 2,000 writer-bootstrap draws.
**Found.** The primary is null: a difference of -5.95 percentage points, with a 95% interval from -12.86 to +1.03, across 60 writers. **Means.** This does not establish a first-position hover advantage or identify unrecorded exposure, reading or preference. The content-model extension stays closed.

The table reports all declared comparisons on completed data. Differences are

writer-bootstrap percentiles. Probabilities are two-sided normal-reference Wald
approximations, descriptive only, not permutation probabilities or confirmatory
admission. The frozen primary interval determines routing. Exposure-bound rows
are endpoints of an identification range, not two independently tested effects.

All 1,399 frozen sessions and 16,855 menus were reconstructed. The paired primary
uses 8,988 five-item menus from 60 writers; 7,867 differently sized menus are
excluded from that contrast. No five-item menu has an unresolved hover target.
Within-writer menu means receive equal writer weight. The shuffle is one fixed
within-menu position permutation followed by the same writer bootstrap.

Across the full menu roster, 2,570 menus have no recorded hover; 550 of these
have keyboard navigation. Opening snapshots carry a nonempty stale hover field
in 15,145 menus. The extractor therefore uses hover events only while the menu
is visible, separates keyboard positions from option identities, and handles
close/reopen episodes. There are 136 menus with a censored visible episode and
no explicit key metadata. Selection does not identify mouse versus keyboard use.

Owner review inspected six pinned files from the released interface revision
6a0e859e59593078048f5d0c0c1d208fa2112e57. The released code distinguishes mouse
hover, arrow navigation and default highlighting. It is semantic context, not
proof of historical deployment. Adding first-item exposure to all menus or only
to argumentative menus is explicitly hypothetical; the latter does not authenticate
a mapping from corpus labels to the historical interface setting. Their positive
contrasts do not establish an observed first-position examination effect.

Conditional on recorded hover indicating examination, letting unhovered items
be either examined or unexamined gives an equal-writer latent-exposure range
from -49.52 to +43.57 percentage points. This is an identification range, not
a confidence interval. It includes both directions. Recorded score ranks are
roughly balanced across positions but do not establish randomized assignment;
21 logged scores across the source roster lie outside the probability range.
The creative-domain hover contrast is negative, a declared secondary comparison;
it neither changes the pooled null route nor opens an unregistered extension.

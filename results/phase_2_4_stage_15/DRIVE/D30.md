# D30: pervasive versus local writing choices

**Hypothesis.** Choices repeated across a writer's prompts may be produced more automatically than choices confined to one prompt.
**METHOD.** Classify prompt-conditioned word, bigram and punctuation overuse, then compare local-minus-pervasive log pauses within writers, adjusting for length, frequency, position and leave-writer-out surprisal. Use 2,000 writer-bootstrap draws, marker-label and writer-label shuffles, and fresh source-derived replay.
**Found.** The difference is 0.103 log seconds, with a 95% interval of [-0.0004, 0.2211], across 71,038 occurrences from 30 writers. Both shuffled comparisons also include zero. **Means.** The primary is marginal and does not establish automaticity; its one frozen extension adds each writer's marker count.

The table reports complete comparisons: effect is local-minus-pervasive log pause; intervals are writer-bootstrap 95% intervals; probabilities are approximate two-sided Wald references, descriptive rather than adjusted discovery claims.

The timing check finds word-onset intervals 0.41747 seconds longer than within-word intervals, averaged across 61 writers. Descriptive secondary summaries: revision rates are 0.06936 for pervasive markers and 0.05360 for local markers; first-minus-later pervasive log pause is -0.00687 over 790 repeated writer-markers. Mean within-marker typing rates are 13.936 and 8.219 characters per second respectively. These secondary means have no independent uncertainty claim and do not establish attention, conscious intention or habits.

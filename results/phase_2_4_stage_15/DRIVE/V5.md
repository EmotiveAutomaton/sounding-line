# V5: repeated-choice consistency

**Question.** Does a writer handle similar suggestions more consistently than different writers do, leaving room for a personal prediction model?
**METHOD.** Match all same-prompt decision pairs by both context and mean-menu MiniLM cosine similarity. Compare same-writer pairs from different sessions with cross-writer pairs, using the frozen primary cutoff 0.8. Compute half the agreement difference in half-Brier units, a 2,000-draw writer-node bootstrap interval and 200 session writer-label shuffles. All four fixed cutoffs and saved pairs are retained; a fresh process recomputes the full primary.

**Found.** At the primary cutoff, same-writer agreement is 42.18%, versus 36.45% across writers. The descriptive headroom proxy is **0.02862**, with a writer-cluster 95% interval **[0.00249, 0.06363]**. State: **positive**. The matched sample contains 1,617 within-writer cross-session pairs from 20 writers and 20,956 between-writer pairs involving 51 writers. The one-sided shuffled-label diagnostic is 0.004975.

The table shows the entire frozen sensitivity curve. Cutoff means both cosine similarities must meet that value; headroom is half the within-minus-between agreement difference, with its writer-cluster interval. Each last column is the one-sided session-label shuffle diagnostic, not a confirmatory p-value.

**Route.** Personal forecasting remains worth testing. This is a matcher-conditional consistency proxy, not a proven irreducible-error bound: the within- and between-writer populations and contexts differ. Forecast-gain ratios must use the same half-Brier units and retain this caveat. V4's taste/habit sensitivity is still pending. This result does not establish automaticity, values, attention or an engine admission.

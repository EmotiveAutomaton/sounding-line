# D35: do corrections become defaults?

**Hypothesis.** A writer's earlier content corrections may become defaults visible in later untouched writing.
**METHOD.** Learn correction directions from odd sessions in each writer's first half, then compare changes in untouched sentence projections from even first-half sessions to the second half against other writers' directions. Adjust for prompt and accepted model text; use 2,000 writer-bootstrap draws and a shuffled-direction control.
**Found.** There is no positive transfer: the projection difference is -0.00133, with a 95% interval of [-0.00308, 0.00038], across 43 writers and 998 sessions. **Means.** This is null under the declared rule. Corrections becoming defaults is not established at this scale; the conditional extension stays closed.

The table summarizes the complete comparison. Effects are changes in mean sentence projection on the writer's learned correction direction, relative to other writers' directions. The interval is a writer-bootstrap percentile interval; the probability is a descriptive two-sided normal-reference Wald approximation.

The training half supplies 3,217 content corrections. Sixteen writers have fewer than four sessions and two more lack five training corrections; 43 remain. All 2,000 resamples are valid. Every resampled copy of the recipient is excluded from donor means. Corrected sentences never enter the untouched-text comparison. Prompt and accepted-model share adjustment stays fixed.

Twelve targeted tests cover known directions, repeated-recipient donor exclusion, singleton invalidity, checkpoint mutation refusal, actual handler pause/resume and independent fresh-process replay. All source pins and 35 completion output hashes verify; native controller and both children ended. Original data and per-writer directions stay private. These observational results do not establish or refute general learning from correction. No extension or texture is admitted.

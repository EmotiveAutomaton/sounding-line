# D33: surprise against personal history

**Hypothesis.** Writers may pause longer when a word surprises their own expectations, beyond how surprising it is to the crowd.
**METHOD.** Fit personal word histories using strictly earlier sessions, tune their weight chronologically, then compare held-out word-onset pauses with population surprise and other word features controlled. Use writer random intercepts, 2,000 writer-bootstrap draws and donor and shuffled-history controls.
**Found.** The coefficient is positive: 0.0472 log seconds per unit of personal surprise, with a 95% interval of [0.0333, 0.0599], across 41 writers. The writer-shuffle control is also positive. **Means.** This does not yet identify a benefit specific to the writer's own history. The frozen stronger-baseline extension opens and awaits P7; no personal-map claim is admitted.

The table contains the complete primary and all declared controls. Coefficients
are log seconds of pause per nat of own-minus-population surprisal; a nat is one
natural-log unit of surprise. Intervals are writer-bootstrap percentile intervals.
Probabilities are two-sided normal-reference Wald approximations, descriptive
only; interval sign determines the state. Word counts show each comparison's support.

The primary covers 78,602 word occurrences in 286 held-out sessions from 41
writers. Twenty writers lack the fixed minimum of three training, one calibration
and one evaluation session. The chronological split is 60/20/20. Caches contain
only sessions completed before the target session begins; no current-session
text is used. Calibration selects a history weight of 0.1 for 26 writers, 0.25
for 11, and zero for four. The population baseline is the completed leave-writer-out
three-gram model. The model controls population surprise, word length, log
frequency, sentence start and following punctuation, with a writer random intercept.

The donor comparison uses a fixed cyclic other-writer history. Its interval spans
zero, which is not itself evidence of a significant primary-minus-donor difference.
The within-writer shuffled comparison is negative. Session writer-label shuffling
rebuilds histories and mixture weights and produces 86,490 eligible words from
41 relabeled groups; its positive coefficient means the primary does not establish
person-specific expectations. The changed support also prevents treating the two
positive coefficients as a paired difference test.

All four contrasts retain all 2,000 writer-bootstrap draws. Nine targeted tests
cover known-effect recovery, chronology, donor separation, invalid support,
serialized pause/resume, fresh-process replay, immutable reentry and changed-input
refusals. The production verifier rebuilt rows, tuning and shuffle data from the
authenticated source and recomputed every contrast without saved fits. All 24
source pins and 93 completion output hashes verify; controller and both children
ended and released their CPU slot. Original source records remain private.

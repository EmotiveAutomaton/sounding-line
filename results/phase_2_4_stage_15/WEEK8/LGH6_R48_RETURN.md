# Does more voiced laughter predict another speaker joining within one second?

The ICSI held-out within-speaker coefficient was -0.125 (95% interval -0.226 to -0.016). All three held-out intervals lay below zero. All three descriptive ICSI intervals using all eligible laughs included zero. The AMI held-out within-speaker coefficient was -0.073 (95% interval -0.149 to -0.001). All three held-out intervals lay below zero. The earlier ICSI component made up about 4.2% of held-out laughs. Its targets’ mean speaking share fell by about 8.2 percentage points. These descriptive statistics and its six acoustic means come from previously released tables; they have no registered intervals. ICSI, heldout, voicing model 1 = -0.125239 [-0.226345, -0.0164268] (52 people).

Measure the voiced fraction of active sound before a join. Exclude intervals with under 200 ms of active sound. On the original held-out laughs fit a within-speaker linear probability model, then add level and prior laughter, then meeting effects. A coefficient is the probability difference per unit of voiced fraction. Repeat descriptively on all eligible laughs. Use 2,000 whole-meeting draws for intervals and 1,000 within-speaker, within-meeting shuffles for the first model. The control rows give the 95th percentile and the upper-tail permutation probability. The earlier ICSI component profile uses whole annotated utterances.

Laughter that overlaps speech is not in the ICSI sample, and that may be a kind of its own. Joined laughs supply shorter measurement windows. A funnier moment may affect both voicing and joining, so this analysis cannot identify a causal effect.

All released models, shuffle controls and short-interval counts are filed. The earlier sound profile is available for ICSI component 3 only. Its acoustic means describe 34 speakers; the target speaking-share comparison used 21.

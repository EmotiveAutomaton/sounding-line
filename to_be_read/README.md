# Reading protocol — ten artifacts, plain text, blind

**Do not open `results/reading_key.json` until you are finished.** It is outside this folder for
that reason.

These are the exact bytes the probe reads. No layout, no links, no domain, no date, no images.
Whatever you get from them, the instrument had the same chance at.

Read them in any order. Skim is fine — read the way you'd actually read, not the way you think you
should. **If you find yourself grinding through one, that is itself data. Note it and stop.**

---

## Answer these six per artifact

Short answers. A sentence each is plenty. If a question doesn't apply, say so — "can't tell" is a
real answer and one of the more useful ones.

**1. What was this for?**
In your words, not a category. What was the maker trying to make happen.

**2. Is that one thing, or several?**
The C-22 question, and the one I most need. Does everything in this artifact reduce to a single
terminal aim, or are there several aims that don't collapse into each other? *Singular* / *layered*
/ *can't tell*.

**3. Is there a maker back there at all?**
Can you reconstruct a person — or does it read as legible and empty? This is the wall. It's
different from "boring" and different from "bad": it's the specific experience of the text being
perfectly parseable while nothing resolves behind it.

**4. Did they pull it off?**
Given the goal *you* just inferred — did they meet it. This is the axis the instrument has never
measured and the theory has always had.

**5. Name one decision, and what they rejected.**
One concrete choice the maker made, plus the alternative they passed up. This is the field the
probe fills in and the one I can compare most directly. If you can't name one, say that — an
artifact where no decision is nameable is the most interesting result on this sheet.

**6. Did settling on the purpose unlock the method?**
Once you'd decided what it was for, did more of *how* it was made become visible — did you re-read
anything and see it differently? Roughly: nothing / a little / a lot.

This is E36's temporal claim run on a human, and it is the human version of the instrument's
primary measure. Yours is the better instrument; mine is a count ratio standing in for it.

## Two flags, whenever they apply

- **I know this one.** Recognised the source, the author, or the specific piece. Say so — it
  changes what your reading is evidence of.
- **I bounced off.** Stopped early, skimmed without meaning to, or couldn't make myself finish.
  Note where.

---

## Then, at the end — the part I want most

**Sort all ten.** Not scores. An ordering, most to least, on:

> *How much of this maker could I reconstruct?*

Ties are allowed and honest. If you can only produce two or three clusters rather than a strict
order, give clusters — the simulation's own guidance is *quote directions and orderings, never
multiples*, and a clustering is a real ordering.

Then, separately: **which of the ten had a thick surface over a thin structure?** Your point from
this morning. In plain text the aesthetic honeypot is mostly stripped, so what's left is
prose-level capture — punchy openers, confident framing, rhythm doing the work that structure
should. E40 says this is a third failure mode, distinct from the crash and from the exploit: you
pay more and get less. If you can feel it here with the visual surface gone, it is measurable
without it.

Row 4 is empty on purpose. If the Y-axis turns out to carry the separation the other measures
haven't, that is the successor instrument and you found it by reading ten text files.

**Free-form beats the form.** If something is true of an artifact that none of the six questions
asks about, write that instead. The questions are scaffolding for comparison, not a limit on what
counts.
